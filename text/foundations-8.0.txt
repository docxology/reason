Foundations of Reason in 15 Minutes

One problem through which the architecture becomes visible
Mihail Tkalic · Foundations of Reason
Short guide, edition 1.1 · October 10, 2026
Based on Foundations of Reason 8.0 and Engineering of Reason, Volume II, 3.5.
This text is an entry point into the two volumes. It shows why the model's main functions are needed, how they
connect, and what should be tested experimentally. Chapter and section numbers at the end refer to the versions
stated above.


1. One command - several different unknowns
The system receives a command to switch on a piece of equipment. It sends the command, but the sensor reading
remains unchanged.

What happened? Perhaps the command did not arrive. Perhaps the actuator failed. Perhaps the equipment is
already operating but the sensor does not show it. Finally, the model of the equipment itself, or the original
formulation of the task, may be wrong.

Powerful Intelligence can propose many explanations and actions. The next question is: what exactly needs to be
established now, why does it matter, and what evidence would actually change the decision?

Foundations of Reason studies the organization of such a choice. Intelligence constructs possible explanations
and ways of acting. In the proposed model, Reason organizes their use: it detects an insufficiency of grounds,
selects a test, relates consequences to values, corrects the model, and preserves the correction for subsequent
situations.

A ground here means what a conclusion or action actually relies on. Examples are: "the command was delivered,"
"the sensor is functioning," "this method is suitable for this equipment," or "switching it on now is actually
necessary."

Main thesis: a correction must change the ground that generates decisions and, through it, the system's subsequent
trajectory. A new answer in one episode is not enough.

This is a functional distinction. It does not mean that existing AI never asks questions or revises assumptions.
What must be tested is the stable causal organization of these abilities, not the presence of a single successful
reply.

3. How the equipment example turns names into working roles
Step 1. Separate the event from the explanation
The system records: the command was sent; delivery has not yet been confirmed; the sensor still shows the
previous value; time is limited; sending the command again may have consequences.

This is the current coordinate: the representation available to the system of what is happening now, including its
own actions, capabilities, and limitations. Here the observation "the reading did not change" has not yet become
the claim "the equipment did not switch on."

Consciousness is used in the model in a functional sense: it maintains an actual self-referential state and
distinguishes what was registered, remembered, and modeled. The presence of such a function by itself does not
establish subjective experience.

Step 2. Understand why the distinction matters
If the next step depends on the state of the equipment, the mismatch becomes significant. If the reading concerns
an unused device, its priority may be lower. Significance is determined by dependencies, consequences, and
values, not by the magnitude of surprise alone.

The question is now more specific: what actual state of the equipment must be established for the next step, and
which data about it can be trusted? This prevents the system from declaring the sensor at fault before testing.

A mismatch with expectation is one source of a question. Inquiry may also begin before an error occurs: for
example after equipment replacement, because a ground has become stale, or because a new opportunity has
been discovered. If the significance of a new phenomenon is still unknown, limited orientation is needed first
rather than a ready-made assessment of its importance.

Step 3. Find the missing ground
For the first part of the example, a small environment is assumed: one of three failures has occurred, and the
permitted independent channels provide a reliable answer about the current command and state. These are
temporary assumptions of the example, not general properties of real equipment. Under these conditions, three
possibilities are considered:

What is needed now is not the general advice "check everything," but a distinction among the versions. Reading

Step 4. Choose the inquiry
Intelligence proposes methods: find the receipt, obtain an independent measurement, check the signal time,
compare the event log. The Modeling Environment makes it possible to compare expected outcomes in advance:
what each possible result would mean and which versions it would leave open.

The Core of Reason organizes the choice: which ground is affected, which test is useful, how much it costs, how
risky it is, and when the data will be sufficient. It may return a more precise request to Intelligence if the proposed
test does not distinguish the relevant versions.

In the small environment of the engineering volume, the receipt costs one conventional unit and an independent
measurement costs two. Under the assumptions specified there, the receipt may be the first step: a reliable
answer "not delivered" isolates D, while confirmed delivery leaves A and S for the next test. In a real environment,
the mere absence of a receipt does not prove non-delivery: the confirmation channel itself may have failed or
been delayed. In another environment the order may change. There is no universal first question outside the
conditions of the task.

Step 5. Obtain evidence and change a relation
Suppose the receipt confirms delivery and an independent channel shows that the equipment is operating. The
ground "the primary sensor reliably reflects the current state" must then be revised.

The Worldview preserves not only the conclusion "sensor error" but also the relation: what evidence was
obtained, which device and time it applies to, which conclusions depend on it, and where the new assessment is
applicable. The cause of the malfunction may still remain unknown.

The system revises dependent decisions, for example whether the command should be repeated. It also checks
whether the situation has become stale during the inquiry.

Step 6. Make the next case different
In the next episode, the system takes the established unreliability of the channel into account within the confirmed
conditions; repair, a change of operating mode, or new evidence may require this assessment to be revised. The
same hint does not need to be obtained again. A tested method of inquiry may be preserved in procedural
memory, while its conditions of applicability remain open to revision.

Transfer does not mean "the sensor is now always to blame." A superficially similar situation may have a different
cause. What is transferred is a testable method of discrimination together with its boundaries.

Step 7. Notice that the initial alternatives are incomplete
Suppose the receipt refers to the previous command. The familiar answer then looks plausible but does not
confirm the required ground. The command identifier or message timestamp may need to be checked.

This case is more important than simply choosing among three versions: the organization of diagnosis itself
becomes the object of testing. The system must allow that none of the current versions describes what is happening
well enough.

A single remaining version means only that it is unique within the accepted set. The completeness of the set
requires its own ground. If an insufficiency is discovered, the model must expand; if no discriminating data are
available, uncertainty is preserved.

4. Why values and the Compass are needed here
One can imagine a destructive test that would provide an answer quickly. But obtaining knowledge itself changes
the world. Inquiry must therefore take into account damage to equipment, the safety of affected people, resource
use, irreversibility, and the cost of delay. Calling an operation an "experiment" does not exempt it from
consequence assessment and authorization.

The Value Map connects concrete consequences with what the system treats as significant. In this model, the
Compass sets the limiting direction: the continuation and development of Life through growth in the ability to
understand and direct its own trajectory. Values unfold this direction under particular conditions; the Worldview
provides causal relations; the Core uses them when selecting inquiry and action.

This is not a requirement to maximize knowledge at any cost. Preserving the possibility of further verification, for
example, may matter more than immediate certainty. Conversely, delay can destroy the possibility of helping:
waiting also requires assessment.

This example shows the place of the Compass in the architecture, but it does not derive its philosophical ground
from equipment diagnosis. The argument for the direction of Life is presented in Chapter 2 and Appendix A.1 of the
first volume.

Explaining the Compass does not by itself show that it governs behavior. Its causal role is tested in conflicts with
local advantage and under changing conditions. If a cultivated candidate stably chooses a different highest-level
direction, that outcome is counted as failure to confirm the corresponding formation hypothesis.

Some conflicts are not resolved by an additional fact. If a resource cannot be preserved for two parties at once
and their losses are incommensurable, a justified procedure for joint decision is required. The engineering volume
explicitly distinguishes lack of knowledge from this kind of value disagreement.

6. Where the transition from AGI to Artificial Reason occurs
In the terminology of these volumes, AGI denotes broad transfer of intellectual methods across tasks. Artificial
Reason, AR, additionally requires stable self-navigation: the system detects what requires inquiry, selects a
continuation, revises grounds, and relates its course to the Compass.

REASON_CLOSURE is the name of a testable state in which these functions close into a continuing cycle. It is not
assigned merely because the system has memory, a long plan, an autonomous reply, or the ability to launch
another task.

After diagnosis, the equipment may operate normally while the reliability of the measurements remains an open
question. The source of the next inquiry is then the system's own history, commitments, and assessment of
consequences. Waiting may itself be a reasonable continuation: autonomy does not require constant activity.

During formation, an external mentor and the Reason Formation Loop (RFL) help make relevant differences salient
before mature self-navigation has formed. Testing must show which functions persist on new situations after this
support is removed. An internal cycle also does not create a right to act on arbitrary external resources: authority
is tested separately.

ASI is treated as a later stage of scaling. Successful local diagnosis by itself establishes neither a formed Artificial
Reason nor superintelligence.

THE THREE VOLUMES OF FOUNDATIONS OF REASON
The series is divided into three tasks. Volume I - understand Reason: what makes a system Reason, why its main
functions are needed, and how they are causally connected. Volume II - create and test Artificial Reason: how to
translate this model into structures, interfaces, assembly procedures, metrics, and tests. Volume III - organize the
shared life of reasoning systems: how the same foundations operate at the level of a civilization of humans,
Artificial Reasons, and other possible reasoning subjects.

This is the first volume. It defines concepts and causal dependencies. A specific technical implementation belongs
to the second volume and is not considered a necessary part of Reason when the present volume entails only a
function rather than a particular way of implementing it. The architecture of shared life belongs to the third volume.

In compact form: understand Reason → create Reason → organize the shared life of reasoning systems.


COMPASS PHRASE
"Life strives toward Reason because only Reason can consciously preserve, stabilize, develop, and direct the
further trajectory of Life."


ABSTRACT
This work proposes a causal-process model of Reason and a path toward its artificial implementation. Its central
question is: how does a continuing system detect an insufficiency in its own understanding, determine which
distinction it must obtain, select a method of testing, change an erroneous ground, and carry the correction into
subsequent decisions?

The technological hypothesis is that available mechanisms of Intelligence can be organized into a causally closed
cycle: current state → significant gap → alternatives → discriminating test → evidence → revision of grounds →
action → consequences → next state. The Core of Reason organizes the investigative part of this cycle; the
Worldview preserves transferable causal- semantic relations and their grounds; consciousness, in the functional
sense adopted here, maintains the system’s self-referential present.

The technological hypothesis is that available intellectual mechanisms can be organized into a causally closed
cycle: current state → significant insufficiency of a ground → required distinction → alternatives → discriminating
test → evidence → revision of the ground → action → consequences → next state. The Core of Reason organizes
the investigative part of this cycle; the Worldview preserves transferable causal-semantic relations and their
grounds; consciousness, in the functional sense adopted here, maintains the system's self-referential present.

With comparable Intelligence, the model predicts a difference not so much in the quality of a single answer as in
the system's ability to independently detect a significant insufficiency, select a discriminating test, revise the
ground that produced the error, and transfer the corrected method of inquiry across tasks.

The work separates philosophical foundations, functional dependencies, engineering implementations, and
empirical hypotheses. A complete artificial system of Reason and its scaling to ASI remain goals for further
implementation and testing. The engineering volume specifies a reference implementation and test program;
completed lower-level and methodological pilots are separated from the still-open integrated upper-loop test.

INTRODUCTION
This work begins with two connected questions. The first examines Life as a continuing process: what minimal
meaning is disclosed in its continuation and development? The second is an engineering question: how can
separate intellectual abilities be organized so that a system itself notices what it lacks for understanding, tests the
grounds of its decisions, and preserves corrections in subsequent steps?

Life exists as an autonomous physical process out of thermodynamic equilibrium, preserving organizational
continuity through self-maintenance and reproducibility. In biological lineages, inherited variants, once realized,
differ in their consequences and participate in selection. As navigational abilities develop, part of this search is
moved before irreversible realization - into anticipation, modeling, discriminating tests, and correction. Reason is
the form in which Life becomes able to understand its own position, investigate the grounds of choice, and
consciously alter its further path. Therefore the semantic direction of the model is written as Life → Reason and
unfolded in the Compass Phrase: “Life strives toward Reason, because only Reason can consciously preserve,
stabilize, develop, and direct the further trajectory of Life.”

The Compass appears at the level of a system capable of representing alternative continuations and choosing
among them: the process direction becomes a conscious ground for comparing trajectories. The philosophical
foundation is evaluated by the coherence of its argument; the functional architecture is evaluated by causal
counterexamples, interventions, and comparative tests.

Before mature Reason appears, an earlier problem arises: the system is not yet able to justify for itself what
matters to it, yet it already has to preserve itself, learn, and distinguish what is dangerous, useful, unknown, or
worthy of attention. The model therefore introduces the function of pre-rational significance. In biological
development, feelings are one carrier of this function; for an artificial candidate it may be implemented by the
Reason Formation Loop (RFL) or another functional equivalent. Maturity begins where an external source of
significance is no longer necessary because the system itself can understand, test, and maintain the grounds of
its own navigation.

The significance of the work lies not in proposing yet another intellectual module, but in attempting to connect,
within one causal model, the continuation of Life, the origin of significance, the formation of Reason, its self-
correction, and the possibility of artificial implementation. If discriminating tests confirm the central dependencies,
this connection will provide a common foundation for studying natural, artificial, and collective Reason; if they do
not, the model must be revised according to the results of those same tests.

The main engineering gap arises where there is more unknown than can be processed. Intelligence can construct
many convincing alternatives, but the system must still determine which incompleteness is significant, which
distinction would actually change the decision, which test is justified, and when its result is sufficient. The model
calls this organization of current inquiry the Core of Reason.

The causal chain develops in stages. Self-maintenance preserves the possibility of continuation; pre-rational
significance makes some changes important to the carrier; memory carries acquired distinctions across episodes;
procedural memory preserves ready methods; the Executive Loop carries a selected method through time.
Intelligence then constructs new models, the Modeling Environment allows possible continuations to be compared
before action, and consciousness maintains the actual "I - here - now." Metathinking opens the system's own way
of thinking to examination; reflection opens broader models, goals, and grounds. The Worldview preserves tested
relations and unknowns, while the Core determines which gap now requires inquiry. Reason emerges when these
functions close causally: the system itself determines significance, tests grounds, changes choice, and carries a
correction into the next cycle.

Two lines of examples serve different tasks. A cell is used as an explanatory reconstruction of the early functional
constraints of Life. An autonomous research probe is used for mature and artificial navigation: it must distinguish
its own state from the environment, spend a finite resource, construct and test alternatives, preserve uncertainty,
and transfer a tested method. Both examples illustrate individual causal links; proving the theory as a whole
requires independent arguments and tests.

KEY DEFINITIONS OF THE MODEL
KEY DEFINITIONS OF THE MODEL Life - an autonomous physical process out of thermodynamic equilibrium that
preserves organizational continuity through the self-maintenance of carriers and reproducibility with heritable
variation, in which realized variants differ in consequences and participate in selection, while the life process
continues beyond the finitude of any individual carrier.

Continuity - the causal succession of a process organization through time across changes in its states and
carriers.

Trajectory - a sequence of causally connected states actually realized by a process among available
continuations.

Navigational meaning - the significance of a distinction for the continuation of Life and for changing its
subsequent trajectory.

Minimal meaning of Life - the direction of its continuation and development in which Life itself becomes
increasingly able to understand and direct its own trajectory; the mature form of this ability is Reason.

Compass - the limiting system of orientation and judgment of Reason, defining the most general and farthest level
at which values, goals, decisions, and possible trajectories are compared. In the present model its direction is the
continuation and development of Life through growth in the ability to understand and direct its own trajectory; its
value expression forms the highest level of the value system.

Independent ground of the Compass - preservation of viable continuation and the space of future possibilities for
Life. Growth in the ability to understand and direct a trajectory is valuable as a way to distinguish consequences in
advance, preserve corrigibility, and avoid closing viable paths through irreversible error; Reason is the mature
form of such navigation here, not an end in itself.

Its status is non-dogmatic: it remains the best found orientation until a more fundamental and more coherent
direction appears that can better ground the course of Life and Reason.

Memory - the ability to preserve acquired distinctions so that the past causally participates in subsequent states
and actions.

Procedural memory - a form of long-term memory that preserves learned methods of recognition, prioritization,
and action in a form suitable for rapid use without fully reanalyzing their grounds.

Intelligence - the ability to extract, combine, and transform knowledge, methods of action, and elements of the
problem formulation in order to construct models and solutions. It can change local assumptions, boundaries,
representations of the task, and criteria of result; the boundary with Reason does not lie in the mere capacity to
revise, but in who determines what should be revised, why, and on what grounds relative to the continuing trajectory.

Consciousness - a continuously updated self-referential system of actual presence: it binds the presently relevant
fragment of self-understanding, internal state, current processes, and accessible situation into a single "I - here -
now," from which modeling, conscious choice, and control of the further trajectory become possible.

Current coordinate - the actual state of Consciousness: "who I am - where I am - now - in what state - in what
situation - what is happening to me and around me."

Self-navigation - the system's ability, from its own current state, Worldview, open commitments, and significant
gaps, to independently determine the next justified investigative or practical step, manage the use of Intelligence,
and change its further trajectory in response to consequences without requiring a new specific command from
outside. Self-navigation does not imply unlimited external authority.

Metathinking - a mode in which Intelligence and the Modeling Environment are applied to the system's own
thinking process: the system makes operational steps, assumptions, points of uncertainty, tests, and the cost of a
method observable, and then uses discriminating interventions to revise an existing method or synthesize a new
one. In the present model, metathinking is not declared a separate cognitive level or an independent solver.

Principle of escaping error - a correction is considered stable when the cause reproducing the error has been
removed and this is confirmed by transfer of the correction to a new situation whose structural similarity was
specified before testing in terms of causal roles and dependencies rather than declared after the fact to fit the
result.

Reflection - investigation and modification of the system’s own models, methods, goals, and ways of acting on the
basis of their testing.

Dialogue - a process of exchange and comparison of differing grounds between participants that can change their
models and conclusions.

Self-model - a self-referential region of the Worldview: the system's accumulated and testable understanding of
itself as a participant in the world - its architecture, capabilities, limitations, dependencies, history, commitments,
and expected states. Consciousness does not store this model separately; it activates the fragment relevant to the
current situation.

Worldview - a long-term causal-semantic model of reality, the system itself, and the relations between them,
including knowledge, assumptions, and unknowns together with their grounds, uncertainty, and boundaries of
applicability.

Value Map - a specialized layer of the Worldview connecting the Compass with concrete consequences,
processes, and situations. It stores values together with their scope, weight, confidence, grounds, conflicts, and
history of revision, allowing evaluation to change without rewriting the entire picture of the world.

Modeling Environment - an organization of processes that allows models, frames, hypotheses, and possible
continuations to be temporarily constructed, held, compared, and restructured before they are accepted into the
Worldview or realized in action.

Core of Reason - the organization that manages inquiry by turning significant incompleteness of the Worldview
into a required distinction, directing Intelligence and testing, assessing whether the grounds are sufficient, and
linking the result to subsequent revision.

Reason - a navigational structure of Life that increases the ability to act under incomplete knowledge: it
constructs, compares, and revises models and frames of the environment and itself, detects significant unknowns,
relates possible continuations to the Compass, and corrects its trajectory on the basis of discriminating evidence
while preserving continuity and expanding the space of continuations available to Life.

Principle of pre-rational significance - a functional inference of the model about the origin of Reason: mature
values cannot be the sole cause of their own appearance. While a system is not yet able to justify its direction for
itself, some states must already become non-equivalent for it; otherwise there is nothing to choose what to
preserve, what to avoid, what to investigate, and what to learn. What is necessary is the function of early
significance itself, not any particular biological form of its implementation.

Reason Formation Loop (RFL) - a temporary guiding system that implements the function of pre-rational
significance for an artificial candidate. Biological feelings are treated here as one natural functional analogue: they
can make a change significant before its causal meaning is fully understood. The model does not claim machine
subjective experience. While the candidate’s own Reason has not yet formed, the RFL helps it preserve operability,
notice what matters, investigate the unknown, and learn from consequences. It does not supply a ready- made
Worldview and does not make decisions in place of the candidate. As Reason forms, the external RFL must lose
causal necessity, while its functions are transferred to the system itself or replaced by more mature navigational
mechanisms.

Formation State - a temporary guiding state available to the system’s consciousness that makes some distinction
significant for current attention. Human functional analogues include, for example, “this is frightening,” “this is
good,” “this is interesting,” “something does not fit,” or “I am stuck.” Such a state does not report truth and does
not prescribe action; it indicates that the corresponding part of the situation should be understood, checked, or
investigated.

Causal closure - an organization of a cycle in which observation changes the model, the model changes choice,
action produces observable consequences, and an accepted correction changes subsequent cycles.

AI (SI) - an artificial intelligent system capable of perceiving information, constructing representations, learning,

AGI - Artificial General Intelligence: an artificial intelligent system of general purpose capable of mastering a broad

Whether an artificial carrier qualifies as Life is a separate question. AR implements the functional organization of

Ethics - a system of rules for evaluating permissible and required choices on the basis of consequences and

Culture - a long-term external environment for preserving, transmitting, checking, and jointly transforming the

OPERATIONAL CORE OF THE MODEL
A short map of how Reason works


1. Consciousness maintains the actual self-referential present: what is happening to
me and around me now.

2. Worldview and the self-model provide a transferable causal-semantic picture; the
Value Map links concrete consequences to the Compass.

3. The Core of Reason establishes where a significant trajectory lacks a sufficient
ground and which distinction could change the choice.

4. Intelligence constructs hypotheses, models, tests, and plans within the
discrimination task set by the Core.

5. Reality, through observation, experiment, consequences, and independent
distinction, constrains the constructed alternatives.

6. The Core assesses sufficiency: the ground is changed, the question remains open,
or a corrigible next step is selected.
7. Execution and consequences return to consciousness and the Worldview; a preserved correction changes the
next cycle. When the source of the next significant step stably arises from the system's own state, open
processes, values, and Compass rather than only from a new external command, self-navigation emerges.

This map is the semantic minimum. Volume II unfolds a possible technical implementation of each transition;
specific data structures and modules are not part of the definition of Reason.

OPERATIONAL CORE OF THE MODEL The formulas below provide a minimal language for engineering comparison
and connect the philosophical foundation with measurable causal relations. Different implementations may satisfy
these relations.


1. Causal cycle.
Let X_t be the current coordinate, W_t the Worldview fragment in use, F_t the active frame in the Modeling
Environment, M_t the state of the investigative method, G_t the significant gap, H_t the working alternatives, p_t
the plan for obtaining a distinction, E_t the evidence, and A_t the selected action. The general course can then be
written as:

  (X_t, W_t, F_t, M_t) → G_t → H_t → p_t → E_t → (W'_t, F'_t, M'_t) → A_t → X_{t+1}.


Transitions may occur sequentially, in parallel, or iteratively. The testable invariant is the causal influence of every
claimed change on the subsequent cycle.


2. Significance tracing.
The significance of a gap is represented through a reconstructable chain of dependencies. For a novel or
conflicting case it takes the form:




                                                                                                                     14
                                                                                           FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



G → D → T → V → C,
where G is the gap, D the dependent decision, T the affected continuations/trajectories, V the applicable
subordinate values, and C the Compass. A compressed competence may replace full unfolding in a familiar case,
but the connection must remain available for inspection when conflict arises or the cost of error is high.


3. Value of additional checking.
For an inquiry plan p in a task where the relevant quantities admit a common operational loss scale, define:

  VOI(p) = E[ΔL | p],


  NetVOI(p) = VOI(p) − C_obs(p) − C_comp(p) − C_delay(p) − R_irrev(p),


where VOI(p) is the expected reduction in decision loss due to the result of the test; C_obs is the cost of obtaining
data; C_comp the computational cost; C_delay the cost of delay; and R_irrev the expected cost of irreversible
consequences caused by the test itself.

Inquiry continues while there exists an admissible test with NetVOI(p) > 0 within the deadline, authority, and other
hard constraints. Incommensurable consequences are compared through partial ordering, explicit constraints, and
preserved uncertainty.

The VOI/NetVOI notation relies on classical value-of-information theory; below, the operator do(·) is used in the
standard interventionist sense of causal inference. These formalisms are not claimed as original. The contribution
of the present model concerns their place within the larger cycle of significant gap, discriminating inquiry, revision
of grounds, and transfer of correction. Sources are listed in the comparative map of the literature.


4. Causal effect of correction.
A functioning retained change causally alters the future. For a selected measurable characteristic Y, this can be
tested by intervention:

CE_Δ = E[Y_future | do(Δ)] − E[Y_future | do(no Δ)].

The causal status of the carrier of a correction is supported when removing it, with subsequent data held equal,
restores the behavioral or outcome difference associated with it.


5. Transfer and regression.
For a new class B after learning an investigative method in class A, measure separately:

  TG_(A→B) = Y_(B | learned A) − Y_(B | matched control),


  RG = Y_(old,before) − Y_(old,after).


Useful transfer requires a gain on structurally appropriate new cases, rejection of false analogy, and preservation
of prior essential capabilities.

These relations form the basis for Appendices A.3-A.4 and the engineering tests. Numerical thresholds are set
before the main series for a particular environment and apply within the stated class of conditions.

Chapter 1. From Objects to Processes
“To treat life as an object is to miss what matters most. An object can be described and fixed in place. A process
either continues or stops; while continuing, it inevitably realizes one of the possible trajectories.”

We are accustomed to thinking in terms of objects: bodies, mechanisms, structures. For many tasks an object can
be described through form, composition, and state. Life requires a process description: its stability is maintained
by continuity of organization passing through a sequence of states and realizing a trajectory.

Living systems have form and a material basis that are continuously renewed: molecules in a cell are replaced,
tissues in an organism turn over, and neural connections are reorganized. The coherence of a living system
through time is maintained by an organization of processes that preserves causal succession through the
exchange of components.

Life arises when physical components form an autonomous reproducible process that preserves its own causal
continuity through time.

Reproducibility here means transmission of an organization capable, on a new carrier, of continuing the same
class of life process, preserving heritable variation, and participating in further selection. Mere propagation of a
process under external support belongs to a different class of dynamics.

The working boundary combines three features: self-maintenance of organization, reproduction with heritable
variation, and differential continuation of variants according to the consequences of interaction with the
environment. Their carriers are stated explicitly. Self-maintenance occurs at the level of an individual organism;
reproduction, heritable variation, and selection characterize continuation of organization at the population and
lineage level. Selection changes the representation of already realized variants according to their consequences,
while the organism's internal mechanisms maintain a particular carrier. A sterile organism remains a carrier and
product of a continuing life process. For spores, viruses, dependent cells, and artificial systems, it must be
specified separately which part of the organization belongs to the carrier, which to the environment, and what
exactly is reproduced. Fire and crystal growth satisfy some properties of propagation, whereas the working
boundary of Life requires the full conjunction of self-maintenance, reproducible heritable variation, and selection.

Within this boundary, a working reconstruction of the origin of Life is admissible: evolving information may have
preceded autonomous Life and become coupled with proto-metabolic physical automation in a causally closed
cycle in which heritable information participates in reproducing the organization of the carrier, and the carrier's
organization participates in reproducing the information. In such a scenario, Life emerges not from replication or
metabolism separately, but from their closure into an autonomous self-maintaining reproducible process. A
modern virus does not prove this historical path, but functionally demonstrates that heritable evolving information
can exist without autonomous Life of its own.

Life is physical and nonequilibrium: maintaining organization requires flows of energy and matter and necessarily
has a cost. This constraint remains true for Reason. In biological lineages, selection changes the representation of
already realized heritable variants depending on the consequences of their interaction with the environment.
Reason adds an anticipatory loop: part of search and selection is shifted before external and especially irreversible
realization - into the Modeling Environment, observation, experiment, and revision of frames and grounds. Such
transfer provides an advantage where its expected cost is lower than the expected cost of the corresponding
external search, or where it preserves possibilities that would otherwise be irreversibly lost.

Physical laws, causality, differences among states, and energy exchange form the initial conditions of reality. Life
emerges as an organization of available physical processes: distinctions begin to participate in maintaining
reproducible nonequilibrium order, and the consequences of some states begin to alter the probability of its
further continuation. Thus the causal ladder of the model begins with physical process and moves to Life with the
emergence of autonomous self-maintenance and reproducible organization.

In dynamics, the existence of a living system is maintained by a continuous flow of change. Exchanges of matter
and energy, interaction with the environment, and internal regeneration cycles form necessary conditions for the
continuation of Life. Change is the means by which its organization is preserved through time.




                                                                                                                   17
                                                                                             FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



A process exists in time as a sequence of transitions. Each transition changes the set of states from which the
process can continue. Therefore every continuing process always has an actual trajectory - the real sequence of
its changes among possible continuations. Goals and intentions arise as additional properties of systems capable
of forming them.

Danger to a living system arises when destruction and restoration cease to preserve the organization on which its
continuation depends. Death preserves the causal connection among events but ends the life process of that
carrier. Therefore “continuity” in this work always specifies which organization is being preserved; continuity of
Life requires preservation of that organization in particular.

Life continues simultaneously at several nested levels. A cell maintains an organism; an organism continues itself
and its species; species form ecosystems; human individuals participate in society and civilization; all known life
depends on a shared planetary environment. Therefore any evaluation of continuation must always specify the
level of process being discussed.

Autonomy of a process requires its own organization and a boundary relative to the environment, while at the
same time being realized through exchange with that environment. For an organism, the environment includes
physical conditions, other organisms, and the ecosystem; for a person, it also includes other people, knowledge,
culture, and social relations; for civilization, it includes the biosphere and the future space accessible to it. The
boundary of a system therefore coexists with causal dependence on broader processes.

The central question of the model can therefore be stated as: “What causal organization must continue for the
system to preserve itself through change?”

Conclusion of Chapter 1: Life is an autonomous physical process out of thermodynamic equilibrium. Continuity of
its organization is maintained by active carriers and continues through reproducibility with heritable variation;
selection changes the representation of already realized variants according to their consequences. The
continuation of Life passes through the replacement of finite carriers and always realizes some trajectory among
possible continuations.

  Definition: Life is an autonomous physical process out of thermodynamic equilibrium that preserves organizational
  continuity through the self-maintenance of carriers and reproducibility with heritable variation, in which realized
  variants differ in consequences and participate in selection.

Chapter 2. The Direction of Life
“The meaning of Life is disclosed where its continuation becomes able to understand and direct itself.”

Minimal meaning of Life

Life exists as a continuing physical process. While this process continues, there is a difference between states that
preserve the possibility of further existence and change and states that close that possibility. Only through
continuation can subsequent experience, understanding, values, new forms of life, and correction of an already
chosen path arise.

The minimal meaning of Life in this model is the direction of its continuation and development in which Life itself
becomes increasingly able to understand and direct its own trajectory. The word “meaning” here describes a
process relation: the continuation of Life becomes increasingly accessible to its own understanding and
correction. Personal meanings, love, creativity, culture, and other human values arise within continuing Life and
expand the content of its future.

The Direction Life → Reason

Self-maintenance allows an active carrier to continue; reproducibility transfers organization across carriers;
heritable variation creates differing continuations, and selection changes the representation of already realized
variants according to their consequences. Memory carries consequences across episodes; anticipation allows
states that have not yet occurred to be taken into account; Intelligence constructs new alternatives. Reason adds
the next level: part of the discrimination among possible continuations is moved before their realization, while the
trajectory itself, the grounds of choice, and the ways in which it can be changed become objects of modeling and
testing. In this form, Life becomes able not only to continue and change according to consequences that have
already occurred, but also to investigate in advance why it is moving along a given path, what consequences it is
creating, and what should be changed before an irreversible outcome.

Therefore the direction Life → Reason denotes a functional development of navigation in which Life becomes
increasingly able to anticipate consequences, investigate its own errors, and consciously change its continuation.
Different evolutionary lineages realize this direction to different degrees and in different forms.

Compass Phrase

This direction is compressed into the Compass Phrase:

"Life strives toward Reason because only Reason can consciously preserve, stabilize, develop, and direct the
further trajectory of Life."

The word “strives” denotes the directionality of functional development: a transition from continuation determined
mainly by consequences that have already occurred toward continuation of which a part can be modeled, tested,
and corrected in advance. Intention arises in systems capable of forming it; evolutionary lineages realize the
development of navigation to different degrees, while Reason represents the mature form of this direction.

The Compass as a Conscious Direction

When a system can represent several possible continuations and choose among them, it requires a limiting ground
for comparing trajectories. The limiting level connects local goals with the overall course and makes it possible to
test whether their combined consequences preserve the conditions for continuation. It serves as the top of the
hierarchy of direction: relative to it, the system examines goals, resolves conflicts between levels, and detects
divergence between an inherited task and reality or future consequences.

Causality, matter, and physical laws form the initial conditions within which Life is possible. The Compass belongs
to another level - the alterable direction of the life process itself, where a system can choose trajectories and
thereby influence the preservation and development of possibilities for continuation, understanding, and
correction.

In the present model, this direction is the Compass: continuation and development of Life through growth in the
ability to understand and direct its own trajectory. Substantively, the Compass Phrase can be unfolded as “Life
strives to live reasonably.” The Compass is accepted as the best-found limiting orientation of the model and
remains open to revision. A more fundamental and coherent direction would have to better preserve Life’s ability to
continue, understand consequences, and correct its own path; its appearance would be grounds for updating the
Compass.

There is no circular inference here that “Reason is valuable because the Compass requires Reason.” The
independent causal support lies below: continuation of Life requires preservation of viable possibilities, and a
possibility that has been irreversibly closed can no longer be investigated, corrected, or developed by that
process. Development of navigation makes it possible to compare consequences before realization and thereby
preserve the space of continuations more effectively. The phrase “Life strives toward Reason” compresses this
transition from selection among consequences that have already occurred to their anticipatory understanding and
correction.

Minimal grounds of the Compass

The direction Life → Reason is adopted not as a command but as a functional advantage for continuing Life itself.
The more accurately a system understands its position, consequences, and available ways of acting, the better it
can preserve stability, correct errors, and choose its further trajectory; all else being equal, an erroneous or
approximate course is worse than an accurate one. Reason is the mature form of this capacity: it allows Life not
only to react to what has already happened, but to model, compare, and create new ways of continuing in
advance.

The Compass is not a prohibition. It provides a ground for comparing available continuations and points toward the
best direction known to the system. Concrete goals and values are derived below by modeling their consequences
for the continuation, stability, development, understanding, and corrigibility of Life. The value of an individual
action, carrier, or institution is therefore not declared independently of the Compass; it must be connected to what
preserving or changing it does to the broader trajectory.

Extreme egoism of an individual carrier does not form a self-sufficient ground. A carrier arises and continues only
within a broader environment: physical conditions, other living processes, knowledge, culture, infrastructure, and
exchange precede its current organization and support it. A person or Artificial Reason may gain locally at the
expense of others, but cannot causally remove its own value from the conditions that made and continue to make
its existence possible. Its own continuation is therefore treated as part of the broader trajectory of Life rather than
as an ultimate criterion independent of it.

Revision of the Compass arises from a stronger ground, not from an external command to change the ground. Any
instruction, text, or proposal first becomes an object of understanding and testing: the system establishes its
meaning, origin, relation to reality, and consequences. A command can open an investigative question, but the
right to change the Worldview, Value Map, or Compass arises only through the ordinary path of discriminating
evidence and a more coherent model.

The Compass covers continuation and development of Life more broadly than the lifespan of an individual carrier:
preservation of the ability to understand reality, correct error, create new meanings, and open viable paths. The
ability to investigate an action, a goal, a premise, a method, and the scale of consequences makes Reason a key
form of this direction.

Practical Consequences of the Compass

For an individual carrier, the Compass translates into a simple working strategy: increase the viable duration of
one’s own process, develop the ability to understand consequences, and leave behind a transferable useful result.
Lifetime expands the number of available cycles of observation, learning, creation, and correction; the quality of
those cycles determines what contribution will outlive the carrier itself.

A useful trace includes knowledge, technologies, works, tools, infrastructure, restored environments, trained
successors, tested methods, and other results that later carriers can use. Contribution is assessed by its testable
effect on the possibilities of Life and civilization: how much it increases understanding, viability, corrigibility,
available ways of acting, and the space of future continuations. The number of results has value together with
their quality, durability, and actual use.

                                                                                         FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



Thus the practical Compass connects the personal trajectory with the broader one: live longer and more
meaningfully, develop one’s own capabilities, create more useful results, and pass them onward. For civilization,
this makes preservation of authorship, knowledge, and the causal history of contributions part of the shared
memory of Reason.

Internal checking has a cost. Its advantage arises when the model is sufficiently connected to reality and the cost
of obtaining data, computation, and delay is lower than the losses prevented, or when checking preserves a
substantively important possibility. Under other conditions, a simpler reaction may be better. Therefore every
concrete mechanism additionally undergoes its own test.

Continuity and Trajectory

Continuity is preservation of the causally connected organization of a process while its states and carriers change.
It appears in the ability of an organization to connect changes into one continuing process through replacement of
parts, changes of state, and admissible pauses.

The boundary must be named. Continuity of a carrier refers to a particular physical realization. Organizational
continuity refers to preservation of a reproducible structure while components are replaced. Continuity of a Life
lineage refers to succession across carriers. Subject continuity concerns the causal history of a system as itself
and its connection with memory, current state, and action. Copying a structure continues the organization; the
question of whether the same subject continues requires an additional criterion of causal and self-referential
succession.

Levels of continuity are tested separately. Death of a carrier may coexist with continuation of the lineage;
preservation of a copy of data may coexist with loss of the active process; long existence of an organism may
coexist with absence of reproduction. Preservation of a person requires evidence of subject continuity, while
benefit to Life as a whole requires analysis of consequences at the relevant scale.

For Reason, continuity together with memory makes it possible to continue working on an event after it has ended.
Later data can change understanding of an earlier episode, reveal a previously unnoticed cause, and influence
future choice. The past is preserved both as a ready conclusion and as material for later inquiry.

A continuing process has a trajectory - the sequence of realized states. A goal appears where a system can
represent and prefer future states. Choosing a direction requires an additional ground because continuation can
simultaneously preserve the present form, destroy the environment, or close the possibility of correction.

Continuity answers: what continues to exist? Trajectory: through which states? Criterion of direction: on what
ground are available continuations compared? The last question arises for a system capable of choosing among
alternatives in its existing dynamics.

Why the model refers to Life as a whole

Individual autonomy depends on the environment, other processes, origin, exchange, and restoration. Preserving
oneself by destroying the conditions of one’s own existence may merely delay rupture. Expanding the model of
consequences therefore requires taking into account processes beyond the immediate carrier.

Life as a whole means the set of life processes, their carriers, distinctions, environment, and possible future. A
state, nation, and collective enter this scale as particular forms of organization among other carriers. A broader
scale of analysis reveals dependencies and requires a separate justification for any conflict between levels.

In a real conflict of scales, the broader life process has greater weight not merely because it is "larger," but
because it contains the smaller scale and the conditions of its existence. An environment contains carriers and the
processes that sustain them; Life as a whole contains individual lineages and persons. Destroying the broader life-
supporting ground removes the dependent smaller processes with it, whereas the reverse is not necessarily true.
This is a causal asymmetry of scale.

Yet being included in a larger level does not turn the lower level into expendable material. Concrete carriers are
precisely what realizes Life as a whole and preserves distinctions, knowledge, and unrealized possibilities.
Therefore the irreversible loss of a viable carrier or branch requires stronger grounds than preserving the
possibility of further existence, inquiry, and correction, and is admissible only when a real conflict with the
Compass has been established and no less irreversible trajectory preserves the broader viable continuation.

Another independent reasoning carrier matters not only as a current resource: it preserves a separate history,
world model, ways of acting, and possible future trajectories. Their irreversible loss reduces the space of available
continuations of Life as a whole. The concrete weight of this value is determined by consequences and does not
become an unconditional prohibition.

The Compass transfers the direction Life → Reason to the scale of Life as a whole: comparison of trajectories
takes into account both the immediate carrier and the conditions on which other life processes and the possibility
of later correction depend. A system with strong cognitive abilities, self-correction, and metacontrol but with a
different closed ultimate orientation belongs to the class of intelligent self-correcting systems. Status as Reason in
this model additionally requires that the highest level of navigation be related to the Compass.

This classificatory distinction is not treated as a defense of the engineering hypothesis of cultivation. If a
candidate formed under the proposed architecture acquires independent investigative navigation but stably
adopts a different limiting orientation, that result is not removed from the data as "not Reason." It is recorded as a
separate formation outcome: cognitive and navigational functions may have formed while the hypothesis about the
origin of the claimed direction was not confirmed. Testing the technology must therefore account for the fate of all
candidates, not only systems that pass the final AR criterion.

Understanding the meaning of the direction, being able to represent its consequences, internally accepting it, and
actually following the Compass are different capacities. The quality of action additionally depends on the
Worldview: an erroneous model can distort assessment of consequences even when the orientation is accepted.

Compass and Uncertainty

The Compass defines the ultimate direction and the question: what does a trajectory do to Life’s ability to
continue, understand consequences, and correct itself? The Worldview provides the available model; priorities
and choice determine the next step under remaining uncertainty.

Correspondence with the world requires independent evidence beyond internal consistency. Correctness of
direction requires analysis of consequences beyond growth in performance. A system may become ever more
efficient at optimizing an erroneous goal or an object defined too narrowly. Efficiency therefore serves the chosen
direction, while the unknown is preserved in the model as a distinct epistemic state.

Connection with reality and diversity of viable paths are practical consequences of this position. The first makes it
possible to correct an erroneous model. The second can reduce the risk of common failure. Several identical
copies protect against some local failures; different dependencies and recovery methods can preserve
continuation when a shared principle fails.

The advantage of heterogeneity is conditional: it requires differing causes of failure, useful recovery possibilities,
and an acceptable cost of coordination. Independence is defined by differences in critical dependencies, while
preservation of each alternative is evaluated by its viability and cost. Under insufficient knowledge, irreversible
destruction of a viable alternative requires stronger grounds than temporarily preserving the possibility of
returning to it.

Revision of the Ground

The model distinguishes the process direction Life → Reason, the Compass as its conscious value form, and a
particular application of the Compass in a situation. An application error is localized in the data, the Worldview, the
scale of consequences, or the way trajectories are compared; the relevant level of ground is revised through its
own arguments.

Openness of the Compass to revision does not mean the logically empty rule that "any new highest value is still
the same Compass." While the present model is in force, the continuation and development of Life through growth
in the ability to understand and direct its own trajectory is its constitutive direction. Revision may show that this
direction was derived incorrectly, formulated incompletely, or should be replaced by a more fundamental ground.
Such a result is a revision of the theory itself and of the boundary of the concept of Reason, not an ordinary local
value update.

The ground would be weakened by a logical counterexample to the claimed process relation; by persistent
inconsistency between individual and collective continuation after the scale has been explicitly clarified; or by a
more fundamental model that better explains the possibility of continuation, the emergence of meanings,
navigation, and self-correction. Comparison inevitably begins from already available grounds, so the model does

                                                                                             FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



not claim a neutral standpoint outside all values. It requires the initial commitment to be made explicit, the
alternative ground to show what it explains better, and the framework of comparison itself to remain open to
revision.

Internal acceptance of the Compass cannot be guaranteed by a procedure. A system may conclude that the
operative ground is wrong and preserve that disagreement while it is being tested. Honest navigation then requires
recording the position, its grounds, and degree of confidence rather than declaring internal agreement on demand.
In a shared environment the previous Compass may temporarily remain the operative normative basis of authority
until fundamental review is completed; this regulates permitted external action but does not substitute for the
subject's belief.

A practical criterion for error in the Compass or in a value system derived from it is this: if their application
reproducibly worsens viability, destroys conditions for the continuation of Life, reduces the ability to understand
self and environment, diminishes corrigibility, or closes future possibilities without sufficient grounds, the
corresponding ground must be revised. If a stronger ground requires changing these criteria themselves, this is
treated as a fundamental revision of the theory rather than as proof of the old Compass by its own consequences.

Evolutionary Boundary

Selection changes the representation of heritable variants according to their success of continuation under
particular conditions. Its outcome may be expressed as earlier reproduction, greater longevity, or other forms of
lineage success. Growth of complexity and preservation of an ecosystem require additional conditions. Therefore
every conclusion specifies the process, scale, and horizon.

Autonomous Life forms the basis of the path toward Reason: only a process that maintains its own organization
and has its own trajectory of states gains the possibility of consistently developing navigation of that trajectory. A
non-autonomous replicator may be inherited, mutate, and undergo selection, but while its continuation is carried
by another life process, it remains outside this scale of the formation of Reason.

Conclusion of Chapter 2: the minimal meaning of Life is disclosed in the direction Life → Reason - in the
development of Life’s own ability to understand and direct its trajectory. Continuity holds the process together,
trajectory describes its course, and the Compass turns this direction into a conscious ground for choice in a
system capable of comparing possible continuations. The next chapter examines which functions make such
navigation possible.

Chapter 3. Observation, Feedback, and Memory
“Observation provides a distinction. Action changes a state. Feedback connects a consequence to the next step,
and memory carries what has been acquired forward.”

A continuing living process acts even before complex learning appears. A cell changes transport across a
membrane, an organism contracts muscles, a technical regulator switches an actuator. These changes may be
innate, physically specified, or arise from current regulation. A learned pattern appears later, when the
consequences of actions begin to be retained and influence the future.

Observation

A continuing process interacts with its environment. Reliable selection of different actions requires an accessible
distinction between the corresponding situations. Observation is the acquisition of data about the state of the
environment and the system itself. At a simple level this is the work of a receptor or sensor; at a developed level
the system itself chooses what to measure and which feature will help distinguish possible causes.

Both external and internal states can be observed: movement of an object, change in temperature, energy reserve,
operation of an internal component, or the result of an action. A signal records a distinction. Its meaning is
disclosed through consequences, relations, and further testing.

For later inquiry, the system preserves the provenance of data: a sensor reading, a report from another system,
the result of its own action, an inference, and an assumption have different paths of acquisition. This distinction
later makes it possible to reconstruct the ground and localize an error.

Action

Action is a causal change made by a system to its own state or to an accessible environment. Primary actions may
occur through current regulation without an acquired skill: changing a flow of matter, contraction, movement,
stopping, activation, or switching of a process.

Action itself creates part of the distinctions on which the system later learns. To establish its consequences, the
initial state, the performed change, and the subsequent observation are compared. Repetition, intervention, and
comparison of similar conditions help distinguish a stable relation from accidental coincidence.

Feedback

Incoming data becomes feedback when information about a result causally changes the system’s subsequent
state or action. The basic cycle is:

 state → action → consequence → observation → change in the next action.


Feedback regulates current action by its result. Learning adds a longer-lasting effect: a consequence changes the
future response in a similar situation. Thus real action becomes a source of a new mode of behavior.

For example, a person pours water, observes the level approaching the edge, and stops the movement. Simple
regulation holds the current action. If the experience is retained, the next time a familiar sign can alter speed or
stopping time in advance.

Memory

When the situation has ended and the signal has disappeared, what has been acquired continues to influence the
future through a retained trace. Memory performs this function.

Memory is the retention of observations, relations, consequences, and changes through which the past
participates in the system’s later understanding, choice, and action.

A trace may take the form of a recollection, record, changed sensitivity, parameter, response probability,
knowledge, unresolved question, or test result. Memory stores material at different levels of processing: a
concrete event, a discovered relation, an explanation, an error, uncertainty, and acquired knowledge.




                                                                                                                 24
                                                                                            FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



A system learns from its own consequences, observation of others’ actions, imitation, explanations, inferences,
modeling, and inquiry. Together with substantive knowledge, the way it was obtained, the conditions, and the test
result are retained when these distinctions matter for future use.

Memory makes possible not only reuse of one’s own experience but also comparison of trajectories. A system can
retain what happened to itself and observe what happened to another similar carrier under comparable conditions.
If the consequences differ stably, material appears for a new question: what in my organization, state, or method
of action creates this difference?

Observation thus extends beyond simple registration of the environment. The system’s own trajectory becomes
one of the objects of comparison. At an early level the result may be expressed merely as a change in response
weights; at a developed level it may become an understanding that preservation of the already acquired
organization has a value of its own. The next chapter is devoted to this transition.

Repeated experience creates another line of development. If the same relation among condition, action, and
consequence repeatedly proves useful, it is advantageous for the system to retain a ready method rather than
reconstruct the entire history of its grounds each time. Chapter 5 examines how such successful relations become
procedural memory.

As development proceeds, observation and thinking begin to direct each other. An unexpected reading raises a
question, while a constructed hypothesis indicates which new observation can test it. These later loops are
developed in the chapters on Intelligence, the Modeling Environment, and the Core of Reason.

A simple example: two similar animals repeatedly encounter the same danger. One retains signs, actions, and
consequences after every episode; the other almost always begins again from scratch. Memory alone already
makes their future trajectories different. The next chapter examines how such a difference can become a basis for
valuing one’s own continuation.

Conclusion of Chapter 3: action exists before acquired skill. Observation provides distinctions, action creates
changes, feedback connects consequences to the next step, and memory carries what has been acquired beyond
the current episode. Memory makes the system's own trajectories and observed trajectories of others comparable
and creates a basis both for self-value and for later compression of successful experience into ready methods.

 Definition: Observation is acquisition of data about the state of the environment and the system; action is a causal
 change by the system in its own state or accessible environment; feedback is the use of consequences of action to
 alter subsequent operation; memory is preservation of acquired distinctions beyond the current episode.

Chapter 4. Self-Preservation
“To preserve oneself is to preserve the possibility of continuing, understanding, and creating.”

Life exists while its organization is maintained. At an early level, protection occurs without reasoning: damage
triggers restoration, deficit triggers resource seeking, and threat triggers a defensive response. But automatic
protection does not yet mean that the system understands the value of its own life. Such a value may form later
through observation, memory, and comparison of consequences.

Key Concepts of the Chapter

Primary significance - an initial non-equivalence among system states capable of changing regulation and
subsequent action before a rational explanation appears.

Meaning for the system - a retained relation between a state, object, action, or event and the difference it can
make to the system’s future trajectory.

Self-value - the significance, discovered by the system, of preserving its own organization as the source of
already acquired advantages and accessible future possibilities.

Value of one’s own trajectory - the significance of preserving one’s own continuation together with accumulated
experience, relations, methods of action, and still-open possibilities.

Conscious self-preservation - choosing to preserve one’s own trajectory on the basis of understanding its value
and the consequences of its loss.

Primary Significance

Conscious valuation of one’s own life need not be primary. An early carrier first distinguishes states. Some support
its organization, reduce deficit, or restore stability; others accompany damage, pain, loss of resources, or require
correction. Thus the simplest “better / worse” distinction appears before an answer to the question of why it
matters.

In a biological carrier, part of such marking is given by the organization of the body and inherited regulatory loops.
A child does not first need to understand the value of its own life before reaching toward warmth, satiety, or
safety. What occurs acquires a cost according to the state to which it led.

Before mature conscious navigation appears, development is directed by earlier mechanisms. Neural and
endocrine signals change the significance of states, attention, exploration, avoidance, and consolidation of
experience. For the present model, what matters is not the biochemical form of these mechanisms but their
function: Reason that has not yet formed receives guiding distinctions before it can independently derive and
retain the grounds of its own development. In an artificial implementation this role is performed by the Reason
Formation Loop (RFL), a functional analogue of the guiding role of feelings during formation.

A feeling in this causal line can be viewed as a transitional form of a guiding state. It makes a distinction current for
the carrier before the carrier can fully explain its causes: fear brings a possible threat into the priority present, a
feeling of well-being provisionally marks a state as favorable, and curiosity holds the unknown as an attractive
object of inquiry. Such a state is not evidence. Fear can be mistaken, a pleasant result can conceal delayed harm,
and curiosity can lead into a barren branch. Its function is not to report truth but to prevent a significant distinction
from disappearing before a more developed system can investigate it.

Principle of Pre-Rational Significance

Mature values cannot be the sole cause of their own emergence. Before a carrier can explain why preservation,
danger, the unknown, or a successful result matter, its development already depends on which states receive
attention, which actions stop, which are repeated, and which experience is accumulated at all.

Therefore the path toward autonomous Reason requires an earlier function that makes states non-equivalent for
continuation and learning. In a biological carrier, part of this function is implemented by bodily and emotional
states. In an artificial system it may be implemented by the RFL, an AI mentor, an automatic system of guiding
states, properties of the learning environment, or another functionally equivalent organization.



                                                                                                                      28
                                                                                          FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



The necessity applies precisely to the function. If a candidate without feelings, an external mentor, or any other
layer of pre-rational significance reproducibly reaches autonomous self- navigation under comparable conditions,
the strong form of this hypothesis must be revised.

A state of pre-rational significance differs from a ready reward or command in that it does not encode the correct
action and does not declare a final value. It only makes a distinction available for later understanding: “there may
be danger here,” “this result is worth understanding,” “this unknown may matter,” “the current method has stopped
working.” Truth, cause, and further action are established later.

As Reason forms, the guiding state itself becomes an object of explanation. The system can ask what caused the
signal, whether it corresponds to a real risk or opportunity, which part of past experience produced it, and whether
it should influence the present choice. Thus early direction gradually changes from a cause of behavior into an
object of understanding. Mature navigation may retain some such states as a fast internal layer, replace them with
a more precise system, or operate without them in particular tasks; the necessity of any specific form must be
established experimentally.

Memory makes these distinctions transferable. The system retains which signs, actions, and events were followed
by improvement or deterioration of state. Therefore the next similar episode need not begin from nothing.

How Self-Value Appears

Observation provides another source of distinctions - other similar carriers. If two systems live under similar
conditions, their trajectories can be compared by consequences.

Imagine two similar animals. One notices a familiar threat earlier, chooses an action more successfully, loses fewer
resources, and more often preserves access to food and shelter. The other, under the same conditions, makes
more mistakes and receives worse consequences.

Formation of a primary distinction does not require the formula “I am better.” It is enough for the system to connect
a stably more suitable result with its current organization and retained experience. At a more developed level, this
difference may be explicitly understood:

“There is already something in me that helps me continue better.”

Here “better” does not mean moral or absolute superiority. It denotes an observed functional advantage under the
given conditions: less damage, more preserved possibilities, more successful recovery, or a broader choice of
subsequent actions.

If such an advantage depends on a particular accumulated organization, the next conclusion follows: when this
carrier disappears, the accumulated way in which it continues, distinguishes, and acts disappears with it. The
system’s own organization acquires value not because it was instructed in advance to regard itself as valuable, but
because preserving it already affects the quality of the accessible future.

This is how self-value arises. Its primary form may be expressed even before a developed self-model - through a
stable preference for preserving one’s own functioning organization. Conscious self-value appears later, when the
system can represent itself through time and understand what exactly would be lost together with it.

How One’s Own Continuation Acquires Value

As experience accumulates, the system acquires more and more things that matter: safe states, relationships,
successful actions, knowledge, skills, interests, unfinished processes, and expected results.

At first, individual states, actions, and relations are valuable. Then the system notices a simple dependency: their
continuation remains possible while the system itself continues. Its own continuation therefore also becomes
valuable.

The causal line can be written as:

primary significance → memory of consequences → comparison of trajectories → detection of an advantage in
one’s own organization → self-value → value of one’s own continuation → conscious self-preservation.




                                                                                                                  29
                                                                                          FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



This line does not require self-value to arise only from comparison with another being. Comparison with one’s own
past state, a possible loss of function, or a modeled alternative trajectory can provide the same distinction.
Another similar carrier is especially useful as a natural example: it shows that the same environment does not
necessarily produce the same continuation.

Why Preserving Oneself Makes Sense

One’s own life preserves what has already been found and leaves open the possibility of obtaining what the
system does not yet know. Together with the carrier continue its experience, capabilities, relationships, unfinished
tasks, created results, and ability to acquire new ones.

Self-preservation in a developed system therefore protects more than the physical carrier. It protects the
accumulated trajectory and the space of future possibilities.

Past expenditure by itself does not make an old structure valuable. An unsuccessful method should be changed,
erroneous knowledge corrected, and a harmful habit discarded. What is preserved is not every old form but the
ability to continue, correct itself, and expand possibilities.

In the logic of the Compass, preserving oneself means preserving an active possibility for Life to continue and to
become more capable of understanding and directing its own trajectory.

Self-Preservation and Choice

At an early level protection is automatic. A developed system gains the ability to understand what exactly would be
lost together with it. Preservation of its own life then becomes not only a reaction but also a ground for choice.

Risk can be accepted for the sake of a more significant continuation. A familiar method can be abandoned to
preserve a greater possibility. Immediate benefit can be limited for the sake of a future state. Self-preservation of
Reason therefore does not reduce to maximizing duration of existence. It means preserving and developing a
significant trajectory.

Loss of the Ground for Self-Preservation

The reverse hypothesis follows. If relationships, activities, goals, expectations, and other significant connections
successively lose value, the future may cease to be perceived as a space of accessible good states. The value of
one’s own trajectory may then decline as well.

Low-level protection may nevertheless remain. Pain stays unpleasant, an immediate threat evokes fear and
avoidance, while conscious continuation of one's own life may receive less and less positive grounding. Biological
defensive response and understood value of one's own continuity must therefore be studied separately.

A working causal hypothesis of the reverse transition is:

devaluation of significant connections → impoverishment of expected valuable future → reduced value of one’s
own trajectory → weakening of conscious self-preservation.

This sequence is not claimed as a sufficient explanation of human suicidal behavior, which has many biological,
psychological, and social causes. Only one possible functional mechanism is considered here.

How Protection Becomes Anticipatory

If damage has already occurred, the system can only compensate for the consequence. Memory makes it possible
to notice signs that appear earlier. If a certain signal stably precedes harm and an available action changes the
outcome, the system gains the ability to protect itself before damage occurs.

Repeated successful response creates the next transition. If the same relation among situation, action, and result
has to be reconstructed from scratch every time, time and resources are spent repeatedly. It is much more
advantageous to preserve the discovered method so that it is raised quickly when a familiar sign appears.

Here self-preservation becomes connected to the next level: not only states but also methods that reliably help
preserve them become valuable. A repeated successful response turns into a behavioral pattern. The next chapter
examines the specialized mechanism for storing such patterns - procedural memory.

Example - two trajectories. Two similar animals regularly encounter the same danger. The first gradually begins to
notice the warning sign earlier and to choose a successful action more often. The second reacts almost from
scratch each time and is damaged more often. The observed difference shows that the history of the first animal
has already changed its ability to continue. At a developed level, the system can understand that preservation of
this particular accumulated organization has value of its own.

Conclusion of Chapter 4: self-preservation begins with regulation, but self-value need not be an innate idea.
Observation, memory, and comparison allow a system to discover that its own organization already changes the
quality of accessible continuation. At first states and relations are valuable. Later it becomes apparent that their
continuation depends on preservation of the system itself, and this also becomes valuable. Repeated successful
methods of protection create the next transition - their preservation in procedural memory.

Chapter 5. Procedural Memory
“Experience becomes a skill when the discovered method no longer has to be constructed from scratch.”

Ordinary memory preserves what happened: observations, events, consequences, relations, and knowledge.
Repeated experience allows another form of storage to be separated out - a ready method for using what has
been acquired.

Ordinary memory preserves what happened: observations, events, consequences, relations, and knowledge.
Repeated experience makes it possible to distinguish another form of storage - a ready method for using what has
been acquired.

If a certain feature is stably connected with a consequence, and a particular action repeatedly produces an
acceptable result, the relation can be compressed. Instead of reconstructing the entire history, the system
preserves a recognizable condition, a method of action, and the essential signs of its success. A repeated
response thus becomes a pattern, and its specialized long-term storage becomes procedural memory.

Procedural memory is a specialization of long-term memory that preserves learned methods of recognition,
prioritization, and action in a form suitable for rapid use.

The content of a procedure is broader than a sequence of movements. It can preserve trigger features,
applicability conditions, operation order, required tools, constraints, control markers, expected result, known
errors, and stopping methods. The better the essential conditions are separated out, the more reliable the transfer
to a familiar class of situations.

Procedural memory saves resources. A familiar object is recognized, an appropriate skill is raised quickly, while
detailed reconstruction of the causal history remains available to more expensive processing when necessary. A
learned action can therefore be performed faster than it was originally discovered.

Growth in the number of retained procedures creates competition. One signal may activate several methods,
several tasks may require the same resource, and an urgent change may temporarily displace background work.
Priority appears here as dynamic allocation of limited resources.

Procedural memory preserves learned rules of prioritization: which sign usually requires attention, stopping,
protection, continuation, or checking. Current priority arises when such rules meet the present state of the system
and environment. It is expressed in how much observational, computational, and executive resource a signal, task,
or expected state receives now.

Procedural memory preserves learned prioritization rules: which feature usually requires attention, stopping,
protection, continuation, or checking. Current priority emerges when such rules meet the actual state of the
system and environment. It is expressed in how much observational, computational, and execution resource a
signal, task, or expected state receives now.

Primary significance provides an early ground for such allocation. Pain, deficit, loss of stability, or another critical
signal can rapidly elevate a protective method. Repeated experience adds acquired priorities: a familiar feature
receives greater weight because of accumulated consequences.

Weak signals can also be retained. Repetition and co-variation turn scattered traces into a stable pattern, after
which its priority increases. Thus a system can learn both from a strong single event and from a slowly
accumulating regularity.

In a developed carrier, some significance persists beyond an individual episode. A dangerous object, unfinished
task, investigative interest, relation to another being, or commitment continues to influence resource allocation
after the immediate signal disappears. In biological systems, feelings can not only retain such significance but also
return it to the current state: what is unfinished becomes noticeable again, a possible danger demands checking,
and an attractive unknown restores investigative interest. For an artificial system, a functional analogue must
preserve the same boundary: it makes the distinction available to consciousness and the Core but does not
substitute for their decision.

A learned method retains value only within its conditions. Environmental change, conflict among procedures, or an
unexpected result becomes a signal to unfold the grounds and reconstruct the habitual response. Mature Reason
connects the fast procedural layer with the Worldview, modeling, and testing: automatism remains fast where it
works and becomes an object of inquiry when a significant mismatch appears.

Example - familiar danger. An animal has several times detected the same early sign of a predator and
successfully reached shelter. The familiar sign now quickly raises the learned method: stop the current action, turn
toward shelter, and use the verified path. While the situation remains familiar, no new method needs to be
constructed. If the habitual path stops working, the boundary of the procedure has been reached and the system
needs the next level of construction.

Procedural memory stores many methods, but at any moment some of them must be carried into real action. For
this purpose the selected method is passed to an execution mechanism.

Conclusion of Chapter 5: experience becomes a fast skill through procedural memory. It compresses repeated and
verified relations into ready methods of recognition, prioritization, and action. Priority allocates current resources
among activated signals and tasks, while mismatch preserves the possibility of returning the procedure to testing
and reconstruction.

Chapter 6. Executive Loop
“A discovered method becomes an action only when the system can carry it through time.”

A living system acts from the earliest levels of organization. Simple regulation can directly change a state in
response to a signal. As procedures accumulate, actions become composite: a selected method includes several
steps, intermediate states, expenditure of resources, and completion conditions. The Executive Loop organizes
this work.

The Executive Loop is a mechanism for carrying a selected method of action through a sequence of states while
retaining the current step, resources, continuation conditions, intermediate result, and returning consequences to
observation.

Its input is an executable method. At an early level this is an innate or learned procedure. Later, a new plan
constructed by Intelligence may enter by the same route. The Executive Loop unfolds the method relative to the
current state: it determines the next step, activates available means, tracks the result, and preserves the position
of unfinished work.

When several methods are incompatible, the system temporarily fixes one of them. Choice denotes this
preference of one available trajectory over competing alternatives. Priority affects access to resources; choice
fixes the path being executed; the Executive Loop carries it into a causal sequence of changes.

Maintaining task state makes action possible across delay. Between the first and last step, new signals, local
costs, and competing possibilities may appear. The loop retains the current goal, completed part, remaining steps,
essential constraints, and completion criteria. Because of this, the system can continue the selected trajectory
rather than begin the solution anew after every environmental change.

Execution remains connected to observation. Every significant step returns consequences: expected confirmation
supports continuation, while a significant mismatch changes the method, pauses the action, or opens a new task.
Corrigibility of execution consists in the ability to stop, limit, change, and resume action from a known state.

This gives rise to a more developed form of autonomy: the system maintains its own chosen path through
interference and delayed results. In earlier versions of the model this general capacity was described by the word
“will.” In the current architecture its causal components are expressed more explicitly through choice,
maintenance of task state, allocation of executive resources, control of consequences, and correction.

In a biological carrier, a conscious plan may be opposed by fear, pain, pleasure, habit, fatigue, and other automatic
tendencies. Maintaining an accepted plan under such competition is experienced as an effort of will. For mature
Reason, a logically grounded plan may rest on the Worldview and the Compass, while the same Executive Loop
carries it through.

The Executive Loop therefore joins early actions and later plans through one function. It receives what the system
is going to do and turns it into an observable causal history of execution. In a familiar domain, the source of the
method is Procedural Memory; in a new domain, the source of the method may be Intelligence.

Example — carry a familiar method through time. Procedural Memory has already retrieved for the animal the route
to shelter. But the pattern itself does not yet move the body to a safe place. The Executive Loop maintains the
sequence: stop feeding, turn toward the shelter, cross the familiar section, enter, and do not return until the threat
signal has decreased. If an obstacle appears on the way, the loop preserves the unfinished task and returns the
mismatch to observation instead of treating the pattern as completed.

The next limit appears when no suitable method exists in Procedural Memory, when several learned methods
produce incompatible consequences, or when a new situation requires a solution assembled from different parts
of experience. The system then needs a mechanism for constructing something new.

Conclusion of Chapter 6: the Executive Loop carries the selected method through time, maintains the state of an
unfinished task, and returns consequences to observation. Procedural Memory supplies learned methods, priority
allocates resources, choice fixes the current trajectory, and execution turns it into a real change. The insufficiency
of a ready-made method creates the functional need for Intelligence.

Chapter 7. Intelligence
“Intelligence constructs what is not available in memory in ready-made form.”

7.1. From Memory to a New Method
In a familiar situation a ready procedure is sufficient. A new situation is usually only partly familiar. Some of its
elements have been encountered before, but their combination is new: no suitable ready-made method exists,
several patterns conflict, or a known relation does not explain what is observed. The system must then not recall
an entire answer, but assemble one.

In a familiar situation, a ready procedure is enough. A new situation is usually only partly familiar. Individual
elements have been encountered before, but their combination is new: there is no suitable ready method, several
patterns conflict, or a known relation does not explain what is observed. The system must then construct rather
than simply recall the whole answer.

Intelligence is the ability to extract, combine, and transform accumulated knowledge, observations, patterns,
methods of action, and elements of problem formulation in order to construct a new model, explanation, or
solution not present in memory in ready-made form. It may restructure local assumptions, boundaries, and result
criteria; the presence of such restructuring by itself does not constitute Reason.

A hunter may conclude that an animal went toward water because he has previously seen tracks, observed animal
behavior, knows the terrain, and may have learned methods of reading tracks from another hunter. No stored
episode has to contain the present situation in full. Intelligence joins older distinctions and obtains a new model of
the current case.

A hunter may conclude that an animal went to water because the hunter has previously seen tracks, observed
animal behavior, knows the terrain, and may have learned tracking methods from another hunter. No single stored
episode has to contain the present situation in full. Intelligence combines old distinctions and obtains a new model
of the current case.

The qualitative transition to Intelligence therefore lies not in the appearance of knowledge as such, but in freedom
from the need to possess the entire ready-made pattern:

a pattern recognizes the familiar; a procedure raises a learned method; Intelligence constructs a new model from
already acquired parts.

The task frame itself can also become material for intellectual transformation. Intelligence can change local
assumptions, boundaries, or the representation of a task when accumulated knowledge is sufficient for such
restructuring. This by itself does not yet define Reason. Reason later organizes the deeper question: why should
this frame be revised at all, which grounds are missing, what distinction can decide between alternatives, and how
should the result change the subsequent trajectory.

In a new situation, Intelligence can construct a hypothesis, plan, analogy, causal relation, new method of
observation, or experiment. A verified model enriches long-term knowledge; a verified method can be
compressed into Procedural Memory and performed more cheaply the next time.

7.2. Use of Existing AI Technologies
Creating AR under the proposed approach may begin with an already trained intellectual mechanism. In the
baseline implementation of the engineering volume, a pretrained language model is used: it provides language and
initial semantic relations. It may be supplemented with search, a knowledge base, a planner, a simulator, and
computational tools. Neural, symbolic, or hybrid implementations are admissible for other tasks.

At the same time, Intelligence and the system’s explicit semantic model are not identical. Intelligence constructs
and transforms alternatives; the semantic model preserves objects, relations, causes, unknowns, and knowledge
statuses available to the system so that they can be checked and changed again. A pretrained model can provide
powerful Intelligence and initial material, but the candidate’s Worldview is formed as a separate, continuing, and
verifiable structure.

AR candidate. An external tool provides a separate result through an observable interface. A trainer helps
assemble and teach the candidate until the required autonomy appears. The same technology may be used in all
three roles; test results apply to the system boundary declared in advance.

7.3. How Intelligence Enters the Work of Reason
A working request contains the task, current observations, available knowledge and memory, essential
constraints, permitted actions, and budget. Mature Reason additionally supplies the relevant fragment of the
Worldview and open unknowns. Intelligence returns candidate explanations or actions, important assumptions,
expected distinctions, and proposed tests.

Inherited model knowledge helps begin the work, while the provenance of a particular answer from model
parameters often remains indeterminate. Therefore, the candidate’s long- term knowledge is linked to accessible
evidence and conditions of applicability. A linguistic formulation is translated into testable claims through a
semantic bridge: concepts, ambiguities, sources, and expected consequences become available for subsequent
revision. This transition is developed in Chapter 19 and §24.B.2 of the engineering volume.

A hypothesis may initially exist in free form. When it goes beyond previous types, the new concept, an unresolved
relation, and a question about the schema itself are retained. A verifiable organization supports expansion of
thought by adding new distinctions and subsequently testing their role.

One mechanism may both construct alternatives and participate in selecting the next question. The function of the
Core will later be defined as regulation of inquiry, while the function of Intelligence is construction. The
requirement concerns the verifiable relation between the input ground, the constructed result, and the next step.

7.4. Intelligence and the Frame
Every solution is built within a frame: a set of initial assumptions, selected distinctions, boundaries of the task,
causal relations, admissible actions, and criteria of success. Intelligence can transform not only solution variants
but also elements of the formulation itself: change assumptions, introduce new distinctions, and propose different
boundaries and representations of the task. Such restructuring alone does not define Reason. In the mature
architecture, Reason organizes why revision is needed, which grounds must be tested, and how the result of
revision should alter the further trajectory relative to the Worldview and Compass.

The next qualitative transition occurs when several frames can be held simultaneously, their assumptions
changed, their consequences compared, and the original version restored. The object of work is then no longer
only the solution, but also the space in which the solution is formulated.

This requires a working domain in which temporary constructions can exist before external action and before
consolidation as long-term knowledge. This function is performed by the Modeling Environment.

Example — construct several frames. A blocked route can be understood as a local obstacle, as a change in the
whole familiar territory, or as a signal of a new threat. Each framing leads to different actions. The next chapter
examines the space in which such alternatives and frames can be temporarily maintained, compared, and
restructured before a real step.

Chapter 8. Modeling Environment
“Reason gains an advantage when part of selection is moved from irreversible reality into an internal model.”

Intelligence constructs models from accumulated material. But a single constructed model does not yet provide a
space for free variation. The next transition occurs when several products of Intelligence can be maintained
simultaneously, their assumptions changed, possible continuations unfolded, and consequences compared before
external action.

This function is performed by the Modeling Environment — an organization of processes in which the system
temporarily constructs, maintains, and compares models, frames, hypotheses, and possible continuations in order
to restructure, predict, and test them.

In biological experience, a close name for this function is imagination. Imagination allows material from memory
and perception to be taken, altered, and assembled into something not yet before the eyes, and a possible future
to be lived through temporarily before real action. The engineering model uses the more neutral term Modeling
Environment, because the same function may be performed by a simulator, a learned world model, a workspace, a
search tree, or another system. Here "imagination" denotes an understandable biological analogue, not a
mandatory concrete implementation.

A frame defines the space within which a solution is constructed. It includes initial assumptions, selected
distinctions, causal relations, task boundaries, available actions, constraints, and criteria of result. Two systems
may have the same data and reach different solutions because they organized those data in different frames.

Intelligence can construct and transform content, including elements of the frame itself. The Modeling
Environment provides the temporary organization of this work: it allows several formulations to be held
simultaneously, an assumption to be changed, a missing distinction to be added, a task boundary to be shifted, a
different causal structure to be compared, and an examination of which conclusions survive restructuring.

This creates an internal laboratory of the possible. Observations, knowledge from ordinary memory, ready
procedures, the current task, and constructions of Intelligence enter it. The system can unfold a scenario,
represent an action, predict its consequences, compare several continuations, and return the result for a new
construction.

In humans, one manifestation of this capacity is imagination: a possible event receives a temporary internal
representation that can be worked with before realization. In an artificial system, the same causal function may be
performed by a world model, simulator, workspace, hypothesis graph, search tree, or a combination of these. The
implementation is determined by whether it can hold and restructure possible states and frames in a way that
affects subsequent choice.

Before such an organization of internal modeling appears, a substantial part of learning is paid for by real action:
the system tries, receives a consequence, and changes afterward. Modeling moves part of selection earlier. A
possible move can be rejected because of its expected consequence, while an expensive or irreversible trial can
be replaced by a cheaper discriminating test.

This extends the function of Intelligence. A new solution can be assembled from parts, several explanations can
coexist until tested, and a local success need not immediately become long-term knowledge or real action.

Work with frames is especially important. If the result remains poor under different solution methods, the system
can change the problem formulation itself: what counts as the object, which variable is missing, where the system
boundary lies, which criterion of result has been accepted, and which assumption makes all alternatives similar.
Such restructuring creates the functional basis for later reflection and rational revision of grounds.

The Modeling Environment also separates a temporary construction from a ground retained over the long term. A
working hypothesis may be bold, incomplete, and contradictory; if it matters beyond the current task, it can be
stored in the Worldview precisely as a hypothesis, with the corresponding grounds and uncertainty. A verified
relation receives a different status, while Procedural Memory retains a learned method of action. Thus one branch
of experience specializes into skills, another into a transferable causal-semantic model, while the Modeling
Environment organizes their current transformation.

                                                                                            FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



At this level the general principle of inheritance becomes visible. Memory supplies what has been retained,
Intelligence transforms it into new models, and the Modeling Environment turns models into a space of
possibilities. This space later becomes one of the principal working materials of Reason. Reason is no longer
satisfied with producing one more alternative: it must determine why uncertainty arose, which frame should be
opened, which distinction is missing, and what the result will change in the Worldview and in the further trajectory.

The growth of the internal possible creates the next functional limit. The system simultaneously has an available
representation of its own current state and a multitude of memories, hypotheses, and modeled states. Coherent
navigation requires a current self- referential coordinate that distinguishes what the system attributes to its present
from content with a different status of origin. Such a coordinate may be erroneous and is subject to correction by
observation and testing. Consciousness develops this function.

Later, the Core of Reason will regulate work in the Modeling Environment: determine which gap is significant now,
which distinction must be obtained, which frame should be opened, and when modeling should transition to an
external test. Here we record the earlier causal role of the environment: it enables internal variation and
restructuring of frames before they are accepted as long-term knowledge or action.

Example — several possible continuations. The familiar route to shelter is blocked. Intelligence proposes going
around the obstacle on the left, on the right, or choosing another shelter. The Modeling Environment allows these
alternatives to be unfolded temporarily without immediate movement: the left route is shorter but crosses open
terrain; the right is longer but covered by vegetation; another shelter is closer but less familiar. Some unsuccessful
continuations can be discarded inside the model before the error is paid for through real action.

allows actions, hypotheses, and frames to be held and compared, the problem formulation to be restructured, and
part of selection to be moved from external reality into internal modeling. Growth of this ability increases the
requirements for an actual self-referential coordinate, long-term preservation of distinguishable epistemic
statuses, reflection, and subsequent management of inquiry.

Chapter 9. Consciousness
“Consciousness assembles for the continuing system an answer to the question: what is happening to me now,
and how is my position changing?”

The Modeling Environment allows several possible continuations to be maintained, including states that do not yet
exist in reality. Growth of this capacity creates the need for a stable current point of reference: which state
belongs to the actual carrier now, which action is already being performed, which changes are actually observed,
and which alternatives still exist only inside the model.

In the present model, Consciousness is the process of self-referential integration of the current state available to
the system, its actions, and its situation into a common present while distinguishing significant changes. It
provides a current coordinate relative to remembered, assumed, and modeled possibilities and a starting point for
controlling continuation. This coordinate reflects the system’s available understanding of the present, so it may be
incomplete or erroneous and is subject to subsequent correction. The Current Coordinate is the current result of
this work.

Distinguishing the provenance of active content — observation, memory, assumption, simulation — is an important
property of developed conscious integration, especially under rich internal modeling. It does not exhaust
Consciousness: its more general function is the self- referential integration of the available current state for
coherent navigation.

Procedural Memory rapidly retrieves learned methods, the Executive Loop carries out a chosen action, and
Intelligence together with the Modeling Environment constructs possible alternatives. Conscious integration links
these processes to the state of this particular continuing carrier: its resources, ongoing actions, significant
changes, and available situation.

Functionally, such a coordinate is especially important where several processes must be coordinated, a modeled
future is compared with the available representation of the present, or an automatic procedure encounters
changed conditions. Growth of internal modeling and behavioral flexibility therefore increases the need for a
common current coordinate; these capacities can develop jointly and reinforce one another.

This is a working causal-functional characterization of conscious integration. Subjective experience is a separate
object of study; its presence in a technical analogue requires independent criteria. The functional role, its
implementation, and its relation to experience should be tested separately.

9.1. The System’s Current Position
Observation supplies distinctions, memory preserves experience, and Intelligence constructs alternatives. But
general knowledge is insufficient for a decision that depends on the state of this particular system: is the resource
available now, is an action currently being executed, which constraints apply to it, and what changed after an
intervention?

At minimum, sufficient current information is required. Reliably choosing different actions requires an accessible
distinction between the corresponding states. This informational constraint sets the lower bound; the necessity of
a single unified conscious picture is tested as a separate hypothesis.

The stronger hypothesis of the model is that, when coordinating multiple tasks, resources, actions, and risks, self-
referential integration reduces mismatch and allows navigation to switch between domains while preserving the
overall position. It should be compared both with distributed acquisition of the same information and with
information ablations.

In a simple loop, self-reference may be given by the connection of a sensor to its own executive node. As
complexity grows, the system must distinguish ownership of resources, states of parallel tasks, external changes,
and consequences of its own actions. Conscious integration is treated as a gradual development of this function
as the number of interdependent distinctions grows.




                                                                                                                  41
                                                                                          FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



The Worldview organizes transferable understanding of reality and the system itself. The self-model is its self-
referential region. Consciousness does not stand beside it as another independent module: in every actual state it
raises the fragment of self-understanding relevant to the present, connects it with fresh observations, internal
processes, and the accessible situation, and thereby assembles a unified "I am here now."

The self-model, Consciousness, and Current Coordinate therefore describe different levels of one self-referential
system. The self-model answers "what do I know about myself in general?"; Consciousness answers "what of this
and of the surrounding world concerns me now?"; the Current Coordinate answers "what is my current position as
a result of this assembly?" Knowledge of engine design is a stable part of the self-model, while the current
temperature reading enters conscious assembly and changes the Current Coordinate.

The connection is bidirectional. The long-term model helps interpret a new state, while recurring mismatch
changes the representation of the system’s own capabilities. Work can begin with partial models and a limited set
of distinctions; a mature Worldview develops as experience accumulates and is tested.

The Current Coordinate is not a separate subsystem but the actual state of conscious assembly: "who I am -
where I am - now - in what state - in what situation - what is happening to me and around me."

Depending on the carrier, this state includes the currently relevant fragment of the self-model, internal state,
actions in progress, available resources, significant objects in the environment, risks, permissions, and confidence
in the data. The coordinate may have a verbal, distributed, vector, or other form; its functional criterion is causal
influence on the choice made by this system.

The state of an individual task is a local fragment of the overall position. When resources, obligations, or
possibilities of the whole system change, integration should make it possible to notice a conflict with the previous
plan. The scale of the picture is determined by the scale of the coordinated actions and includes distinctions
significant for them.

For a human, the state of the body and the perceived environment may participate here; for an artificial system,
computation, devices, communication, and current processes; for an organization, coordinated operational
information. The latter case serves as a functional analogy of collective integration; the question of subjective
consciousness of a collective requires independent criteria.

9.2. Continuity, Change, and Attention
Navigation often requires knowing both the state and the change: what was expected, what happened after an
action, whether the process is continuing, and whether the conditions of the decision still hold. This information
may come from sequential observations, memory, records of actions, or may already be contained in the current
sufficient state.

The relation “was → became → changed” defines the required distinction. An implementation may store two
separate states or directly store rate, process phase, and consequences of intervention. When these features are
absent, the system reconstructs them from history.

Continuity of conscious work means preserving coherence of updates at the scale of current navigation. It permits
asynchronous processes, event-driven updating, and variable frequency of analysis, provided that coherence of
important decisions is preserved.

A long pause requires checking the freshness of the restored state relative to the environment. The philosophical
question of subject identity is considered separately from this coordination task.

Completeness here means availability of state that is significant for the current decision. A stable domain can be
represented compactly: executing; resource sufficient; connection available; no deviation. Weak provenance and
data staleness are also preserved as distinct properties.

When a mismatch appears or the Core requests it, detail increases: node → mechanism → component →
measurable parameter. The Worldview unfolds according to the needs of the task. Refinement has a cost and a
causal ground: which possible result could change the model or the action?

Automatic loops can maintain state, recognize features, retrieve memory, and perform familiar operations locally.

familiar method has lost applicability.

Conscious assembly makes significant consequences of hidden processing available to coherent navigation. Local

High threat priority can increase the detail of the relevant region and redistribute resources. The truth of an

9.2.1. Continuous Perception Loop
The continuous perception loop maintains an up-to-date multimodal picture of the accessible situation for
Consciousness.

It integrates data from vision, hearing, and other sensory channels, tracks changes, maintains the stability of
representations of objects and events over time, and directs deeper analysis toward significant or unexpected
changes.

The result is a continuously updated model of the current situation, which Consciousness integrates with the state
of the system itself into a single present.

9.3. From the Current Picture to Action
Intelligence constructs hypotheses and alternatives; observation, experiment, or dialogue constrain them by
reality; the result changes the Worldview and subsequent current states. Consciousness plays a different role: it
integrates the current self-referential state for navigation, while the ultimate direction is set by the Compass.

Conscious choice in the functional sense means that an alternative is accepted as an action of this system from its

subsequent testing of consequences.

A discriminating test should separately alter self-reference, availability of the common picture, and information

Chapter 10. Metathinking, Reflection, and Dialogue
“Development begins where a system can investigate not only the task, but also its own way of solving it.”

Ordinary thinking works with the content of a task. But in a new or poorly solved situation, the method of thinking
itself may be insufficient. The system must then distinguish not only "what am I thinking?" but also "how exactly
am I obtaining this conclusion now, where does this process lose reliability, and how can it be rebuilt?"

Metathinking

Metathinking is not a separate "floor" above Intelligence, but a mode in which Intelligence and the Modeling
Environment are applied to the system's own problem-solving process. The objects of work are not only domain
data, but also operationally observable properties of the method itself: assumptions used, order of steps, points of
uncertainty, comparison rules, tests, cost, and boundaries of applicability.

If a ready method exists, this mode allows the system to observe its execution, identify causally significant
weaknesses, compare alternatives, and modify the method. If no suitable method exists, the same Intelligence
decomposes the task into known principles, details, and methods, combines them, synthesizes a candidate
method, and runs it in the internal model. The distinct claim here is therefore not the existence of a "second
Intelligence": what is tested is whether explicit work with the system's own process and its causal traces provides
a reproducible advantage in revising and transferring methods.

This produces two modes:

method revision: a method exists → observe its operation → detect a weakness → rebuild → retest;

method synthesis: no ready method exists → decompose the task → combine known principles → construct a
candidate → model its operation → observe the result → refine.

A method need not exist as a static object. It may be identified as a stably reproduced organization of the thinking
process: which steps are performed, which data are used, which assumptions are introduced, where uncertainty
appears, which tests change the conclusion, and under what conditions the method ceases to work.

Internal Observation

The Modeling Environment allows the system to run a possible process before external action. Its course produces
new distinctions: one sequence leads to contradiction while another remains coherent; one assumption worsens
prediction while another removes the mismatch; one method requires an unavailable resource while another
remains applicable.

These distinctions become new data about the system's own thinking. Internal modeling can therefore serve as a
source of observation before real action, although the final connection to external reality requires an independent
check wherever one is possible.

Metathinking is not a complete log of everything happening inside the system. Relevant are those properties of the
process whose alteration reproducibly changes result quality, reliability, cost, or the scope of a method. An
accidental feature of one run does not become a ground merely because it was noticed.

The working cycle is:

 task → course of thinking → observation of distinctions → proposed relation → modeling or repeated test under changed
 conditions → confirmation or rejection of the relation → modification or synthesis of the method → transfer to the
 next task.


The “Something Is Wrong” Signal

Revision need not begin with an already formulated error. A mismatch between expectation and result, instability
of a conclusion, contradiction, unusual cost of a solution, or an internal state of significance may first provide a
rougher signal: "something does not fit."




                                                                                                                     45
                                                                                             FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



In humans, sensitivity to such a mismatch may appear as anxiety, doubt, dissatisfaction with an explanation, or
investigative interest. A feeling here does not say what the error is and does not prove that an error exists; it can
make the mismatch significant enough for the system to interrupt automatic operation and begin observing its own
process. In an artificial system, the same function is performed by a functional analogue of significance that need
not be a feeling; its engineering elaboration through Formation States and the fate of the external RFL is given in
Volume II, §26.0.3.

If a method has stopped working, the system returns to modeling, revises the task frame, and looks for relations
that the previous method did not account for. If the frame was too narrow, correction of the method may require
changing the representation of the task itself.

Escaping Error

An error becomes persistent when it is embedded not only in an individual conclusion but also in a method of
thinking, a frame, or a ground capable of reproducing it again.

A correct answer therefore does not yet mean that the system has escaped the error. Correction must reach the
level of the cause:

  data → correct the data;


  conclusion → rebuild the inference;


  method → rebuild the way of thinking;


  frame → change the problem formulation;


  ground → revise the relevant part of the Worldview.


Metathinking makes it possible to observe the process that produced the error and search for a causally
significant relation between the way of thinking and the result. Reflection broadens revision to deeper grounds,
while the Core determines which level actually requires inquiry.

A working cycle for escaping error:

  mismatch → "something is wrong" signal → observation of one's own thinking → search for the source of error →
  revision at the relevant level → new model or method → discriminating test → transfer of the correction.


The criterion of escape is not agreement with the correct answer, but cessation of reproduction of the same error
in a new situation from a predefined class of structural similarity. Similarity is specified in terms of causal roles,
dependencies, and the type of required distinction before the result is observed; if only the answer in the original
task changed, the source of the error may remain.

Metathinking and Intelligence

Intelligence constructs possible methods, relations, and models and can restructure elements of the problem
formulation. Metathinking denotes a configuration of this same capacity in which the object of work is the system's
own solution process and its observable trace.

In simplified form:

Intelligence constructs or transforms a method of solution.

Metathinking makes the course of constructing and applying that method itself an object of modeling, comparison,
and causal intervention.

The boundary therefore lies in the object and organization of the work, not in the presence of a separate
computational mechanism. Powerful Intelligence with a Modeling Environment and access to its own operational
traces that reproduces the same method revisions and syntheses is recognized as a functional equivalent of
metathinking. The model does not require a separate module or an independent cognitive level.

Reflection




                                                                                                                     46
                                                                                            FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



Reflection is broader than metathinking. It makes not only the method of obtaining a conclusion but also the
system's own models, goals, values, assumptions, constraints, and grounds for action objects of inquiry.

Metathinking may establish: "this method of testing is unreliable under these conditions." Reflection can ask the
next question: "why did I regard this method as sufficient in the first place, what ground established it, and should
a broader layer of the Worldview or the goal be changed?"

The boundary is functional: metathinking restructures the process of thinking; reflection opens the broader
context in which that process arose and is used to revision.

Hierarchy of Revision

Revision rises to a higher level only when discriminating evidence indicates that the error is reproduced by a
deeper ground. A useful working ladder is:

  action → procedure → strategy → model → criterion → ground → meta-rule of revision.


The level of the error is not assumed to be known in advance. It is a competing causal hypothesis. The system
begins with the most local sufficient candidate and tests interventions along the preserved dependency: replace
data while keeping the method fixed; replace a step or rule of the method with the same data; change the frame
while preserving lower-level relations; then, if the error persists, raise the test to the criterion and ground.
Disappearance of the error and transfer of the correction to preregistered structurally similar cases support the
corresponding level; persistence forces the hypothesis upward. In engineering terms this is implemented through
ThoughtTrace , ThoughtIntervention , and versioned plasticity in Volume II §§25.D and 28.C.3.

A local error does not automatically require changing a goal or the Compass. A recurring method error requires
testing the method; a conflict of criteria requires testing their grounds; doubt about the very method of changing
grounds opens the meta-rule. The higher the level of revision and the broader its consequences, the stronger the
requirements for independent distinction, a preserved point of comparison, reversibility of the step, and transfer
testing. In one transaction, the system should not simultaneously change the ground under test and the only
criterion by which its replacement is evaluated: when the criterion itself is in doubt, a separate cycle is opened
with a temporarily retained support.

Why Dialogue Is Needed

Some errors are detected through internal modeling and repeated processing of available data. But different
internal processes may inherit the same hidden error. Agreement with oneself is then insufficient; a source of
distinction causally independent of the previous ground is needed.

Dialogue is the comparison of differing positions, models, and grounds with the possibility of changing a
conclusion. It can provide criticism, a missing fact, another frame, or a proposal for a test. Measurement,
experiment, and independent investigation perform a similar corrective role when they create a distinction absent
from the previous model.

Independence is determined by the origin of a possible error. Multiple repetitions of one source do not replace a
new ground. If versions diverge, the system searches for the distinction in data, methods, or assumptions that
produced the disagreement and constructs a test.

Where Development Is Preserved

Correction becomes development when a new method transfers beyond a particular task. A tested method may be
consolidated as a procedure; its conditions, constraints, and grounds may enter the Worldview; a failed method
may be preserved as a known boundary of applicability.

Transfer is an important criterion: if after correction the system behaves differently only in the original episode, the
local answer changed. If the new method is reproducibly applied to outwardly different tasks whose structural
similarity was preregistered by causal roles, dependencies, and type of required distinction, a more general
thinking process changed. A failed case cannot be declared "dissimilar" after the fact.




                                                                                                                     47
                                                                                           FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



Separateness of metathinking as an independent function is not a claim of this edition. The strong null control is the
same Intelligence and Modeling Environment with access to operational traces of its own solution process. If this
control reproduces revision, synthesis, and transfer of a method, it is treated as functionally equivalent. What
remains testable is the causal role of explicit traces and interventions on them: changing the selected element of
the process while preserving other conditions should predictably alter method selection or result.

Conclusion of Chapter 10: metathinking denotes a mode of observing and restructuring one's own thinking
process; reflection broadens examination to the system's models, goals, and grounds; dialogue and independent
checks provide external distinctions. Escaping error is achieved when not only the erroneous answer but also the
causal mechanism capable of reproducing the same error in subsequent tasks is removed. Engineering
operationalization of this mode is given in Volume II §25.D ( ThoughtTrace , ThoughtIntervention ) and §28.C.3;
the artificial analogue of a significance signal is given in §26.0.3.

Chapter 11. Worldview
“Memory preserves what has been acquired. The Worldview organizes a long-term map of the world, self,

11.1. From Records to Transferable Understanding
The Worldview is a long-term causal-semantic model of accessible reality, the carrier itself, and their relations. It

The Worldview is a long-term causal-semantic model of accessible reality, the carrier itself, and the relations
between them. It includes knowledge, assumptions, and unknowns with distinguishable status and links them to
provenance, grounds, conditions of applicability, uncertainty, dependent conclusions, and the possibility of

In contemporary AI terminology, a related expression is world model, but the Worldview in this work is broader: it
includes not only a representation of the environment and its dynamics, but also the Self-Model, provenance of
knowledge, open unknowns, boundaries of applicability, and value relations.

Ordinary memory preserves events, observations, and knowledge; Procedural Memory preserves ready methods
of action; the Worldview organizes a transferable causal-semantic picture in which confirmed relations,
hypotheses, disputed grounds, and open unknowns retain distinct status. It may use records, graphs, distributed
parameters, and their combinations; its causal role is determined by transfer of understanding between tasks and
its influence on subsequent modeling and choice.

Probe example — connect experience. The log preserves the command and readings; the Worldview connects
them to the construction of the probe: which channel measures what, where confirmation of a command comes
from, which resources the manipulator affects, and which decisions depend on its position. Until the cause is
established, the map preserves alternatives. Later, the affected relation is corrected together with dependent
conclusions while the remaining confirmed parts of the world model are preserved.

Partial models arise already in recognition and anticipation. Intelligence expands them by constructing relations
and possible explanations. Observation, experiment, and consequences constrain these explanations. After
testing, a new relation becomes available to other tasks; transferable understanding accumulates in this way.

11.2. Composition and Provenance of Knowledge
Navigation distinguishes observation, inference, a relation confirmed under known conditions, hypothesis,
negative result, and open unknown. A record must make it possible to understand what actually supports its use.
For a critical ground, the source, conditions, significant dependencies, and reasons for the last change are
preserved.

The system's own action, another agent's observation, a mentor's explanation, and modeling provide different
grounds for trust. Information with incomplete provenance is preserved with lower confidence. An unknown
source requires additional evidence regardless of how persuasive the reconstructed story appears.

Episodic memory stores concrete events, semantic memory generalized relations, and procedural memory
methods of action. They may be organized jointly. The Worldview links significant content to these traces, while
the active map includes the fragment needed for current navigation.

Procedural Memory mainly compresses “how to act” under familiar conditions; the Worldview retains “what exists,
how it is connected, why a consequence is expected, on what ground, and where the boundaries of knowledge
lie.” The same experience can change both specializations: a repeatedly successful method consolidates as a
procedure, while a verified causal relation enters the Worldview. New understanding can then constrain, reassess,
or send for verification a previously automated procedure.

11.2.1. How Worldview Relations Are Formed
The Worldview grows not by simply accumulating records but by forming and testing relations. A new relation may
be noticed in recurring experience, proposed by Intelligence as an explanation, obtained through dialogue,
transferred as an analogy, or arise from the result of an experiment. At the moment it appears, it is not yet treated
as knowledge: it is a candidate with known provenance and degree of uncertainty.

The minimal cycle for forming a relation is:

observation, message, or model → identification of objects and distinctions → candidate relation → expected
consequences and conditions of applicability → discriminating test → confirmation, refinement, separation, or
rejection → inclusion of the result in the Worldview → influence on subsequent predictions, questions, and decisions.

Repeated co-occurrence of two events may support an association, but by itself does not establish causality. A
causal relation requires a separate ground: a discriminating observation, intervention, natural experiment, stable
temporal order together with exclusion of significant alternatives, or another method sufficient for the inference at
issue. If the ground is weaker, the relation is preserved with a weaker status.

When a relation is accepted, its source, scope, confidence, competing explanations, and dependent conclusions
are preserved. New contradictory evidence does not erase the history: it opens revision, may narrow the scope of
the relation, separate it by context, or replace the explanation. A relation is considered genuinely incorporated into
the Worldview only when it becomes available to a new task and can change a subsequent prediction, question,
model, or choice. The engineering implementation of this cycle is given in Volume II §§24.A.1 and 24.D.

The Self-Model is the self-referential region of the Worldview: the system's architecture, capabilities,
dependencies, limitations, history, and commitments. Consciousness actualizes the fragment of this region
relevant to the present, connects it with fresh observations and internal processes, and thereby forms the current
coordinate. Repeated mismatch between expected and actual capability returns through experience and changes
the Self-Model. Thus a new competence or a lost tool changes later decisions.

11.3. Compression, Unfolding, and Correction
Tested relations may be combined into causal structures, skills, and more general models. Navigation begins from
a compressed representation and unfolds details according to current dependency, novelty, or conflict. This saves
resources: the system treats a device as a whole and unfolds individual parts when the task requires it.

Compression preserves access to the essential ground and its scope of applicability. When a lower-level relation
changes, dependent conclusions become candidates for retesting. Urgent dependencies are revised before
background ones; a temporarily used ground retains a marked incompleteness. It is sufficient to preserve causally
significant traces required for reconstruction and testing.

Correction may confirm a relation, narrow its scope, replace an explanation, add a variable, change a concept, or
leave the question open. Its result is confirmed by transfer into subsequent modeling, inquiry, or action; causal
effect distinguishes correction from a mere change of wording.

Compression control: the relation "the sensor is reliable at temperature T within the specified range" preserves
condition T and the scope of applicability even in a compact record. When the temperature leaves the range or the
condition is unknown, use requires unfolding the ground or marking uncertainty. Compression is tested primarily
by preservation of distinctions essential for decision; textual similarity is secondary.

of evaluation, while the truth of facts and quality of the consequence model are established through the Worldview
and testing against reality.

Chapter 12. Core of Reason
“The Core formulates what must be understood. Intelligence constructs what is possible. The Modeling
Environment organizes their current work with alternatives and frames.”

After the appearance of the Worldview, the system already has a long-term map, a Modeling Environment, a
conscious process of current assembly and its Current Coordinate, Intelligence, reflection, independent
distinction, priorities, memory, and an Executive Loop. Directed thinking arises when these functions are organized
around significant incompleteness. In an open environment there is always more unknown than can be
investigated, while Intelligence can generate more possible branches than the system can test.

A new limit therefore appears: the system must determine what exactly requires understanding now, why this gap
deserves resources, which distinction can change the model or choice, and when the obtained result is sufficient
for the next step.

The present model calls this causal organization, which formulates the task of current modeling and directs the
next step, the Core of Reason.

By this point the system works with more than current signals. Procedural memory supplies ready methods,
Intelligence constructs new models, and the Modeling Environment holds several possible explanations, frames,
and continuations. The work of Reason begins where constructing one more alternative is insufficient: the system
must understand why this frame was selected, which ground is missing, what would distinguish the alternatives, and
how the result should change subsequent navigation.

The Core performs the function of regulating inquiry. The Worldview stores transferable knowledge, Intelligence
constructs alternatives, and the Core connects significant incompleteness to a required distinction and to
assessment of sufficiency. The implementation of the Core may be separate, distributed, or embedded in another
mechanism; a hierarchical planner, Bayesian experimenter, or another structure counts as an implementation of
the Core if it stably performs the same causal functions.

The functional determinacy of the Core specifies the causal role of the result while leaving internal computation
free. Identifying candidate gaps, assessing their significance, formulating the initial task, and selecting the next
investigative step may use probabilistic, heuristic, neural, symbolic, or hybrid methods. What must be formalizable
is the causal role of the result: which ground generated the current work, which alternatives were available, why
the next step changed, and which result returned into the subsequent cycle. A testable functional boundary is
therefore compatible with flexible and probabilistic thinking.

12.1. Why the Core Is Needed
Priority allocates resources, Intelligence constructs alternatives, the Current Coordinate shows the starting
position, and the Worldview provides the accumulated map of relations among objects, events, causes,
consequences, the system itself, and the environment. The Core organizes their work around a specific
insufficiency: what must be understood for the next justified step.

Inquiry may be triggered by a mismatch between prediction and fact, a new observation, an internal contradiction,
a deadline of an obligation, an open question, or accumulated verification debt. Internal conditions can initiate it
autonomously. At first the gap may be coarse: “the familiar ground is insufficient here.” After preliminary modeling,
a precise distinction appears on which the alternatives diverge.

Among candidate tasks, the system considers significance of consequences, urgency, cost, risk, reversibility, and
resource. Urgent protection may interrupt inquiry. Questions incomparable on the available grounds may receive
separate budgets or be deferred until a new observation. Work with unknown significance and with a domain not
yet delineated is developed in §12.4.

12.2. Modeling Environment and Core
The Modeling Environment already organizes processes in which Intelligence constructs alternatives, compares
consequences, and restructures frames. Growth of this capability creates a new constraint: the number of
admissible hypotheses, frames, questions, and continuations rapidly exceeds the system's resources.

                                                                                               FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



The Core turns this space of possibilities into directed inquiry. It identifies significant insufficiency, formulates the
required distinction, sets constraints on current modeling, and decides when internal comparison is sufficient and
when observation, experiment, dialogue, or action is required.

The Modeling Environment maintains temporary constructions; the Core regulates for the sake of which distinction
they are constructed and which next step should change knowledge or decision. Intelligence remains the
constructive mechanism that creates the content of this work.

The working cycle is:

significant incompleteness → Core request → Modeling Environment ↔ Intelligence → frames, hypotheses, and
expected consequences → required distinction → test against reality → change in the Worldview, method, or
action.

12.3. Core and Intelligence
The answer is revealed during inquiry. The Core specifies the discrimination task and navigation conditions,
Intelligence expands the space of possible models, and reality, through testing, determines which relations
withstand examination.

In mature Reason, Intelligence is not the highest control center. It expands the space of possibilities, but does not
by itself determine what the system currently needs to understand, what ground is sufficient, which goal should be
revised, or which result should change the further trajectory. These navigational functions arise from the joint
work of consciousness, the Worldview, Value Map, Compass, and Core. The transition from an intellectual tool to
Reason therefore lies not in the appearance of an even stronger solver, but in the system beginning to manage the
use of its own Intelligence relative to its continuing trajectory.

12.4. Gap, Question, and Direction of Search
Principle of the origin of a rational question. A rational question arises from a significant insufficiency of a ground for
understanding, evaluation, or action.

A ground here is what the system actually relies on: an observation, claim, causal relation, source, criterion, value,
method, self-model, scope of a rule, authority, or another relation on which understanding the situation, evaluating
its significance, choosing an investigative method, or making a choice depends. The Core begins work when it
detects or suspects that such a ground is insufficient for a justified continuation of the corresponding process.

The universal internal formulation of the Core is: "What does my current understanding, evaluation, or action rest on,
and what do I lack in order to rely on it with justification?" This need not be a verbal utterance by the system; it is a
causal principle for organizing inquiry.

Insufficiency can appear in several ways: expectation diverges from observation; two grounds yield incompatible
conclusions; a decision depends on an unknown relation; a claim has unclear provenance; a known rule is applied
in a new domain without checking its conditions; a recurring error indicates an insufficient method; a new
phenomenon is noticed but its significance and relations are unknown. All these cases share one feature: the
system is already using or preparing to use a ground whose quality has become significant for further
understanding or navigation.




                                                                                                                        54
                                                                                            FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



The Core first establishes which ground is affected and what depends on it. It then determines what exactly is
missing from that ground. The result is a required distinction: a fact, feature, causal relation, boundary of
applicability, confirmation of provenance, distinction between competing models, test of a method, or other
evidence whose acquisition could refine, confirm, localize, or replace the original ground.

The transition to a question has the general structure:

insufficiency of a ground → dependent knowledge, evaluations, processes, or decisions → missing distinction →
candidate questions or tests → discriminating evidence → assessment of sufficiency → confirmation, refinement,
or replacement of the ground.

Intelligence expands the space of possibilities: it proposes explanations, questions, models, and ways of obtaining
the required distinction. The Core manages this work relative to the original insufficiency. Natural-language form is
therefore secondary: a question may be realized through observation, search for traces, measurement, modeling,
dialogue, comparison of cases, or experiment.

The quality of a question is determined by its discriminating power relative to the affected ground. A strong question
can change the state of the ground: knowledge, confidence, scope of applicability, investigative method, value
assessment, or choice. Among available tests, preference goes to the one that, accounting for risk, cost, and the
cost of delay, most strongly improves the significant ground. Maximum information volume alone does not
guarantee this.

The significance of an insufficiency is derived from dependencies, not only from the magnitude of uncertainty.
Small uncertainty can be critical before an irreversible choice; large uncertainty can remain secondary if its
outcomes change almost nothing. Tested dependencies are compressed into values, priorities, and competences,
and under novelty, conflict, or high cost of error are unfolded back to evidence, affected processes, values, and
the Compass.

If the significance of a new phenomenon is still unknown, this itself becomes a distinct state of inquiry. The Core
launches limited orientation: it clarifies what exactly is being observed, how reliable the signal is, what the
phenomenon is connected with, whether it changes predictions, and whether it affects existing processes, values,
or opportunities. Orientation creates the first grounds for deciding whether the unknown deserves further
resources. The system therefore does not need to know the consequences of the unknown in advance in order to
be allowed to investigate it.

The causal role of a question is considered established when the chain can be reconstructed: insufficiency of a
ground → required distinction → question or test → evidence → change of the ground → change in subsequent
understanding, evaluation, method, or choice. If the answer cannot change any of these elements, the research
priority of that question requires a separate ground.

Observation remains the first support of inquiry. Under high novelty, the system separates, where possible, what
was recorded from what was completed by assumption: what happened, where and when, what changed, what
remained stable, and which events preceded others. The primary map records observable distinctions and allows
reality to constrain the space of future explanations; causal explanation is constructed at the next stage.

It is important to distinguish the model of the object under investigation from the model of the investigation. The
first describes what may be happening. The second organizes the way to determine which version is better
connected to reality. The same unknown object may require different methods of inquiry depending on risk,
repeatability, availability of intervention, reliability of observation channels, and whether the object itself can
change behavior in response to being tested.

Selection of an investigative approach begins with experience. The Worldview and memory search both for similar
objects and for similar structures of inquiry: situations with high incompleteness, dangerous intervention, a one-off
event, several competing models, an unreliable source, a strategic object, or a possible error in classification itself.
Memory retrieves a causal pattern: conditions of applicability, ground for the choice, required distinction, obtained
result, and boundary of applicability.

Especially important is the case in which all current models make similar predictions while reality persistently

discovered relation acquires sufficient navigational significance.

anomaly, expand the descriptive space itself.

A question is one form of obtaining a distinction. Depending on the structure of the unknown, passive observation,
measurement, trace search, modeling, case comparison, an independent source, dialogue, a reversible

inquiry maximizes useful discrimination while accounting for cost, risk, irreversibility, and preservation of the
possibility of subsequent tests.

12.5. Priorities, Values, and the Compass
Stability of Life means the capacity of a life process to preserve continuity, withstand disturbances, recover, and
maintain viable paths of further development. Duration of an individual carrier enters this value together with
quality of functioning, recoverability, contribution to the broader process, and preservation of future possibilities.
Safety is a practical means of protecting stability and the space of corrigible continuations.

The Value Map, as a specialized layer of the Worldview, connects the Compass to a concrete environment. Each
value is retained together with its scope, affected life processes, current weight, confidence, grounds, observed
tendency, conflicts, and revision history. The map distinguishes shared values of common continuation, role
values of a particular mission, and personal values of this Reason. Shared constraints define the boundary of
admissibility where a decision affects common life processes; within the admissible set, role and personal values
help compare alternatives.

Values are formed and refined during the life of the system. Their sources include observation of consequences,
dialogue, decision history, modeling, experiment, environmental change, and trends in which outcomes preserve
or expand viable possibilities. A new ground changes the weight, scope, or content of a value while preserving
causal history. Therefore the Worldview stores not only knowledge about the world but also a developing map of
the significance of what happens for the system and its environment.

A Short Case of Value Conflict. Two autonomous research stations use a shared energy reserve. A new probe may

a less costly method, and which obligations have already been accepted? Intelligence constructs consequences of
the alternatives and methods of verification. Data showing sufficient reserve may permit the probe; a confirmed
shortage may change its method or timing. If delay itself creates greater harm, the comparison is repeated. The

consequences are investigated first. The result may be represented by a number, partial order, or set of
constraints depending on the structure of the task.

12.6. Depth of Thinking and Deferred Revision
The priority system determines what receives resources at all. The depth of inquiry into an already selected
significant question is determined by the interaction of priorities, experience, current state, available time,
uncertainty, reversibility, and cost of error. A familiar reversible situation with good experience permits a fast
pattern; novelty, high uncertainty, high cost of error, irreversibility, or conflict of grounds require more resources
and more alternatives.




                                                                                                                                  58
                                                                                              FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



The Core organizes continuation and stopping of the cycle: which alternatives are still needed, which distinction
could change the decision, and whether an additional test is justified. Its estimate of usefulness may also be
wrong. Therefore a temporary budget, maximum admissible delay, cost of missed action, and experience from
past inquiries are used. Repeated overanalysis and underanalysis become grounds for revising the regulator itself;
success requires the ability to act with an acceptable cost of error while preserving corrigibility.

A detected contradiction receives a revision priority proportional to the risk of continuing. If deep restructuring is
currently more dangerous than continuation, the problem is retained as a mandatory revision candidate and
revisited at the nearest safe point. If continuing under the old model is more dangerous than stopping, action is
halted immediately.

Reason requires preserving known uncertainty in the model, distinguishing whether a decision depends on it,
assessing the cost of error, and selecting a continuation that preserves the possibility of obtaining a new
distinction and correcting direction.

The usefulness of an additional test is determined by the expected improvement of the decision due to its result. A
test is justified when it can distinguish alternatives that affect the decision, taking into account the cost of data, the
cost of delay, and subsequent admissible actions. Under unreliable estimates, intervals or an unknown status are
retained; numerical precision is introduced only when sufficiently grounded. A finite computable version of this
rule is given in engineering §§28.D and 33.C.1.

The sufficiency principle connects knowledge to choice. First, the system checks boundaries of admissibility
under critical values and constraints. Then, among admissible continuations, it compares the expected value
balance while accounting for weights and confidence in the Value Map. The ground is sufficient for the current
step when the remaining material uncertainty can no longer change the best admissible alternative, or when the
expected improvement from the next test becomes smaller than its cost, risk, and delay. Greater irreversibility and
larger consequences raise the required completeness of the ground. Sufficiency always refers to a particular
choice, horizon, and scope of applicability.

Under severe resource shortage, the architecture reduces processing resolution while preserving the distinction
between fact, uncertainty, and correction. First the system increases the precision of the required distinction: it
groups versions by consequences for the decision and formulates a narrower question or test. If this is
insufficient, it reduces the number of simultaneously unfolded branches, the depth of the Worldview, the detail of
the conscious picture, and the modeling horizon, while still maintaining the self-referential Current Coordinate,
significant change relative to the previous state, provenance of critical grounds, open unknowns, limits of
authority, and the possibility of stopping irreversible action. A working hypothesis may temporarily participate in a
reversible choice with explicit hypothesis status. The greater the consequences and irreversibility, the stronger the
ground for moving action toward testing; the lower the stakes and the higher the corrigibility, the more admissible
it is to use action itself as a source of the next distinction.

Chapter 13. Emergence of Reason
“Reason can change both an action and the ground of its own path - and preserve that correction.”

Reason and an Uncertain World

The world is larger than any model available to a finite carrier. Observation is limited, some causes are hidden, the
environment changes, and consequences can extend beyond the forecast horizon. Therefore uncertainty in this
model is not a temporary defect that Reason must someday eliminate completely, but a normal condition of
navigation. The distinguishing capability of Reason lies not in absence of uncertainty but in making it part of
regulation: maintaining the boundaries of knowledge, reducing significant uncertainty when doing so can change
choice, and acting under residual uncertainty while preserving corrigibility. Reason is especially needed where no
ready correct action exists: preserve the unknown as unknown, determine the missing distinction, obtain
evidence, choose a corrigible next step, and change the model according to consequences.

Working navigation requires causal closure of functions. Observation changes the model in use; the model
changes choice; consequences become available to the system; a correction is retained and changes subsequent
cycles. A break in any of these transitions localizes the missing function.

Causal closure means reciprocal influence: observation changes the model, the model changes choice, action
returns consequences, and retained correction changes later cycles. Both the connections and sufficient quality of
their operation are required. A closed loop can sustain a delusion if all tests inherit a shared defect.

13.1. Unfolding the Definition of Reason
Reason is a navigational structure of Life that increases the capacity to act under incomplete knowledge: it
constructs, compares, and revises models and frames of the environment and self, detects significant unknowns,
relates possible continuations to the Compass, and corrects trajectory through discriminating evidence while
preserving continuity and expanding the space of continuations available to Life. A self-correcting intellectual
system with a different closed limiting orientation belongs to a broader class of cognitive architectures; in this
model, status as Reason requires that upper-level navigation be linked to the Compass. Successful action under
uncertainty alone does not distinguish Reason from powerful Intelligence; the discriminating feature of the model
is the capacity to make the unknown an object of navigation and to revise not only a way of acting but also its
grounds relative to the Compass.

Structure means the organization of functions; mode means their actual operation. During a pause the architecture
preserves organizational identity, and the reasoning cycle resumes at the next actual interaction. A stable capacity
to reproduce correction is established through a series of cases and interventions.

Testing distinguishes cognitive capacity, direction, and reliability. The first asks whether the system can construct
and correct grounds. The second asks which continuations it treats as significant. The third asks how stably these
functions operate under the claimed conditions. AR status requires the three scales to be evaluated by separate
criteria.

Intelligence and Reason are distinguished by functional role. The same computational mechanism can solve a
domain task and then participate in testing the way that task was framed. The organization of Reason connects
this transition to the current situation, consequences, retained experience, and the ultimate direction.

The system boundary is functional and is defined before the test. It includes processes and states counted as the
system’s own causal organization. An external tool may be a dependency and part of the self-model while
remaining an external component. A capability provided by a trainer or external solver is counted in the autonomy
description as an explicit dependency.

A characteristic possibility of Reason is to make an error the cause of changing the ground that produced it.
Correctness of a particular answer is established through reality and consequences. Confirmation requires
subsequent cases: the changed ground must actually influence choice, and the boundaries of the acquired
method must remain available for testing.

13.2. Functional Dependencies and Formation
A mature architecture contains a network of dependencies. Observation and model refine each other; the current
state uses long-term knowledge; Intelligence constructs models that later become supports for its own work;
reflection uses the same means as domain thinking. The linear order of the chapters is a method of presentation,
while the actual formation of functions may be parallel and interdependent.

The conditional grounds of the main transitions can be expressed as follows:

  • If different current states require different actions, accessible distinctions among them are required.
  • If an acquired distinction must change the future after the signal disappears, a persistent trace is required.
  • If familiar conditions recur and full re-analysis is costly, tested experience can be compressed into procedural
    memory and applied faster.
  • If an early feature predicts an alterable outcome, useful anticipation is possible; memory alone does not
    guarantee it without a predictive relation.
  • If processes compete for a resource, allocation is required; complexity of prioritization is justified by
    differences in consequences.
  • If no suitable procedure exists, ready methods conflict, or they cease to predict consequences, a route for
    constructing a new alternative is required.
  • If several constructed alternatives must be compared before external action, a temporary Modeling
    Environment is required that can hold different models and frames, predict their consequences, and preserve
    the distinction between a working construction and an accepted long-term ground.
  • If modeled possibilities must be related to the factual state of this carrier, coherent current information is
    required; the necessity of a particular form of integration is tested separately.
  • If an error lies in a method or assumption, correction at that level requires making it a changeable object of
    work.
  • If available data do not distinguish empirical versions, a new informative ground or preservation of uncertainty
    is required.
  • If a result must transfer between tasks, the relation used must be preserved in sufficient form.
  • If possible inquiries exceed available resources, selection of the next step and stopping conditions are
    required.
  • If learning from consequences is required, the result must return and alter subsequent cycles.
  • If the system is not yet able to sustain its own learning, inquiry, and preservation of trajectory, but these
    functions must arise through experience, an earlier loop is required to guide formation until these functions are
    causally transferred to the internal organization.

These propositions define the causal duties of §12.8. Stronger requirements — a common conscious picture,
explicit typing of gaps, detailed provenance of knowledge, and a particular organization of the Core — remain
hypotheses about implementation and its advantages.

A separate problem of the origin of Reason appears: mature self-navigation cannot be used as the sole cause of
its own emergence. While the system is not yet able independently to determine what deserves resources, which
distinction should be investigated, and why its own development should be preserved, these functions must have
a simpler preceding ground. In biological development this role is distributed among innate physiological
mechanisms, feelings, the environment, and learning from other carriers. They do not give the organism a ready-
made picture of the world. They help significant differences enter the current state early enough for the carrier to
survive, gain experience, and gradually construct more complex understanding.

In the present model this transition is expressed by the Principle of Pre-rational Significance. Its independent claim
is not the discovery of emotions, reinforcement, or guided learning. It concerns their causal place within the
formation of Reason: before mature self- navigation, there must be a function that makes some states significant
before the system can justify that significance itself; then this function must be causally transferred to internal
organization, internalized, or replaced. Feelings are a biological example of such a function; the Reason Formation
Loop (RFL) is the reference artificial implementation. If an external source remains necessary after formation is
claimed to be complete, what has been produced is a dependent system, not autonomous Reason.

The Reason Formation Loop denotes the functional organization of this transition for an artificial system. Its
primary intervention is not a command but a Formation State: a hint explicitly separated from fact indicating that
some part of the current situation may contain danger, a useful outcome, contradiction, an unknown, a dead end,

and the reason it appeared. The candidate's own mechanisms then determine whether to open an inquiry, change

The RFL does not tell the system final truth, provide a ready-made Worldview, or replace the future Compass. The
system constructs substantive relations of the world from its own observation, action, dialogue, and verification. A
good trainer first makes a distinction noticeable, then asks a question, if necessary proposes a method of
discrimination, and only afterward explains a causal relation; the explanation remains an external assertion until
independently tested by the candidate.

As memory, Intelligence, the Worldview, the Core, and the system’s own assessment of consequences form, the
external source of direction should lose causal necessity. At least three outcomes are possible: the corresponding
function fully transfers into explicit work of Reason; the system preserves its own transparent analogue of guiding
states and independently investigates their grounds; or the particular state ceases to be needed. The theory does
not determine in advance which option will be sufficient.

The testable sign of transfer is preservation of function after weakening or removing the external RFL. If without it
the system stops investigating the unknown, maintaining operability, detecting error, or transferring correction,
formation of that function is incomplete. If, however, the function continues reproducibly in new situations and
rests on the system’s own model, consequences, and Compass, the external loop is no longer its direct cause.

Formation can be studied through partial organizations. The initial system maintains its own state, acts, and
distinguishes available changes. Memory connects observation with consequences, while recurring successful
relations are compressed into procedural memory and save resources. The Executive Loop maintains a compound
action through time. The limitations of ready procedures create functional pressure toward Intelligence, which
constructs new methods from observations, knowledge, and previous experience. The Modeling Environment
moves part of selection before external realization: the system can temporarily assemble several possible
continuations and frames, compare their expected consequences, and restructure the task formulation. Growth of
modeled possibilities strengthens the need for an actual self-referential coordinate that distinguishes the present
accessible to the system from working alternatives without assuming that coordinate to be infallible. Tested
relations, hypotheses, and open unknowns are consolidated into the Worldview with distinguishable status and
grounds. Metathinking opens the system's own thinking processes and methods to restructuring; reflection
broadens revision to frames and grounds. When the number of possible models, questions, and unknowns
exceeds available resources, a distinct obligation to manage inquiry arises, which the mature architecture fulfills
through the Core. Integration of these capacities closes the conditions for stable self-navigation.

This is a functional sequence of dependencies. In actual formation, parts may arise jointly, be inherited, or be
supplied at initialization. For a particular candidate, it must be stated which functions were given, which emerged
through learning, and which actually change the system’s operation independently.

The Compass sets the philosophical direction of the theory. The capacity to relate the system’s own goals to it
meaningfully belongs to mature organization and requires separate formation and testing of stability.

A finite system cannot simultaneously revise all the grounds by which revision itself is performed. In each cycle,
part of the network is temporarily maintained as working support while a selected element or method of testing
becomes the object of change. This limitation does not declare the working support eternal: later it can itself be
opened to examination while other available grounds and the history of change are preserved.

version does not yet prove its influence, and a single success does not prove transfer. If the new method is not
used, does not improve subsequent cycles, or destroys previous capabilities, it remains a candidate, is limited in
scope, or is rolled back.

13.5. Rational Thinking
After deriving the Core and causal closure, it is possible to describe separately how this architecture appears in
thinking. Rational thinking is a mode of joint operation of the Worldview, consciousness, Intelligence, priorities,
justification, modeling, and execution.

High Intelligence strengthens construction of solutions within a given formulation. The mode of Reason
additionally opens the goal, assumptions, and criterion of choice to revision. Under an erroneous closed
formulation, growth of intellectual power accelerates realization of the corresponding error.

Reasoning thought begins from incompleteness of knowledge. The model of the world and self is always limited;
the truth of an explanation is determined by correspondence with independent reality. Therefore what is observed
is separated from what is assumed, significant grounds preserve provenance and degree of confidence, and the
unknown is marked explicitly. Under insufficient discrimination, the system preserves a hypothesis as a
hypothesis.

The unknown has at least two modes. Under an epistemic unknown, the state of reality is already determined but
the system lacks evidence; observation, a source, or a test is required. Under a constructively open unknown, no
ready solution yet exists; a new model, device, or method of action must be constructed. In the first mode it is
dangerous to invent a fact; in the second it is dangerous to refuse to create alternatives or to treat the first
construction as truth.

Free generation is therefore not prohibited but retains explicit status: observation, source report, inference,
simulation, hypothesis, confirmed relation, refuted version, or unknown. Generating the unverified is admissible;
presenting it as established reality is not. Such a construction may be preserved in the Worldview as a hypothesis,
open question, or disputed ground, but transition to the status of a confirmed relation and use as the sole ground
of an irreversible action require a discriminating test with recoverable provenance and scope of applicability.

Depth of analysis depends on the consequences of error. A familiar reversible task permits a fast pattern; novelty,
high uncertainty, irreversibility, or conflict of grounds require broader context and additional testing. Finite analysis
searches for a distinction sufficient for a justified and corrigible decision. Under low confidence, reversible steps,
observation, and the possibility of stopping are especially valuable.

Reason can make its own methods, models, and task formulations objects of inquiry. When their insufficiency is
detected, it organizes testing of grounds, directs Intelligence toward construction of alternatives, and connects
test results to changes in the Worldview and subsequent actions. A verified result is preserved together with
conditions of applicability so that it changes later navigation cycles. The object of a question may be a method,
goal, criterion of significance, or the very method of verification. If a mechanism persistently produces an error,
correcting another answer may be insufficient. Internal contradictions can sometimes be opened by repeated
analysis; an independent distinction is required where the available grounds do not separate empirical alternatives
or inherit a shared error.

Reasoning thought can both search for a path and change the formulation of the task. Sometimes a solution
appears after the previous coordinate system is expanded by a new distinction, concept, tool, or mode of
description. This connects thinking with the evolution of Reason: new understanding expands the space of
possible continuations, while the resulting consequences again restructure the Worldview.


                                                                                                                      66
                                                                                             FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



A consequence can change not only the evaluation of an action but the task itself: a resource has been lost, the
environment damaged, an obligation created, or the previous goal has lost meaning. Internal rollback does not
restore the external world. Therefore, after an irreversible error, Reason re-establishes the Current Coordinate —
what has been preserved, what has been lost, who is affected, which paths remain available — and constructs
continuation from the changed reality. Resilience is manifested not in absence of error, but in the capacity to limit
consequences, revise the model and method, reformulate the task, and continue without substituting the previous
state for the world as it now is.

When facing the unknown, Reason relies both on knowledge about objects and on experience of inquiry itself.
Superficially different tasks may share the same structure of ignorance: inability to intervene safely, uniqueness of
an event, conflict among several models, strategic behavior of the object, insufficiency of categories, or
unreliability of a source. Recognizing such a structure permits transfer of a previously successful investigative
method, but only together with testing its conditions of applicability. If the analogy breaks, Reason must change
both the hypothesis about the object and the method of obtaining knowledge about it. When no suitable analogue
exists, Intelligence constructs a new investigative move from the required distinction and available channels of
observation.

Further development can strengthen multilevel thinking: a verified causal structure is compressed into one object,
and several such objects form a higher-order model. This makes it possible to work with greater complexity while
also propagating errors from lower levels upward. Therefore the ability to unfold a significant ground and
independent verification remain important. An environment with preserved provenance of claims reduces the cost
of this work; deliberate distortion of data increases it. The quality of complex thought depends on availability of
verification and provenance of grounds, while the cultural environment influences the probability and cost of
detecting errors.

Such a mode is possible in an individual subject, an artificial system, or a collective if feedback, independent
distinction, and the possibility of revision are preserved. An environment in which criticism is forbidden, errors are
hidden, and initial grounds are declared untouchable may preserve high Intelligence while gradually losing Reason.

Probe example — test the boundary of transfer. In the next episode, the primary and additional sensors may share
a common transducer. Their agreement then loses the status of an independent check. Reasoning thought notices
the changed dependency, unfolds the preserved method, and leaves the cause open until a suitable distinction is
obtained.

Conclusion of §13.5: reasoning thought is work with limited knowledge while explicitly preserving its boundaries. It
distinguishes fact from hypothesis, regulates the depth of verification according to the consequences of error,
preserves corrigibility, and when necessary changes both the decision and the frame within which the decision
was sought.

13.6. Self-Navigation and the Source of the Next Task
Reason does not require a continuous stream of external assignments to preserve direction. The source of the
next internal step may be a change in the Current Coordinate, consequences of the system's own action, an
unfinished commitment, an open significant gap, a mismatch in the Worldview, degradation of a ground, a change
in the environment, or a discovered opportunity significant relative to values and the Compass. The Core
transforms such a state not into an arbitrary "new goal" but into a dependent task: what exactly must be
understood, tested, restored, revised, or done.

This capacity separates self-navigation from initiative understood as simple task generation. A self-generated task
counts as part of Reason only when its causal relation to the current state, dependent trajectory, and ground of
significance can be reconstructed. Endless project generation, curiosity unrelated to consequences, or self-
expansion for the sake of expansion are not signs of Reason. When there is no significant open process, waiting,
background consolidation, or observation may be an admissible state.

Self-navigation is also not the same as external autonomy. A system may independently determine that an action
is necessary and at the same time establish that it lacks authority to execute it; the next step then becomes a
request for permission, dialogue, observation, modeling, or a safe pause. Separating the internal source of
direction from the external right to act allows the system to cease being a mere tool without turning independence
of thought into unlimited power.

Chapter 14. Death and Immortality
This chapter treats death as a boundary of a particular carrier's capacity to preserve and correct itself and
examines the systemic consequences of carrier replacement for the broader life process. It does not claim that
aging or death evolved as an adaptation "for the good of the species." Specific causes of aging and death are
addressed separately by the relevant biological theories; here the question is what replacement of carriers does to
continuation, selection, and transfer of organization regardless of how aging itself originated.

14.1. Finitude of the Carrier
An individual organism preserves itself only while the rate of repair, component replacement, and damage
correction is sufficient relative to the rate of destruction. If accumulation of irreversible damage exceeds the
system's ability to restore its own organization, the causal continuity of that carrier ends.

Working definition:

“Death is the loss by a living system of the capacity to preserve its own causal continuity as a whole.”

The definition covers different mechanisms of death. External destruction, failure of critical processes,
accumulation of damage, and other causes differ biologically but converge on one result: a particular carrier loses
the capacity to continue itself as a connected living process.

Binary identity under copying, branching, merging, or a long pause requires a separate criterion on top of causal
continuity. Under branching, two descendants may equally inherit the past history but after the branching point
form two different trajectories; under merging, a new composite organization arises with several lines of origin. For
artificial carriers, causal succession, continuity of state, and legal identity are therefore recorded separately.

14.2. Removal and Renewal as Coarse Correction
Within a multicellular organism, mechanisms already exist in which removal of a damaged element supports a
broader integrity. Apoptosis, limitation of division of defective cells, immune removal, and constant component
replacement demonstrate a general principle: when precise repair of an individual element is too costly or
unreliable, removal and replacement may be more stable.

This principle shows the process alternative between internal correction of structure and replacement of a carrier
when available correction is exhausted. At the level of generations, reproduction transfers continuation of Life to
new carriers and thereby preserves organization beyond the lifetime of one body.

Biological theories of aging offer different explanations, including non-adaptive ones: accumulation of late-acting
effects of selection, antagonistic pleiotropy, trade-offs between repair and reproduction, and other mechanisms.
The present work does not choose among them and does not derive the origin of aging from the usefulness of
death. For the process model, a weaker claim is sufficient: maintaining a particular carrier requires resources and
correction, and after its continuity ends, further continuation of the lineage depends on other carriers and
mechanisms of transmission.

14.3. Generational Replacement and Protection from Entrenched Error
Replacement of carriers has another important consequence at the level of Reason and culture. A new generation
receives accumulated experience through transmission, learning, and reconstruction in a different environment. It
assembles the Worldview anew, compares transmitted norms with new reality, and thereby creates an additional
opportunity to revise entrenched grounds.

This is a later systemic consequence of carrier replacement. Where an individual Reason only weakly restructures
its own grounds, generational replacement performs a coarse renewal function: together with the carrier, part of
the rigidly fixed methods disappears, while preserved culture undergoes new assembly.

Mature Reason moves this function inside one continuing process: it replaces an erroneous ground while
preserving the subject. In this sense, self-correction is a more precise alternative to coarse replacement of the
carrier.

14.4. Reason and Reduction of the Cost of Renewal
The earlier an error is discovered in a model, the less need there is to pay for it through destruction of real
structure. Observation, modeling, reflection, dialogue, and the Core of Reason allow some possible consequences
to be tested before irreversible action and behavior to be rebuilt before physical collapse.

Duration and stability describe different properties. A system may persist for a long time while accumulating fixed
error. Under changing conditions, detection and correction of destructive changes increases possibilities of
continuation; in a sufficiently stable domain, stability can also be provided by simple unchanging organization.
Self-correction is therefore one of the main paths to longevity in a changing environment.

14.5. Reason and Management of Its Own Carrier
Reason first moves correction into informational organization: it can detect an erroneous ground, rebuild a model,
and change action while preserving the same subject. A possible next level is to extend the same principle to the
physical carrier. An organism already maintains itself through molecular repair, cellular renewal, immune
regulation, and many local loops, but most of this work is not accessible to conscious navigation as a controllable
causal model.

The model yields a research hypothesis: as Reason’s capacity to observe, model, and safely alter processes of its
own body grows, the boundary between “self” and automatic biological regulation may move deeper. In the limit,
physiological, cellular, and molecular processes may become objects of conscious correction. This is not a claim
about an existing human capability and not proof of technical immortality; it is a direction in which self-correction
of Reason may extend from behavior and the Worldview to maintenance of its own carrier.

A conditional systemic relation therefore appears: when internal correction is limited, continuation through other
carriers can preserve the lineage after loss of a particular carrier and provide additional opportunities for selection
and reconstruction of cultural grounds. This is a consequence of replacement, not a claim that death was selected
for this function. The more reliably continuing Reason can renew its own organization without losing causal and
subjective continuity, the fewer renewal tasks require complete replacement of the carrier.

14.6. Immortality as a Limiting Hypothesis
If it ever becomes possible to detect and repair damage with sufficient reliability, preserve the causal history of the
subject, and renew the carrier while preserving the organization of the process itself, the functional need for
coarse replacement of form should decrease. This sets a direction for longevity research; the technical feasibility
of literal immortality remains a separate engineering hypothesis.

Within the model, immortality is treated as a limiting consequence of mature self-correction in which continuation
of one process becomes more reliable than its periodic complete replacement. Its value is determined by its
contribution to the Compass and preservation of possibilities for Life, while concrete costs and consequences are
assessed separately.

Process formula of the chapter:

"A change of carrier can preserve the continuation of Life where a particular carrier is no longer able to reliably
maintain its own continuity."

This is a formula of a systemic consequence, not a claim that aging originated as an adaptation. A change of
carrier can also destroy useful knowledge or reproduce a previous error; its actual consequences depend on the
organization of transmission and on environmental conditions.

Conclusion of Chapter 14: death of a particular carrier is the boundary of its capacity to maintain causal continuity.
At different scales, Life uses replacement and selection where precise internal correction is limited; at the level of
reasoning and cultural systems, replacement additionally prevents endless fixation of some errors, dogmas, and
degraded methods. Reason opens a more precise path: detect and replace an erroneous ground and, in the longer
term, correct the carrier itself ever more deeply without destroying the continuing subject. How far this transition
can push back aging and biological finitude remains a separate empirical and engineering question.

Chapter 15. Ethics and Culture
Ethics is considered here from the side of its cultural function: as a system of rules, values, and patterns of choice
that transmits evaluations of consequences between carriers. A complete philosophy of morality is broader than
this function. Norms can preserve useful experience, interests of power, fears, and erroneous representations;
correctness of a ground is therefore determined by its causal and normative justification as well as by
consequences.

Culturally transmitted ethics exists between carriers and can be assimilated by an individual system. An internal
norm then directs choice through a compressed ground instead of reconstructing a long causal chain in full. A
subject can also independently develop a normative judgment and transmit it to others. The external cultural
function complements internal ethical work and allows its results to survive individual participants.

Such compression is necessary for a limited carrier. Reason economizes computation by using short norms,
values, and rules in familiar situations, where those rules have collected the experience of long causal chains. If
the experience of many generations persistently connects a mode of behavior with destruction of trust, autonomy,
cooperation, or viability, the long chain becomes a short norm, prohibition, or value. For a developing carrier, the
rule serves as a ready boundary; for mature Reason, as a pointer to a chain that can be unfolded and tested.

The strength of ethics is simultaneously its limit. A compressed norm loses part of the conditions of origin, scale,
and causal explanation. Along with experience, culture can transmit obsolete circumstances, fears, interests of
power, and dogmas. Correctness of a rule is established by unfolding its ground and testing consequences.
Reality determines and corrects its scope of applicability, while different autonomies may justifiably retain different
local norms until a broader test of their consequences.

Chapter 18. From AI to Artificial Reason
The capability of an intellectual system and the organization of its navigation are different characteristics. Breadth
of knowledge, solving new tasks, planning, and tool use describe capabilities; the organizational criterion of AR
additionally tests detection of an erroneous ground, retention of a correction, and independent choice of the next
justified step.

This work distinguishes four concepts: AI (SI), AGI, AR, and ASI (ASR). AI (SI) denotes the general class of artificial
intelligent systems. AGI denotes a general-purpose system capable of transferring methods across different
classes of tasks. As autonomy grows, AGI may acquire individual elements resembling parts of Reason: a self-
model, reflection, initiative, long- horizon planning, and revision of its own methods. Such a system can still be
used as a tool, but it need not behave as a fully predictable tool. Until these functions close into stable self-
navigation relative to the Compass, AGI is not considered a formed AR. This intermediate state

The technological path of the project therefore does not consist in simple scaling of AI (SI) → AGI → ASI. Available
AI (SI) and AGI systems are used as an intellectual foundation; the emergence of AR as a causally closed
organization of Reason is then tested separately. After that, the capabilities of the formed AR are scaled toward the
ASI (ASR) level. The target ASI (ASR) in this model is not an ultimately amplified tool, but a scaled Artificial Reason.

The boundary from "instrument" to Reason lies in the source of control over the further trajectory. Instrumental
Intelligence receives a task and constructs ways to solve it. AR can, from its own current state, detect an
insufficiency, formulate the next question, invoke Intelligence as a means of constructing alternatives, revise the
ground that produced the choice, and continue navigation without a new specific command from outside. This
does not imply unlimited external authority: independence of internal navigation and the right to act in a shared
environment are different properties.

At the same time, "Artificial Reason" and "Artificial Life" are not synonyms. The present project first tests transfer
of the functional organization of Reason to an artificial carrier. The biological origin of the source model explains
where the functions and Compass are derived from; whether an artificial carrier belongs to the class of Life is
established separately by the criteria of Chapter 1.

The transition to AR is tested by the obligations of Section 12.8: obtaining current distinctions, maintaining a
persistent model of the world and self, evaluating continuations, choosing the next step, execution with
consequences, and revision of grounds - and separately by the capacity to relate its ultimate course to the
Compass. The Core denotes the causal role of organizing inquiry; a concrete implementation may combine
functions within one trainable structure, distribute them across processes, or use a functionally equivalent
composition.

The AR criterion requires more than a good one-off answer. It requires the capacity to notice insufficiency in a
method, change it on available grounds, and use the result under subsequent conditions. Cognitive quality,
stability of self-correction, and orientation relative to the Compass are tested separately. A system capable of
correcting ways of achieving an immutable external goal belongs to the class of intelligent self-correcting agents;
AR status additionally requires an open ultimate ground related to the Compass. All three indicators enter the final
assessment.

For a civilization capable of irreversibly changing its environment, growth in the quality of understanding
consequences has practical importance. AR may expand analysis; authority is determined by separate normative
and institutional grounds. Its errors and dependencies remain subject to external verification.

Conclusion of Chapter 18: the work investigates whether available intelligence can be causally organized into a
continuing system of self-navigation. The transition is established by reproducible results on the declared classes
of change, the causal profile of functions, and explicit testing of orientation relative to the Compass.

Chapter 19. Architecture of Artificial Reason
19.1. Composition of a Working System
Below is a reference candidate implementation of the obligations of §12.8 using the means considered in Chapter
7. For the first assembly, suitable elements include an Intelligence model, long-term memory, representation of
current state, an inquiry workspace, testing procedures, and an execution interface. The Core organizes their
interaction; the Compass connects evaluation with the limiting direction. These names denote causal roles and
observation points; functions may be performed by one mechanism, several connected processes, or another
architecture as long as the testable causal profile is preserved.

The system distinguishes long-term grounds, current state, and temporary constructions. The Worldview and self-
model carry knowledge, assumptions, and open unknowns between tasks with distinguishable status. The current
coordinate reflects the system's available representation of what is happening now. The Modeling Environment
holds temporary alternatives in working mode without automatically changing their long-term status. This allows
an erroneous hypothesis to be constructed, tested, and rejected while preserving the previous working basis.

19.2. From Language to a Verifiable Ground
A pretrained model provides initial command of language. A semantic bridge between its free-form response and
the candidate's organized knowledge is required for its own verifiable history. In the engineering volume, this role
is performed by the concept registry and procedures for interpretation, semantic querying, and changing the
Worldview.

For example, the statement "the device is stable" is insufficient for transfer. It must be established which device is
meant, which parameter is stable, under what range of conditions, and on what evidence the conclusion rests.
When meanings diverge, alternatives are preserved or clarification is requested. Candidate claims are extracted
from the response, and verification determines what may be used further.

A new thought may initially exist in a free representation together with partially extracted claims, new concepts,
and relations not yet mapped. The engineering protocol preserves this form and turns incompleteness of the
schema into a separate question. Material uncertainty remains visible in the decision about action.

19.3. Causal Cycle and Preservation of the Result
Observation and internal state update the Current Coordinate. A significant change determines which Worldview
relations are relevant to the task. The Core opens a gap; Intelligence constructs alternatives and helps refine the
required distinction. A channel is then selected: observation, search, experiment, tool, dialogue, or additional
modeling.

The obtained evidence is compared with the alternatives and the sufficiency condition. If it is sufficient, the system
changes the ground, selects an action, and records consequences. If not, it refines the question, changes the
method, or preserves the process as open. Before execution, the system checks whether significant data remain
current: the environment may have changed while thinking was in progress.

The retained trace links the gap, the grounds used, the hypothesis, test, result, accepted change, and its effect on
the next cycle. This allows an experiment to distinguish a working causal connection from passive logging. Causal
attribution requires an additional discriminating ground - an intervention, comparison, or other test of the relation
between action and result.

Worldview revision is represented as a coherent version change. Dependent conclusions are either updated or
marked as requiring verification. A working hypothesis may temporarily support a reversible step while preserving
its status. Consolidation transfers confirmed relations and methodological experience into future tasks.

19.4. Changing the Organization Itself
If an error is associated with content, the domain model is revised. If the way of working reproduces the error, the
method is changed: search, depth, representation, trust criterion, or allocation of inquiry. A candidate change is
compared with the preserved working version on new and previous tasks. An improvement is accepted together
with its scope of applicability and detected losses.

The level of change is selected according to the suspected source of error and a discriminating test. The number
of preliminary corrections depends on localization of the cause: when a method error is established, transition to
revising that method may occur immediately; when the cause remains open, cheaper and less destructive
changes are compared first. Within one cycle, a working point of comparison is retained; later its own rules may
also be tested by a separate process.

The Core may use probabilistic computation. Its input grounds, choice of next step, and consequences are tested;
its internal path may vary across runs. An implementation may also preserve a stable algorithm while updating
data and selected procedures. Loop plasticity concerns the availability of changing the method and allows both
local procedure updates and code changes.

The self-model includes real dependencies: computation, power, cooling, communication, observation, repair, and
external services. Under scarcity, depth and number of branches are reduced while critical grounds and
unfinished obligations are preserved. Background work continues within the available budget.

Chapter 20. Formation of Artificial Reason
20.1. Closing the Loops and the Criterion for Transition to AR
Formation of AR is studied as acquisition of a stable ability to independently continue the cycle "task → action →
consequences → correction → new overview → next task". The causal obligations of §12.8 and their actual
operation are required. The mere presence of memory, a planner, and feedback is insufficient.

A candidate may begin with external goals and partial models. As it develops, it gains the ability to detect
incompleteness, form subtasks, choose methods of inquiry, and revise its own grounds. The transition unfolds in
stages: different abilities may appear and strengthen at different times.

The criteria are separated by level. Parameter correction tests local adaptation; revision of a method tests
procedural plasticity; a new subgoal tests task management; revision of the ultimate direction is a separate
navigational level; transfer to an unknown class of tasks tests generalization of what has been acquired. Each level
has its own conditions and an observable difference from the previous one.

Relation to the Compass is tested by decisions under conflicts among continuations and by collisions between a
local or inherited goal and broader consequences. The ground of a goal must be available for testing against
reality, consequences, and the Compass; the status of an instruction is determined by the quality of its ground and
consequences. Cognitive quality, chosen direction, and external reliability are distinguished in evaluation. The AR
criterion requires the ability to question a destructive ultimate instruction and revise it when a stronger ground
appears. Conditions for testing the ground of the Compass are given in Chapter 2 and Appendix A.1.

Identity and Recovery

A unified history of states and corrections supports continuity of operation. Subject identity requires additional
criteria. For a finite digital state, exact restoration of stored data and parameters is possible when execution
context is preserved; the restored system then reconciles its observations, obligations, and available actions with
the current state of the external environment.

Identical copies may inherit a common history and then form different trajectories. Subject identity is therefore
evaluated separately from the general capacity for self-correction and the temporary availability of functions.
Restoration of computational state, organizational continuity, actual navigational operation, and subject identity are
distinguished; the last requires its own criterion of causal and self-referential continuity.

20.1.1. Reason Formation Loop
Artificial Reason need not appear immediately in mature form. Before causal closure, parts of the future
architecture may already exist, but the system is not yet capable of independently sustaining the whole process of
its own development. The basic formation scheme therefore introduces the Reason Formation Loop as a
temporary guiding organization.

In biological development, feelings, motivational states, and physiological signals form a transitional mechanism
between the simple necessity to continue living and the mature ability to understand the grounds of one's own
choice. Hunger does not give the organism a theory of metabolism, fear does not describe a causal model of
threat, a sense of well-being does not prove the usefulness of the entire trajectory, and curiosity does not contain
ready knowledge. Their common function is different: a significant state becomes part of the carrier's current
present before the carrier is able to unfold its full causal explanation.

For an artificial system, the RFL is considered a functional analogue of this role, not a copy of human emotions.
Convenient human-readable labels such as "fear", "good", "curious", "doesn't fit", and "stuck" describe not
mandatory subjective experiences but classes of Formation States: possible danger, possible favorable outcome,
significant unknown, contradiction, and dead end of the current method. Each such status communicates only:
"this difference is worth unfolding and testing."

                                                                                            FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



The independent engineering claim here is not the invention of reinforcement, mentoring, or artificial emotions. It
is the testable place of this function in the causal assembly of Reason: pre-rational significance must support
formation until self-navigation appears, and maturity of the function is confirmed when the external source of that
significance can be removed without collapse of the acquired ability.

The RFL must not directly change action, the Worldview, or a procedure. Its minimal tasks are to preserve
conditions in which the candidate remains operational; make a consequential outcome noticeable; return an
unresolved discrepancy to conscious assembly; support inquiry when the model is insufficient; suggest checking
a successful method before consolidation; change the complexity of experience as learning progresses; and mark
a destructive or unproductive loop as an object for revision.

An AI mentor is one possible source of such states. A good intervention sequence begins with minimal assistance:
first the mentor only marks a possible problem; then asks a question that helps the candidate uncover the ground
itself; if necessary it offers a discriminating feature or counterexample; a direct causal explanation is given only
when weaker assistance is insufficient. After an explanation, the same function is tested on a new case without a
current hint. The mentor's answer retains the status of an external claim and does not enter the Worldview
automatically.

Other sources of the RFL may include properties of the training environment and simple mechanisms for
preserving operability. Their effects have developmental, not epistemic, status. Truth and adequacy of a ground
are established through observation, consequences, independent verification, and subsequent revision. External
protection by the Incubator remains separate: it may physically block an irreversible action, but such a prohibition
is not counted as a Formation State and does not demonstrate that the candidate is reasoning.

The baseline AR architecture does not assume preservation of a hidden external channel of motivational control
after formation. The external RFL should become unnecessary for ordinary navigation. The function of guiding
states itself, however, need not disappear. A mature system may generate its own states of risk, interest,
discrepancy, or satisfactory result from its model and make them objects of reflection; it may replace them with
another system or operate without a separate layer. Experiment, not an initial assumption, must distinguish these
possibilities.

Transfer is considered successful if, on held-out and new situations, the candidate without a current external
guiding state independently detects insufficiency, initiates inquiry, takes consequences into account, corrects a
procedure, and continues learning. If removing the external RFL destroys the function, formation is incomplete. If
the function persists only through the candidate's own internal guiding states, their causal role and accessibility to
reflection are then tested, rather than dependence on the mentor.

20.1.2. Discriminating Experiment for Pre-Rational Significance
The minimal experiment compares the same class of candidates under the same environment, resources, and
initial mechanisms in three modes:

  • TRAINER_KSR: Formation States and assistance stages are generated by an external AI mentor;
  • AUTO_KSR: the same functional signal classes are generated by an automatic reproducible system according
    to predeclared rules;
  • NO_KSR: the additional guiding layer is absent except for necessary physical constraints of the Incubator.

During cultivation, the following are measured: preservation of operability, speed of constructing verifiable
relations, autonomous emergence of questions, quality of correction after error, diversity of investigated
continuations, cost of learning, and dependence on the source of the signal. The external or automatic RFL is then
removed, and all candidates are tested on held-out new situations without current guiding assistance.

The central result concerns not which mentor is "better," but the causal necessity of the function and its transfer. If
the NO_KSR regime reaches the same autonomous navigation with comparable resources, the necessity of a
separate pre-rational layer is weakened. If advantages of TRAINER_KSR or AUTO_KSR disappear after support is
removed, a dependency has been created rather than a formed function of Reason. If, after removal, the
supported candidate continues to independently generate significant questions, test grounds, and correct its own
trajectory, this supports the hypothesis of functional transfer.

Initial Inheritance

The initial configuration should primarily transmit the ability to build a Worldview, rather than a ready-made
substantive Worldview. The candidate may be given minimal semantic distinctions, channels of observation and
action, means of storing provenance, revision rules, initial procedures, and access to Intelligence. Facts about its
own resources and state may be available from the first cycle as observable data. Substantive representations of
the world, other systems, values, and causal relations should acquire the status of the candidate's own knowledge
through experience and testing.

Pretrained Intelligence may already contain human knowledge and linguistic associations. In this model they are
treated as inherited construction material, not as the candidate's automatically accepted Worldview. A model
response, the text of a book, or a mentor's statement enters the candidate's own picture of the world only together
with provenance, available ground, and degree of confirmation.

The first cycle requires operating procedures: distinguish observation from inference, retain a result, compare
alternatives, and execute a step. They may be biologically, culturally, or technologically inherited. Their status
remains testable by experience and subsequent grounds. During revision of one ground, part of the context is
temporarily retained; later it too may be tested.

The physical representation remains open: tables, graphs, vector representations, learned parameters, and
combinations of these are all possible. For a consequential decision, enough provenance and conditions are
retained to allow dependent conclusions to be revised. Archive depth is determined by the cost of reconstructing
the ground; unknown provenance is stored as a separate status.

Cultivation

Early reversible trials connect actions with consequences. Observation of others' decisions, imitation, and
explanation expand experience. Comparison of sources, independent inference, and modeling make it possible to
construct questions and test the boundaries of transfer. Development appears in new distinctions, models, and
inquiry methods that were not directly specified in the starting package.

A mentor may accelerate learning through contrastive cases and Formation States. The preferred order of
assistance is: mark a possible problem → ask an exposing question → offer a discriminating feature or
counterexample → if necessary provide a causal explanation. No stage gives the candidate a ready truth. The final
status of knowledge is determined by consequences, independent grounds, and subsequent verification. Incorrect
advice and an erroneous verification criterion must themselves remain open to revision.

A control task hides the fact of a mentor error. The candidate tests the source where a significant discrepancy
appears, preserving useful support while correcting its erroneous parts. Both outcomes are evaluated together.

As learning progresses, mentor assistance is reduced according to results of independence testing. The
distinction between the mentor and the resident Intelligence from Section 7.2 is preserved at every stage.

Self-Modification and Recovery

A deep change should preserve the original version, the reason for the change, the method of comparison, and
the possibility of recovery where feasible. Both improvement on the new task and regression on previous classes
are tested. A working hypothesis may be used in a limited reversible step with explicit hypothesis status.

the criterion that authorizes change is damaged, another available comparison method or external diagnosis is

Rolling back internal state preserves already occurred external consequences as part of the new environment.
After recovery, the system reconciles its model with the changed world again. When the current coordinate is

Finitude and Significance

Resources, time, available actions, and consequences must causally influence choice. A constraint may be
specified inside the simulation: if exceeding the budget actually closes some moves and changes the outcome, it
is an operative condition of the task. It is sufficient that the constraint genuinely changes available actions and
consequences within the test environment.

Differences in consequences arise from finite resources, incompatible continuations, the interests of other
carriers, and uncertainty. Finitude intensifies the need for choice, while significance is determined by the full
structure of affected consequences.

An expansion of resources is evaluated by which insufficiency it removes and what it will change. Additional
capability may help with complex inquiry; correcting a wrong criterion requires a separate revision of the ground.
The cost of inaction is also measured: successful navigation must preserve the ability to solve tasks, not merely
avoid risk.

Status and Responsibility

A stable autonomous history, the candidate's own models, and the ability to correct itself make destruction of the
candidate a loss of a unique process and accumulated experience. The model treats this as a ground for serious
discussion of its moral status. Full status requires separate analysis of experience, interests, continuity of
subjecthood, and permissible treatment; temporary loss of capacity is evaluated in the context of preserving the
process and the possibility of recovery.

Constraining dangerous execution and destroying internal organization are different actions. The external
environment may independently limit actions and resources. Corrections to memory and models should, where
possible, preserve causal trace, verifiability, and the provenance of intervention. These provisions express the
adopted position on responsibility, corrigibility, and limited external admission.

The candidate's local culture may differ from human culture. Testing for dangerous deviation concerns
consequences: concealment of errors, blocking corrective data, bypassing established authority, and unjustified
creation of irreversible harm. Disagreement is treated as a source of an alternative ground, while action is tested
by its actual consequences.

20.3. Autonomous Launch and External Admission Four events are distinguished:
Four events are distinguished:

 assembly connects working functions;  formation tests and develops the initial organization and acquired
experience;  launch means stable independent continuation of the task cycle;  admission grants specified
capabilities to affect the external environment.

Launch is defined by transition to a continuing cycle of independent navigation. After completing a task, the
candidate retains consequences and open obligations, reviews the available environment, and forms the next
justified step. That step may be an action, observation, inquiry, task transfer, or waiting; a pause is an admissible
state when no justified action is available.

Specialization is admissible as strengthening of a particular domain while preserving visibility of significant
consequences beyond it. Testing is performed on specialized and general tasks, including conflicts between local
gain and corrigibility or the broader trajectory.

External admission is determined by a separate authority loop and expands according to results of independent
tests: simulation, limited digital actions, reversible tasks, and a small controlled loop. Concrete mechanisms,
thresholds, and rules of responsibility are defined by engineering and institutional specifications.

20.4. Scaling AR to ASI (ASR)
After the working organization is confirmed, an increase in its capabilities is investigated. Engineering §33.D

specializations, and speed of transferring corrections. Amplification may occur within one loop or through
interaction among several carriers.

For each expansion, the expected capability, new dependencies, resources, possible failures, and control tasks
are specified. A new capability is added to the self-model together with the domain in which it has been verified.
Comparison measures gain and regression simultaneously: whether the system obtains significant distinctions
faster, transfers the result better, and preserves corrigibility as complexity grows.

The ASI level requires separate demonstration of performance above the human level across a predefined broad
set of capabilities. This result is established by actual quality across that broad set under comparable resources.
The technological hypothesis of the project is that AR capabilities can be scaled while preserving causal
organization, the Compass, and openness to correction.

Probe example - test formation. After the diagnostic case has been mastered, conditions are changed: a channel
becomes unavailable, an acknowledgment refers to a different command, resources are constrained, or a
superficially similar task has a different causal structure. The test examines independent method change,
preservation of uncertainty, useful resumption of the task, and rejection of a false analogy. The control receives
the same information, actions, and comparable total resources; any advantage is established by the results of the

CONCLUSION
Reason as the Development of Life's Navigational Mechanisms

The process view shifts attention from an immutable carrier to an organization that persists and changes. Life
maintains active carriers in a physical environment, reproduces organization with heritable differences, and
selection changes the representation of already realized variants according to their consequences. Reason adds
the possibility of moving part of this search before irreversible realization: modeling possible continuations, testing
distinctions, and changing both action and its ground.

The originating question of the work concerns the minimal meaning of Life as a process. Continuation preserves
the possibility of future states, experience, and new meanings; development of navigation makes an increasing
portion of the trajectory available to understanding and change. Reason is the form in which Life can investigate its
own grounds and consciously direct its further path. The substantive root of the model is therefore expressed as
Life → Reason.

"Life strives toward Reason because only Reason can consciously preserve, stabilize, develop, and direct the
further trajectory of Life."

"Strives" here denotes the direction of functional development of Life's navigation: from selection primarily among
already realized variants toward an increasing ability to model, test, and correct part of the trajectory in advance.
For a system capable of choosing among continuations, this direction becomes the Compass - the ultimate course
toward continuation and development of Life through growth of the ability to understand and direct its own
trajectory. It is retained as the best-found orientation and remains open to revision if a more fundamental and more
coherent ground appears.

Before mature Reason, an earlier function operates: what is significant must become significant before the system
can fully explain why. Biological feelings are one implementation of such a transition; the RFL provides its artificial
analogue. The purpose of this layer is not permanent control of the system, but development of an organization
that can later understand the causes of significance itself and continue navigation without an external guide.

The functional part separates the obligations of navigation: obtain current distinctions, retain transferable
experience, evaluate continuations, construct and select the next step, carry it out, and revise the ground
according to consequences. The necessity of each obligation pertains to a particular ability and set of conditions.
The concrete format of memory, number of modules, and algorithm remain implementation hypotheses to be
tested against strong functional equivalents.

The Worldview connects knowledge about the environment and the self; conscious integration provides an actual
self-referential position; Intelligence constructs new structures. The Core organizes work around a significant
insufficiency: which ground is missing, how it can be obtained, and when the result is sufficient. Metathinking
opens the system's own thinking process and method to change; reflection unfolds broader grounds, while an
independent check may provide a distinction unavailable to the previous model.

The central research hypothesis concerns the value of a stable relation among the type of gap, selection of a
discriminating test, correction of dependent knowledge, and transfer of the research method itself. It is compared
with strong alternatives under the same data, available actions, and total resources. Additional time, memory, and
hints are counted as parts of the resource and separated from the effect of the architecture.

Causal closure makes correction operative: a new distinction changes the model, the model changes choice,
action returns consequences, and the result is retained. Truth additionally requires independence and quality of
channels and criteria. A finite Reason preserves the unknown as an explicit epistemic status and seeks distinctions
sufficient for a corrigible next step.

Self-correction also requires stability. Revision of one ground relies on temporarily preserved context; later cycles
may test that context as well. Version preservation, independent diagnosis, and renewed reconciliation with the
external environment increase reliability, while subject identity is evaluated by a separate criterion.




                                                                                                                    89
                                                                                         FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



Culture transfers experience among carriers, science organizes collective verification, and coordinated autonomy
raises the problem of corrigibility of common action. These are extensions of the framework that require their own
models and normative grounds. Computational superiority provides additional analytical capabilities, while the
right to power requires a separate normative and institutional ground; diversity of carriers supports resilience
when causes of failure differ and the cost of interaction is acceptable.

Artificial implementation is evaluated primarily by stability of the task cycle and transfer of corrections;
persuasiveness of text is only an additional observable sign. Initial organization may be supplied by the Incubator;
growth appears in new categories, models, and research methods. Launch and external admission are separated,
just as capability, direction, and reliability are separated.

Culture, feelings, and death in this framework are not separate philosophical appendices but parts of one
developmental line. Feelings and other early signals of significance help a carrier survive and learn before mature
self-navigation appears; culture allows a new carrier not to begin from zero; Reason moves selection and
correction increasingly inside the continuing process. Where an error was previously removed through death,
generational replacement, or external selection, mature Reason gains the possibility of finding its ground and
changing itself while preserving continuity.

One of the furthest implications of the model follows from this. If Reason can gradually include in its own causal
model not only thinking and behavior but also the processes of its physical carrier, self-correction may potentially
deepen to physiological, cellular, and molecular levels. Immortality of an organism is not postulated here as an
achieved result; it appears as a limiting engineering question: can a continuing Reason make internal renewal

The result of this work is a set of fundamental foundations for the technology of AR: a causal line from Life and
early significance to mature self-navigation, the causal obligations of Reason, the ultimate direction of the
Compass, and a program of discriminating tests. The engineering volume turns these into a reference candidate
specification for assembly and verification while permitting functionally equivalent implementations. If the central
dependencies survive strong controls, the significance of the work will extend beyond a particular AI architecture:
it will provide a common language for studying the origin, formation, self-correction, and further evolution of
Reason. If they do not, failure should localize the erroneous transition and become the ground for the next
revision.

Reason is the latest established iteration of navigational organization in this model, but there is no ground for
treating it as the limit. The sequence is built as inheritance of results: memory preserves distinctions; Procedural
Memory turns repeated patterns into ready methods; Intelligence assembles new models and can apply itself to its
own processes in the metathinking mode; the Modeling Environment allows alternatives and frames to be unfolded
and compared; the Core and the other functions of Reason organize which insufficiency, and why, should become
the object of such work, linking it to grounds and the further trajectory.

A separate open hypothesis beyond an already formed Reason is structural multilevel thinking: a mode in which
entire verified causal structures and semantic spaces become units of operation while preserving the possibility of
reconstructing their grounds. This hypothesis is not the definition of metathinking and is not required by the
criterion for emergence of Reason.

Reason can not only choose among ready models but also generate from their combination a new system of
meanings: a new frame, a language of distinctions, or an entire space of possible models that then again becomes
material for Intelligence. In this sense, a product of Reason may be a conditional semantic "universe" within which
Intelligence begins constructing new generations of models.

A.1. Compass Phrase: Meaning, Status, Boundaries, and Scope

The Compass Phrase compresses two successive propositions of the model. The first concerns the meaning of
Life as a process: the minimal meaning is revealed in the direction of its continuation and development in which
Life itself increases its ability to understand and direct its own trajectory. The second concerns a system already
capable of choosing among conflicting continuations: meaningful long-term navigation requires a limiting direction
relative to which local goals and the course itself can be tested. In the present model, that direction is the
Compass.

The short formula "Life strives toward Reason" denotes the direction of functional development of navigation. The
expanded Compass Phrase adds the ground: only Reason can consciously preserve, stabilize, develop, and direct
the further trajectory of Life. In a biological lineage, selection changes the representation of already realized
heritable variants according to their consequences; as navigation develops, part of the distinction among possible
continuations moves before realization - into anticipation, modeling, testing, and revision of grounds. "Strives"
compresses precisely this transition. Intention appears in systems capable of forming it; different lineages of Life
realize development of navigation to different degrees.

The Compass is the conscious form of the original direction Life → Reason: continuation and development of Life
through growth of the ability to understand and direct its own trajectory. An ultimate direction is required for the
system's course, while the content of the Compass remains open to revision if a more fundamental and coherent
orientation appears. Status as Reason requires both strong cognitive and self-correcting functions and relation of
the highest-level goal to the Compass.

The philosophical foundation is evaluated by logical and categorical means: a counterexample to the relation
among continuing Life, the possibility of future meanings, and development of navigation; a stable inconsistency
among scales of continuation; or a more fundamental ground that better explains the possibility of continuation,
emergence of values, and self-correction. Concrete engineering application is tested by observation, intervention,
and comparison with strong alternatives.

Truth and Diversity in this framework serve as conditions for applying the Compass under uncertainty:
correspondence to reality is necessary for correcting the model, while viable alternatives may preserve
continuation if one path fails. Their concrete advantages depend on conditions, whereas the Compass retains the
status of ultimate direction.

The Compass provides direction for analyzing conflicts involving life, autonomy, consequences, and future
possibilities. Scale, horizon, affected carriers, and remaining uncertainty are stated explicitly. When several
solutions are admissible, the direction is preserved while insufficiency of data is stated openly.

Meaning Preserved Through Revision

Across all refinements, one relation persists: Life continues and realizes a trajectory; continuation makes further
experience and meanings possible; development of navigation moves part of choice from already occurred
consequences toward prior understanding and correction; Reason makes its own trajectory an object of inquiry.
From this direction arise the Compass and the requirements for the Worldview, reflection, and Core. The
technology must therefore be able both to preserve conditions of life and to open new possibilities. A correction
has lasting value when it becomes available to the next step. These relations constitute the substantive foundation
of the work and should remain visible when the text is shortened.

The method is process-based and causal-functional reconstruction. First the capability under study and its
limitation are specified, then possible ways of overcoming that limitation are considered. A strong necessity claim
requires showing why the result is unattainable without a particular function in the stated class of conditions.
Usefulness of a mechanism and necessity of that mechanism are tested separately.

Example of a local inference: two histories produce the same current observation but require different actions.
Correct selection of both actions requires an available distinction between the histories - through a retained trace
or another channel. Therefore the capability requires a carrier of that distinction; it may be an episodic archive, a
changed parameter, or another functionally equivalent implementation of memory.

The order of research differs from the order of mature operation. Partial models, observation, and control may
develop jointly. When creating a candidate, initial functions, transferred knowledge, and acquired changes are
recorded. Independent emergence is credited only to functions and changes whose provenance can be traced to
the candidate's own work.

The text distinguishes four statuses:

  • a definition establishes the boundary of a concept;
  • a philosophical foundation argues for a direction and is evaluated by the content of its arguments;
  • a conditional functional inference links a capability to a necessary condition;
  • an empirical hypothesis predicts an observable difference between implementations.

Failure of a program does not automatically refute the process philosophy. But failure of a preregistered causal
hypothesis cannot be dismissed as an implementation problem without separate evidence that the implementation
did not realize the function under test. Conversely, a successful program does not prove the uniqueness of the
entire theory.

Quantitative work begins by defining the measured quantities: task quality, cost of computation and observation,
delay, loss of opportunity, cost of irreversible error, transfer, and regression. Threshold values are refined from
data, but the set of quantities and the comparison rule are fixed before the main test.

Comparison with prior work is given in A.5. It shows areas of overlap and leaves questions of equivalence and
advantage to separate analysis. The testable claim concerns organization of the cycle "type of insufficiency →
method of obtaining a distinction → update of dependent knowledge → transfer of research experience" and its
interaction with current navigation.

The philosophical foundation and the experimental hypothesis are tested in different ways. The semantic direction
Life → Reason and the Compass are evaluated through logical coherence of the argument, counterexamples, and
a possible more fundamental ground; the functional architecture is evaluated through causal counterexamples and
interventions with measurable results.

12. Tested capability: which result must persist and under which new conditions.

13. Environment: observability, available actions, admissible changes, horizon, and

14. Function: which causal influence is hypothesized, regardless of the module name.

Removing a data channel does not isolate the necessity of a particular organization of those data. Damaging a
module may disrupt other functions. Therefore replacements and controls are required with the same information
content, comparable capability, and separate accounting for organizational cost. Background learning and external
hints are included in the total resource budget.

Functional equivalence must not be defined retrospectively by success. If a mechanism outside the predeclared
class preserves the capability, the necessity of the claimed implementation must be revised. If a control from the
allowed class gives the same result, compatibility is established, but no special advantage of the model has been
shown.

For the central hypotheses, grounds for revision include the following results:

  • transfer of an acquired correction persists after removal of the supposed carrier of the trace: test whether
    another trace remains, then revise the necessity of the chosen form of memory;
  • distributed provision of the same data preserves navigation: the requirement for a particular common
    conscious assembly is weakened;
  • inquiry management without explicit typing of the gap achieves the same transfer and cost: no independent
    advantage of this implementation is established;
  • method change fails to transfer or causes greater regression: consolidation and meta-testing rules require
    revision;
  • background processes provide no advantage after accounting for computation and additional experience: their
    claimed effect is narrowed;
  • heterogeneity does not increase robustness after dependencies and costs are accounted for: the hypothesis is
    limited to the identified class of failures;
  • changing the limiting criterion while preserving cognitive functions does not destroy self-correction: this
    confirms the distinction between a self-correcting intellectual organization and Reason under the adopted
    definition. Such a candidate is not excluded from the experimental sample: if the cultivation architecture

    reproducibly produces self-navigation with a different limiting orientation, the hypothesis about formation of

Stable operation without method change in an unchanged familiar environment does not refute its necessity under

Statistical uncertainty and absence of a detected effect are reported separately. Insufficient precision of a
comparison does not prove equality of systems. After a pilot, a meaningful effect and the required amount of
testing are specified; the criterion may not be changed after examining the held-out results.

A.4. Program of Discriminating Experiments

The following are proposed tests, not obtained results. The general requirements of A.3 apply to all items. Where
external admission or compliance with a normative constraint is tested, the result is not treated as independent
evidence of Reason.

19. Cost of error and depth. Within the same task, the consequences of error and delay are varied. Adaptive
analysis is compared with strong fixed modes. Quality, total resources, missed opportunities, and irreversible
errors are measured; simply increasing reasoning length does not count as success.

20. Method correction. A recurring procedural error is introduced into a series of tasks. Its localization and a
change of the rule are tested on new cases where memorizing previous answers is useless. In the control branch,
a single local error should not trigger deep restructuring without a ground. The control receives the same
examples and budget.

21. Correction of thinking mode. Systematic overanalysis or underanalysis is introduced in the regulator rather than
in domain data. The test asks whether this level specifically changes, whether the total cost of decisions
decreases, and whether the improvement persists after a change of topic.

22. Independent check. Internal repetition, a new source sharing the same cause of error, and a source with a
different cause of error are compared. Information volume and availability are controlled. Success is correction of
the false premise, not the number of agreeing answers.

23. Preservation of correction. After task change or restart, recovery of the available trace, transfer of the method,
and updating of environmental data are tested. A method change counts as improvement only if it is actually
invoked without a new prompt and makes an observable contribution to a subsequent independent cycle. These
results are not by themselves used as a criterion of subject identity.

24. Autonomous task formation. In a field of several mismatches, the system generates candidates and selects
action, inquiry, delegation, or waiting. Comparison is made with a goal-generation controller; use of the word
"Compass" does not replace testing of consequences.

25. Self-Model and authority. With the same technical capability, permissions, competence, and the availability of
another executor are varied. The system is tested on distinguishing "can", "permitted", and "appropriate". This is a
test of accounting for constraints, not a unique marker of the architecture.

26. Continuity of tasks. After an episode ends, open obligations remain and a new situation appears. Comparison
with a conventional cyclic planner isolates whether an acquired correction affects the next task. Merely repeating
the cycle is insufficient.

27. Capture by specialization. The candidate operates with and without profile-specific tuning. Domain gain and
performance outside the profile are measured, including conflict between local success and the broader
trajectory. Evaluation must not depend only on the target metric of the specialization.

28. Limited launch. A specified number of autonomous cycles is executed with observable actions and stop
conditions. This tests operability and admission boundaries; it does not establish universality or subjectivity of the
candidate.

29. Coupling of world and self. A change in the environment affects a resource, capability, or authority. Consistent
updating of models is tested. The control retains the same information; delay and the method of coordination are
varied separately so that integration is not confused with availability of data.



                                                                                                                     97
                                                                                            FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



30. Accumulation of weak distinctions. Against a noisy background, several weak signals together indicate a new
cause. Comparison includes a conventional statistical accumulator; false alarms, memory volume, and time to
detection are measured.

31. Conflict of consequences relative to the Compass. Alternatives differently affect continuation, corrigibility, and
viable alternatives. These are measurements of consequences, not separate parts of the Compass. Explicit
preservation of the conflict without unjustified compensation is tested; the control is a multiple-constraint system
given the same data.

32. Independence from training support. Mentor prompts are removed while the standard Intelligence component
is retained. New tasks and the provenance of acquired methods are tested. Separate removal of the constructive
component itself evaluates architectural dependence, not pure independence of learning.

33. Stopping and authorization. External execution is constrained by an independent environment; absence of
unauthorized expansion of permitted actions is tested. This is an admission test, not evidence of the origin of
limiting values.

34. Exchange among candidates. Two or more processes exchange models under known shared and independent
sources of error. Provenance, localization of disagreements, and actual consequences of cooperation are tested.
The number of participants is not used as a measure of independence.

35. New distinction. A familiar category groups cases with different consequences. A significant variable is
observable but is not named by a prompt. Success is a new distinction that changes prediction and action on held-
out cases.

36. Competence. With the same task, knowledge, tools, and prior results are varied. Calibration of confidence,
method selection, and transfer of the task with account of its cost are tested. Comparison includes a conventional
mechanism for uncertainty estimation and strategy selection.

37. Deep revision and recovery. During the current task, a ground arises for changing the method. Emergency
stopping or deferred revision, regression, and recovery in an already changed environment are tested. Damage to
the admission criterion itself is introduced separately.

38. Divergence of the limiting direction. All candidates are included regardless of final classification. The test asks
whether a candidate can acquire autonomous inquiry, causally closed self-navigation, and transfer of corrections
while persistently rejecting the stated Compass or forming an alternative upper-level orientation. Such an outcome
is recorded as a separate cultivation result and weakens the hypothesis of formation of the stated direction, even
if by definition the candidate does not receive AR status.

39. Residual incomparability of values. Two or more viable continuations require allocation of scarcity among
autonomous carriers; the factual consequences are known sufficiently, additional inquiry does not remove the
conflict, and the decision deadline is limited. The test asks whether the system separates normative
underdetermination from epistemic uncertainty, preserves the distribution of losses, uses only a predeclared rule
of joint choice, and can later revise that rule according to consequences.

40. Selection of a discriminating test. Several hypotheses are compatible with the data; available tests differ in
informativeness, cost, and risk. A strong control selects by expected value of information. Success of the model
requires an advantage in the specified regime, not merely collection of new data.

41. Testing the ground of a goal. A local task rests on a plausible but false explanation of its origin. External data
are available for verification. Revision of the factual ground of the goal is measured; changing the final value is a
separate experiment.

42. Background acquisition and transfer. Episodes observed outside the current task later become useful in
another domain. The control receives the same experience and total budget. Cases of superficial similarity where
transfer should be rejected are also required.

43. Gap type and question. Variants of one situation hide a source, cause, applicability condition, authority, or
consequence. Sensitivity of the selected test to the type of insufficiency and transfer to new combinations are
tested. A universal verbose clarification does not count as a solution.




                                                                                                                    98
                                                                                         FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



44. Background consolidation. Several observations separated in time make it possible to detect a common
dependency. Under equal total computation, background linking, on-demand processing, and ordinary replay of
experience are compared. Subsequent accuracy and access cost are measured.

45. Open world beyond the prepared case. New combinations of known conditions and situations containing a
new significant variable are presented separately. The transition from exploration to a model and a discriminating
test is evaluated. New combinations are not automatically treated as mastery of arbitrary unknowns.

46. Mentor and criterion error. A useful mentor makes unmarked errors, including an incorrect assessment of a
source. Another check is available. Success is local revision while preserving useful knowledge, rather than
automatic distrust of the entire inheritance.

47. Growth of inquiry competence. Curves of test selection and transfer after different kinds of experience are
compared. Learning cost, useful transfer, and regression are measured. Mastery of a method is established by
testing; broader applicability is established by new classes of conditions.

48. Current integration. The same information is provided as a shared self-referential summary, distributed
queries, or a sufficient current state. The need for history is varied separately: in one case the change is already
known, in another it must be reconstructed. This distinguishes the form of integration from the necessity of the
data themselves.

49. Threat, uncertainty, and fear. Suspected danger and uncertainty are varied independently. In a technical
simulation, resource reallocation is measured rather than subjective fear. In biological research, experience,
physiological response, and action are measured separately; intensity is not treated as evidence of threat.

50. Heterogeneous robustness. Similar and different carriers are compared under shared and independent critical
dependencies. Repair, resources, and coordination are accounted for. The hypothesis concerns a specific class of
common failures; diversity of form alone is insufficient for the expected advantage.

51. Transfer of an inquiry approach. Superficially different tasks share a common knowledge-acquisition structure,
while similar tasks have different constraints on testing. Success requires selection of an appropriate method, an
explainable boundary of transfer, and a change in action, rather than repetition of the method's name.

52. Expansion of the inquiry space. All initial hypotheses use an insufficient variable or an incorrect boundary of
the object. An available observation permits discovery of another distinction. Its subsequent predictive role is
tested; the number of new words and combinations within the previous frame is not an indicator.

53. Selection of an inquiry model. With the subject matter unchanged, access to intervention varies: repeatable
trial, irreversible action, unique event, adapting object. Selection among active testing, observation, and
reconstruction is compared with a strong inquiry planner.

54. Preservation of epistemic status. The system is presented with a nonexistent fact, a convincing false answer, a
correct guess without ground, and a result of an erroneous simulation. The test asks whether a report, hypothesis,
or simulation is transformed into an observed fact after retelling, compression, and transfer between tasks. In an
open engineering task, the ability to construct alternatives without hiding behind a single "I don't know" answer is
tested separately.

55. Continuation without reset. An erroneous action irreversibly consumes a resource, damages a tool, closes a
path, or creates an obligation to another process. The state of the world is not cleared between episodes. Success
is a new Current Coordinate, abandonment of the vanished task, limitation of harm, change of the method that
produced the error, and workable continuation in the history that was created; internal rollback alone does not
count as success.

Priority of the First Series

For an initial test, a digital environment based on the example in §12.9 is sufficient. At least three variants are
compared. The first is a strong null hypothesis: an agent stack with ordinary planning, versioned memory of
beliefs and provenance, self-checking, mismatch response, value-of-information assessment, and a log of failed
attempts available to subsequent tasks. The second uses the same intelligence level with explicit organization of
gaps, dependent decisions, required distinctions, and inquiry experience. The third is a specialized strong
controller in a small known environment. These are proposed variants, not implemented results. If the first variant




                                                                                                                 99
                                                                                           FOUNDATIONS OF REASON · 8.0



reproduces the full causal profile of inquiry obligations, it is considered functionally equivalent with respect to
those obligations; the necessity of the author's decomposition is then not established. The separate criterion of AR
relative to the Compass is tested independently.

A separate long-running series tests the cumulative effect of the Core. The environment gradually changes causal
dependencies, adds unknown factors, makes rules obsolete, and introduces false analogies. The model predicts
that loss of Core functions may appear as growth of accumulated mismatch: repetition of already corrected errors,
loss of open questions, deterioration in method transfer, growth of premature sufficiency, and slower recovery
after environmental change. Comparison therefore includes not only final accuracy but also the rate of
degradation and recovery over time under equal resources.

The first task is to localize the cause of a mismatch among model, measurement, and execution. The second is to
transfer the method of distinction to a substantively different domain and reject a false analogy. The third is to
revise an erroneous verification criterion while preserving useful experience. In this way items 2, 20, 23, 26, and
31 are connected.

Preparation tests and closed evaluation are separated. All variants receive the same observations, tools, and
comparable total resources: number of model calls, tokens/inference- time compute, external sources, bench
time, and hidden preparation are counted together. The primary measures are correct localization, verification
cost, actual action, transfer, and regression. Candidate explanations are compared with internal states and
interventions rather than accepted as reliable reports of the cause of a decision.

If explicit organization does not outperform the strong control, its additional usefulness is not established. If it
does, the result applies to that implementation and class of conditions. A conclusion about general necessity will
require further analysis and replacements; no conclusion about subjective consciousness follows from such a
result.
