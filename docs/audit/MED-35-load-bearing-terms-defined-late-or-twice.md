# MED-35 · Load-bearing terms are defined late, inconsistently or not at all

## Claims under review

- **Vol I guide §1, p. 2:** "A ground here means what a conclusion or action actually relies
  on." **Vol I §12.4, p. 54:** "A rational question arises from a significant insufficiency of
  a ground for understanding, evaluation, or action."; "A ground here is what the system
  actually relies on: an observation, claim, causal relation, source, criterion, value, method,
  self-model, scope of a rule, authority, or another relation on which understanding the
  situation, evaluating its significance, choosing an investigative method, or making a choice
  depends."
- **Vol I guide §6, p. 6:** "REASON_CLOSURE is the name of a testable state in which these
  functions close into a continuing cycle."
- **Vol I §14.1, p. 70:** "Death is the loss by a living system of the capacity to preserve its
  own causal continuity as a whole." **Vol I Ch. 14 box, p. 71:** "Death of a carrier is the
  irreversible loss of the capacity of that carrier to maintain its own causal continuity as an
  operating process."
- **Vol I Key Definitions, p. 11:** "Trajectory - a sequence of causally connected states
  actually realized by a process among available continuations." **Vol I Ch. 6, p. 35:**
  "Choice is the temporary preference of one of the available trajectories"

## Finding

The Key Definitions of Vol I are mostly used steadily, but a few terms that the argument leans
on are missing from them or drift within them, as the claims above show. "Ground", "gap",
"required distinction", "sufficiency", "carrier", REASON_CLOSURE, the Incubator and external
admission have no entry, and the last two appear before they are introduced; "trajectory",
"death" and "ethics" are each defined in more than one way.

## Evidence

- **Vol I §12.4, p. 54:** the definition of ground widens to any observation, claim, causal
  relation and more, and the word "significant" has no stated threshold.
- **Vol II §31.0, p. 78:** "AR emerges at REASON_CLOSURE". The Key Definitions (Vol I, p. 12)
  define only "Causal closure".
- **Vol I §14.1, p. 70; Ch. 14 box, p. 71:** the first definition has no reversibility clause;
  the box adds "irreversible" and replaces "as a whole" with "as an operating process".
- **Vol I Key Definitions, p. 11; Ch. 6, p. 35:** the Key Definition counts only states
  "actually realized", while Ch. 6 speaks of choosing among "the available trajectories".
- **Vol I §20.1.1, p. 85; Ch. 15, p. 74:** "External protection by the Incubator remains
  separate" and "engineering constraints of external admission" are used before §20.2 and §20.3
  introduce them; neither is a Key Definition.
- **Vol I Key Definitions, p. 13; Ch. 15, p. 74:** ethics is "a system of rules for evaluating
  permissible and required choices" in one place and "a layer of compressed evaluations and
  rules of choice" in another.

## Why it matters

These terms carry tests: the Core fires on a "significant" insufficiency, REASON_CLOSURE
defines AR, and the death definition decides whether a pause or rollback destroys a candidate.

## Ideas & possible recommendations

- **New Key Definitions entries.** Entries could be added for ground, gap, required
  distinction, sufficiency, carrier, REASON_CLOSURE, Incubator and external admission.
- **A criterion for "significant".** A criterion could separate an insufficient ground from an
  uncertain one.
- **One definition each.** One definition each of trajectory, death (with a reversibility
  clause) and ethics could be kept.

## Resolved when

- [ ] The Key Definitions define the terms above once each.
- [ ] Chapter boxes and the Key Definitions agree, and death has a reversibility clause.
