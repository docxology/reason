# MED-20 · Coined vocabulary and length are the main barrier to review

**Finding.** The author reports that earlier reviewers could not reconstruct the architecture.
The text explains that better than the readers do. Two volumes of 105 and 118 pages introduce
a large private vocabulary (Compass, Current Coordinate, Core of Reason, Formation States,
RFL, REASON_CLOSURE, Factor X, structure of ignorance, 72 record types), much of which has a
standard equivalent. Volume I's mapping of its terms to standard ones, a good piece of work,
is its last section (A.5's terminological correspondence map). Volume II's bridge glossary
promises "technical correspondence" with Volume I but covers 3 of Volume I's 33 Key
Definitions; nine of its sixteen terms do not occur in Volume I, and the Core of Reason,
Worldview, Value Map, Compass, RFL and Formation State have no entry. Editorial defects
(MIN-01 to MIN-08, MIN-14) compound the difficulty, and continuous chapter numbering means a
reader of Volume II meets references to Volume I sections without context.

## Evidence

- **Already in the text (Vol I A.5, p. 103):** a "TERMINOLOGICAL CORRESPONDENCE MAP" setting each
  coined term against its nearest existing concepts, at the end of the volume.
- **Vol II front matter, p. 12:** "Terms are defined by the causal model in Volume I, version
  8.0; technical correspondence is given in Appendix A."
- **Vol II Appendix A, pp. 115–116:** sixteen glossary entries, of which "consciousness",
  "current coordinate" and "self-navigation" are among Volume I's Key Definitions.
- The near-duplicate paragraphs, truncations and broken numbering catalogued in MIN-01 to
  MIN-04.

## Why it matters

A reader who cannot map a term to something they know cannot evaluate the claim it carries.
Once reconstructed, the architecture is coherent and the testable core modest and clear; the
barrier is presentational, and it is the main reason the work has not been reviewed.

## Recommendation

Open each volume with a one-page glossary that maps every coined term to its nearest standard
term and says what differs (A.5's map is a good start), rebuild Volume II's Appendix A around
Volume I's Key Definitions, write the 10–12 page paper of review §10 as the entry point, and
do a careful human editorial pass.
