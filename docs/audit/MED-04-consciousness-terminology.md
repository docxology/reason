# MED-04 · "Consciousness" and "causal" carry meanings that invite misreading

**Finding.** "Consciousness" in the volumes is a continuously updated, self-referential
integration of the current state, with clear disclaimers about subjective experience.
Functionally it is state estimation including the system's own state, plus what engineers
call situation awareness (Endsley 1995) and psychologists call reality monitoring (Johnson &
Raye 1981; Lau 2019). Volume II then says the assembly "becomes functioning Consciousness" at
REASON_CLOSURE, although the same records are used before and after closure and only the
source of direction changes, while its glossary equates Consciousness with
`ConsciousAssemblyState` without qualification; whether Consciousness exists before closure
depends on which passage is read. Most audiences will read these sentences as claims about
machine consciousness, an argument the work does not need. Separately, "causal" marks both
interventional claims (a methodological commitment) and the "causal-semantic Worldview" (a
description of content).

## Evidence

- **Vol II §25.B, p. 34, and §31.0, p. 78:** the self-referential assembly "becomes
  functioning Consciousness".
- **Vol II Appendix A, p. 115:** "consciousness → ConsciousAssemblyState: an updated process of
  self-related integration of fresh observations, internal states, actions and the available
  situation".
- **Vol I Ch. 9, p. 41:** "Subjective experience is a separate object of study; its presence in a
  technical analogue requires independent criteria."
- **Vol I §11.1, p. 50:** the Worldview as a "causal-semantic" store.
- **Already in the text:** A.5 maps the term onto global-workspace theory (Dehaene & Changeux
  2011) and cites the indicator properties of Butlin et al. (2023), but only in the appendix.

## Why it matters

Terminology that triggers the machine-consciousness debate draws reviewers away from the
testable claims, and a reader who meets "Consciousness" in Chapter 9 without the A.5 mapping
will misjudge what is proposed. Awarding the label at closure makes it look like a finding
rather than a name.

## Recommendation

Use a neutral term in the chapters (for example "current-state integration") with the mapping
to global workspace, self-model theories (Metzinger 2003) and the Butlin et al. indicators
placed in Chapter 9; describe closure as a change in the source of direction, not as the
emergence of Consciousness. Reserve "causal" for interventional claims.

## Resolved when

- [ ] The chapters use a neutral term, or state the functional definition and mapping where
      "Consciousness" is introduced, and closure is not described as its emergence.
- [ ] "Causal" is used in one sense throughout.
