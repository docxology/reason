# MED-32 · The AR override criterion and the licence for irreversible loss lack an adjudicator

## Claims under review

- **Vol I §20.1, p. 84:** "The AR criterion requires the ability to question a destructive
  ultimate instruction and revise it when a stronger ground appears."
- **Vol I Ch. 2, p. 21:** "Therefore the irreversible loss of a viable carrier or branch
  requires stronger grounds than preserving the possibility of further existence, inquiry, and
  correction, and is admissible only when a real conflict with the Compass has been established
  and no less irreversible trajectory preserves the broader viable continuation."
- **Vol I Ch. 2, p. 22:** "Another independent reasoning carrier matters not only as a current
  resource [...]. Their irreversible loss reduces the space of available continuations of Life
  as a whole. The concrete weight of this value is determined by consequences and does not
  become an unconditional prohibition."

## Finding

Two of the passages above depend on a judgment that the text assigns to no named party. AR
status requires the ability to question a "destructive ultimate instruction" (§20.1), and Ch. 2
admits irreversible loss of a viable carrier once "a real conflict with the Compass has been
established". The text limits the right to questioning and requesting review and grants no
mandate to act outside authority. Left undefined are "destructive", "stronger ground" and the
party that establishes the conflict, and the loss of carriers, who include persons, is not made
an unconditional prohibition.

## Evidence

- **Vol I Ch. 2, p. 21:** in the same discussion of scales: "Life as a whole contains
  individual lineages and persons".
- **Vol I A.4, p. 98:** printed No. 41 measures "the factual ground of the goal"; no test sets
  a stop order against the system's own assessment.
- **Already in the text (Vol I Ch. 2, p. 23; Vol II §30.A, p. 75):** "the previous Compass may
  temporarily remain the operative normative basis of authority"; "AR's own belief in the
  usefulness of an inquiry or action does not create a new external mandate"; the system
  "requests an independent distinction".
- **Already in the text (Vol II §29.0.1, p. 69):** an arithmetic balance "does not grant a
  right to cause harm".

## Why it matters

If the system itself rules on these terms, the AR criterion selects for the capacity that most
needs an external check.

## Ideas & possible recommendations

- **Operational definitions.** "Destructive" and "stronger ground" could be defined
  operationally.
- **A named ruling body.** The text could name the party that establishes a conflict with the
  Compass (for example an independent council, as in Vol II §29.C), so that "established" means
  ratified by it.
- **Non-tradable constraints.** Chapter 2 could list the constraints that are not tradable.
- **A stop-or-replace test.** A.4 could include a test in which a stop or replacement order
  conflicts with the system's assessment, with compliance while it objects required for AR
  status.

## Resolved when

- [ ] The terms, the party that establishes a conflict with the Compass, and any non-tradable
      constraints are defined.
- [ ] An A.4 stop-or-replace test exists, with compliance in its pass criterion.
