# MIN-15 · Defined terms change name and capitalization

## Claims under review

- **Vol II front matter, p. 12:** "Terms are defined by the causal model in Volume I, version
  8.0; technical correspondence is given in Appendix A."
- **Vol II App. A, p. 115:** "The fundamental volume defines causal functions and their
  boundaries, while the engineering volume separates those same functions into data structures,
  authorization decisions and execution operations. Correspondence between terms does not mean
  identity: one semantic function may be implemented by a sequence of several technical
  stages."
- **Vol I Key Definitions, p. 12:** "Modeling Environment - an organization of processes that
  allows models, frames, hypotheses, and possible continuations to be temporarily constructed,
  held, compared, and restructured before they are accepted into the Worldview or realized in
  action."

## Finding

Volume II says its terms are defined by Volume I and mapped in Appendix A, yet the volumes name
one component in several ways that Appendix A does not reconcile and print one name in several
capitalizations, so a reader cannot tell whether a coined term or an ordinary phrase is meant,
or whether two names denote one component. The drift reaches identifiers too:
`ingest_proposals` and `ingest_proposal()` (§28.C), "Fundamental risk" and `FoundationalRisk`
(§29.A).

## Evidence

- **Modeling Environment.** Volume I's component is "Modeling Environment" (guide, p. 4; Vol II
  §28.0, p. 52), but Vol II's §28.C heading says "Modeling Workspace" (p. 57) while its
  contents list says "Modeling Environment" for the same section (p. 10), and §33.A.8 and
  Appendix A say "Simulation Environment" (pp. 104, 116). Appendix A has no entry reconciling
  them.
- **Already in the text (Vol II Appendix A, pp. 115–116):** "Correspondence between terms does
  not mean identity: one semantic function may be implemented by a sequence of several
  technical stages." A.1 lists `ModelingWorkspace`/`WorkspaceOverlay` as an engineering
  counterpart of the Volume I notation. So a Volume II record need not be the same thing as a
  Volume I component. What no entry says is how the prose names "Modeling Workspace" and
  "Simulation Environment" relate to the Modeling Environment.
- **Formation guide.** Labels for the external help in formation include "trainer" (Vol I §7.2,
  p. 37), "teacher" (Vol II §29.0.2, p. 69), `TEACHER_LLM` (§22.A, p. 17), `TRAINER_AI`
  (§26.0.1, p. 40) and "mentor" in prose, as in "teacher_agreement_bias tests how strongly the
  candidate tends to accept a mentor's" (§27.6, p. 47). Not all of them need name the same
  role: Vol I §7.2 lists the trainer as one of three roles, beside resident Intelligence and
  an external tool; `TEACHER_LLM` is a value of `hypothesis_origin`, which records where a
  hypothesis came from; and Vol II §33.A.1 (p. 103) separates the roles of the AI builder,
  resident Intelligence, mentor, external RFL and experimenter.
- **Already in the text (Vol I §20.1.2, p. 85):** "Formation States and assistance stages are
  generated by an external AI mentor", which is how the mode `TRAINER_KSR` is described, so the
  text itself equates the trainer with the AI mentor there.
- **Test apparatus.** "test bench v0" (Vol II Ch. 33, p. 102), "Proving Ground v0" (§33.B,
  p. 105) and "lower proving ground" (Ch. 33, p. 101) name one thing; FastGate is the
  "zero gate" in the §32.46 heading (p. 99).
- **Capitalization.** From Chapter 1 of Vol I and Chapter 21 of Vol II to the end of each
  volume, numbered headings excluded, Vol I prints Self-Model 8 times and self-model 13,
  Current Coordinate 21 and current coordinate 6, Procedural Memory 11, Procedural memory 7
  and procedural memory 10; Vol II prints Self-Model 18 and self-model 20.
- **Name map.** Executive Loop, one of the nine functions in the guide table that both volumes
  print on p. 5, appears in Vol II only in that table: no chapter or Appendix A entry uses it,
  and the chapters use identifiers such as `authorize_execution`.

## Why it matters

"Modeling Environment" is a component of the Volume I architecture, and `ModelingWorkspace` is
a Volume II record. Appendix A says that correspondence between terms is not identity and lists
`ModelingWorkspace`/`WorkspaceOverlay` as an engineering counterpart, but it has no entry for
the Modeling Environment and none that relates it to the prose names "Modeling Workspace" and
"Simulation Environment", so a reader cannot tell whether these are one thing or three. Case
changes blur coined terms and common nouns.

## Ideas & possible recommendations

- **One name per concept.** Each component and role could be given a single English name, used
  in both volumes; for the component above, one of Modeling Environment, Modeling Workspace or
  Simulation Environment.
- **One capitalization per coined term.** Fixing a capitalization for each coined term would
  let a capital letter mark a defined term and a lower-case letter an ordinary phrase.
- **Match prose, headings and identifiers.** The same name could be used in all three, so that
  pairs such as `ingest_proposals` and `ingest_proposal()`, or "Fundamental risk" and
  `FoundationalRisk`, agree.
- **Add a name map.** Appendix A could carry a Volume I to Volume II name map covering every
  component, including the Executive Loop, and say which names are aliases of one component.

## Resolved when

- [ ] Each component and role has one name in both volumes or a stated name map, and each
      coined term has one capitalization.
