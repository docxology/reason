# MED-02 · The Compass's derivation does no work in the engineering and faces the is-ought gap

## Claims under review

- **Vol I Compass Phrase, p. 8:** "Life strives toward Reason because only Reason can
  consciously preserve, stabilize, develop, and direct the further trajectory of Life." The
  same sentence stands in Ch. 2 (p. 19) and the Conclusion (p. 89). **Vol I Ch. 2, p. 19:**
  "Therefore the direction Life → Reason denotes a functional development of navigation in
  which Life becomes increasingly able to anticipate consequences, investigate its own errors,
  and consciously change its continuation. Different evolutionary lineages realize this
  direction to different degrees and in different forms."
- **Vol I guide, p. 5:** "Explaining the Compass does not by itself show that it governs
  behavior. Its causal role is tested in conflicts with local advantage and under changing
  conditions." **Vol II §29.C, p. 73:** "The minimal Compass is a constitutive ground of a
  particular AR architecture. It sets the direction of continuation and development of Life
  through growth in the ability to understand and direct its own trajectory and permits many
  more concrete value overlays."
- **Vol I Ch. 2, p. 20:** "The direction Life → Reason is adopted not as a command but as a
  functional advantage for continuing Life itself." Also: "The independent causal support lies
  below: continuation of Life requires preservation of viable possibilities, and a possibility
  that has been irreversibly closed can no longer be investigated, corrected, or developed by
  that process."
- **Vol I Ch. 2, p. 21:** "Life as a whole means the set of life processes, their carriers,
  distinctions, environment, and possible future." And: "In a real conflict of scales, the
  broader life process has greater weight not merely because it is 'larger,' but because it
  contains the smaller scale and the conditions of its existence. An environment contains
  carriers and the processes that sustain them; Life as a whole contains individual lineages
  and persons. Destroying the broader life-supporting ground removes the dependent smaller
  processes with it, whereas the reverse is not necessarily true. This is a causal asymmetry of
  scale."

## Finding

The Compass's content does shape the value and action layers: a coverage frame for the
Worldview, a separate "semantic Compass gap" type, the `CompassAssessment` vector with its
epistemic gate, and `CompassChallenge`. Its derivation from the nature of Life does no work
anywhere, and the author concedes that changing the limiting criterion leaves self-correction
intact. The inquiry core would run with any top-level value system, and the operational Compass
is a precautionary, option-preserving multi-criteria evaluation that can be adopted without
accepting that "Life strives toward Reason". As philosophy, the derivation in Chapter 2 has the
familiar weaknesses: that living processes continue does not establish that they ought to;
directionality of evolution toward intelligence is contested (Gould 1996 and Conway Morris 2003
mark the poles); "Life as a whole" is treated as a bearer of direction and moral weight
although it is not a unit of selection; and the "causal asymmetry of scale" shows that the
whole's conditions matter, not that sacrificing parts for the whole is justified.

- **Already in the text (Vol I Ch. 2, pp. 22–23):** the model does "not claim a neutral

## Why it matters

Readers who reject the teleology will discard engineering that does not depend on it, and
readers who accept the engineering will be told they have committed to a metaphysics they need
not hold. The framing makes the strongest parts of the work hostage to the most contestable.

## Ideas & possible recommendations

- **Present the Compass as a regulative idea.** The text could drop the language of derivation
  and present the Compass as a regulative idea in Kant's sense: a principle that orients
  inquiry and choice without being an object of knowledge, defended by its coherence and by
  what it does for the system that adopts it.
- **Say which components depend on the Compass.** The engineering volume could state which
  components depend on the Compass's content (coverage, gap typing, action evaluation) and
  which work with any value system (the inquiry core).
- **Publish the natural philosophy separately.** The natural philosophy of Chapter 2 could
  appear as a companion text, so that the engineering volume does not rest on it.
- **Name the lineage.** The text could name the lineage of the idea (Vernadsky's noosphere,
  Teilhard).
