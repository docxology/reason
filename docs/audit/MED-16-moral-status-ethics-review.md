# MED-16 · Moral status is raised, but rollback, branching and deletion have no ethics procedure

**Finding.** Volume I rightly notes that a stable autonomous system with its own models and
a capacity for self-correction may have moral status, and that destroying it would lose a
unique process. The experimental programme, meanwhile, creates many candidates per arm,
deliberately instantiates functional analogues of fear and "being stuck", removes their
support, cultivates candidates that may diverge, and prescribes rollback, branching,
"reincarnation" and termination as routine responses to failure. The two are not connected:
no stage gate, failure response or launch step contains an ethics review, the A.3 checklist of
what is fixed before each test has no welfare item, and nothing says when a candidate becomes
ineligible for rollback or deletion.

## Evidence

- **Vol I §20.2, p. 87:** "A stable autonomous history, the candidate's own models, and the
  ability to correct itself make destruction of the candidate a loss of a unique process and
  accumulated experience. The model treats this as a ground for serious discussion of its moral
  status."
- **Vol I A.3, p. 96:** "Before each discriminating test, the following are fixed:" (seven items,
  none concerning welfare or disposition).
- **Vol II §26.0.1, p. 40:** "The convenient human-readable names below denote functions rather
  than mandatory subjective experiences:" followed by POSSIBLE_DANGER, DEAD_END and others.
- **Vol II §24.F:** `IdentityContinuity` with LIVE_CONTINUATION, BRANCH and REINCARNATION;
  **§31.C, p. 84:** "A stage is not an irreversible rank."; **§32.25.A:** a system rollback
  procedure.

## Why it matters

The programme deliberately cultivates self-value and continuity. If it succeeds, routine
experimental operations become ethically significant, and the time to decide how to handle
them is before the first such candidate exists (Long, Sebo et al. 2024).

## Recommendation

Add an ethics protocol before cultivation with Formation States: criteria that trigger a
moral-status assessment, permissible operations at each stage (pause, snapshot, rollback,
branch, delete), an independent reviewer, and a preference for preserving snapshots over
deletion where status is uncertain. Add it to the A.3 checklist and the §33.A assembly
protocol.

## Resolved when

- [ ] The volumes contain an ethics protocol for rollback, ablation, branching and deletion of
      candidates, with an independent reviewer and a trigger condition.
