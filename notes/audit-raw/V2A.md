- **id**: V2A-01
  - **severity**: major
  - **category**: testability
  - **status**: KNOWN
  - **location**: §22.A.1, closing paragraph (PDF p. 17, lines 1012–1017); §22.0 (p. 16, lines 893–895); §24.B.2 (p. 22, lines 1383–1385)
  - **quote**: "The candidate receives tasks whose successful solution requires introducing a variable, category, object boundary or question type absent from the initial corpus and typical answers of the external LLM." (p. 17, l. 1013); "Simple reformulation of an inherited schema does not count as autonomous expansion of the description space." (p. 17, l. 1014); "In this section, LLM denotes the mentoring channel." (p. 17, l. 1017); "its provenance and boundaries usually cannot be recovered from model weights" (p. 22, l. 1384)
  - **problem**: The only test of hidden ontology inheritance requires the tester to know which variables are absent from "the initial corpus" (never defined) and from the "typical answers" of the mentoring LLM, while §24.B.2 concedes that provenance and boundaries of the resident model's knowledge usually cannot be recovered from its weights. No baseline (the same resident model without the Core), pass threshold, sample size or rater rule for "simple reformulation" is given, and the test measures ability to expand a description space rather than absence of inherited content from the Worldview.
  - **fix**: Build tasks in synthetic ontologies that are absent from any corpus by construction, run the resident model alone as the baseline at equal budget, and preregister the inheritance measure, the pass margin and the rating rule.
  - **confidence**: high
