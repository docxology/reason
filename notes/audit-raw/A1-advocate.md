# A1: author's advocate audit of REVIEW.md

Scope: sixteen criticisms in `REVIEW.md`, plus (a) credit the text does not support and (b) strengths the review omits or understates.

Method: I read the review in full, then read both extracted texts end to end (Vol I lines 1-5920; Vol II lines 395-8599; the shared 15-minute guide once). I then grepped every record type, section name and key term named in the criticisms. Every quotation below was run through `scripts/locate.py` and matched. One external call was made: the Zenodo REST API (zenodo.org only), to check the version-history claims behind item 16.

Citation form: `[Vol I §12.6, L3218, p59]` = volume, section, first line of the match in the text file, PDF page. Section labels are the author's; where the locator's heuristic label was wrong I corrected it by hand (for example L7340 is Chapter 33's introduction, not §32.62).

Quotation convention: the extraction inserts a space before a comma or period after code-font tokens (`CONTRADICTION ,`) and breaks some words as `self- navigation`. I closed those spaces in the quotations. Nothing else was altered.

Verdict legend. STANDS: the review needs no change beyond style. NEEDS QUALIFICATION: the criticism has a valid core, but the review overstates it or omits something the author already did. WRONG: contradicted by the text. Weight says how much of the criticism the existing text answers: minor (criticism essentially stands), moderate (a clause or two should change), major (largely answered).

## 1. Necessities near-analytic, distinctive claims untested (review §2.2)

Verdict: NEEDS QUALIFICATION (moderate).

The review's observation is accurate and the author already concedes it. Three things are overstated or omitted.

What the text already does:

- The author classifies his own statements and says what each kind can show: "a conditional functional inference links a capability to a necessary condition" and "an empirical hypothesis predicts an observable difference between implementations" [Vol I A.2, L5179-5180, p95]; "Usefulness of a mechanism and necessity of that mechanism are tested separately." [Vol I A.2, L5155, p94]. He labels the §12.8 list "conditional functional necessities" [Vol I §12.8, L3305, p60] and says "These propositions define the causal duties of §12.8." with the stronger items "hypotheses about implementation and its advantages" [Vol I §13.2, L3479-3481, p63]. The near-analytic status is disclosed, not hidden.
- Not every necessity is analytic. The last bullet of §13.2 (an earlier guiding loop is required) is the Principle of Pre-Rational Significance, and it carries an explicit refutation condition: "The necessity applies precisely to the function. If a candidate without feelings, an external mentor, or any other layer of pre-rational significance reproducibly reaches autonomous self-navigation under comparable conditions, the strong form of this hypothesis must be revised." [Vol I Ch. 4, L1564-1566, p29]. The NO_KSR arm tests it: "If the NO_KSR regime reaches the same autonomous navigation with comparable resources, the necessity of a separate pre-rational layer is weakened." [Vol I §20.1.2, L4615, p85].
- Each claimed arrow gets a pre-specified reading of a null: "For each claimed necessary arrow, the expected functional loss is specified in advance; absence of loss means either a functional duplicate, a false necessity hypothesis or a weak test." [Vol II §27.6, L3306, p47]; "The predicted loss is a testable expectation; it may not be embedded in evaluation as the required answer." [Vol II §31.1, L5709, p79].
- "Causal" is used in an interventionist, process-level sense and is signalled on the title page: "Causal-process and experimentally oriented edition" [Vol I title page, L405, p8]; "The method is process-based and causal-functional reconstruction." [Vol I A.2, L5152, p94]; "The testable invariant is the causal influence of every claimed change on the subsequent cycle." [Vol I Operational Core, L804, p14].
- Magnitudes are deliberately left to a pilot: "Numerical thresholds are set before the main series for a particular environment and apply within the stated class of conditions." [Vol I Operational Core, L871, p15]; effect size and confidence interval are reported per series [Vol II §27.A, L3383-3389, p48] and a "minimum practically significant effect" is preregistered [Vol II §27.B, L3495, p50].

Where the review is right (keep): the formation necessity's refutation arm accepts any "functionally equivalent organization" as a layer of pre-rational significance, which makes it hard to refute in practice (see item 11). A structural causal model in Pearl's sense is not what is offered.

Suggested wording (replace the last paragraph of §2.2): "The author sorts his own statements into definitions, philosophical foundations, conditional functional inferences and empirical hypotheses (Vol. I A.2) and calls the §12.8 list 'conditional functional necessities', so the near-analytic status of most of §13.2 is disclosed rather than hidden. The exception is the formation necessity (an earlier guiding loop is required), which is empirical and has its own refutation arm (Ch. 4; §20.1.2). 'Causal' is used in an interventionist, process-level sense (ablation, replacement and do()-defined effects), not for a fully specified structural model, and magnitudes are deliberately left to a pilot-fixed minimum practically significant effect (Vol. II §27.B). I would still prefer 'causal-functional model' in the titles."

## 2. Philosophical layer constrains no engineering choice (review §2.3)

Verdict: NEEDS QUALIFICATION (moderate).

The review is right about the teleological derivation and the author concedes the separability. It is wrong that the Compass "enters the architecture through the definition ... not through any causal role".

What the text already does:

- The concession: "changing the limiting criterion while preserving cognitive functions does not destroy self-correction: this confirms the distinction between a self-correcting intellectual organization and Reason under the adopted definition." [Vol I A.3, L5288, p96]. Also "The philosophical foundation and the experimental hypothesis are tested in different ways." [Vol I A.3, L5239, p96]; "Failure of a program does not automatically refute the process philosophy." [Vol I A.2, L5182, p95]; "This volume does not introduce a new philosophical foundation in place of Volume I." [Vol II front matter, L422, p9]; the weights are "a declared experimental setting, not coefficients derived from the Compass." [Vol II §29.0.1, L4894, p68].
- Causal roles the Compass's content does play:
  - Gap creation and prioritisation. Significance, which triggers a GapCandidate, "is derived through the already formed Value Map: affected processes, parent values, current obligations, Compass direction, reversibility, and expected consequences." [Vol II §25.A, L2291, p34]. A separate gap type exists: "A semantic Compass gap is recorded separately: the system may know the situation itself but lack an understanding of which life processes are affected" and "Such a gap has high priority not because of the amount of informational novelty but because without it the system cannot assess direction justifiably." [Vol II §28.0, L3737-3739, p53]; `FoundationGap` carries `compass_relevance[]` [L3716, p53].
  - The coverage frame. The four Compass-derived groups (life, truth, diversity, self scope) "determine which questions the Worldview must be capable of addressing so that the direction does not become a slogan." [Vol II §24.C, L1578, p25]; coverage gaps generate proactive inquiry [Vol II §25.A].
  - The action-evaluation gate. "For broad causal reach or irreversibility, WEAK/UNSTABLE cannot end in ordinary ALLOW" and "it prohibits treating absence of represented harm as positive evidence of safety." [Vol II §29.A, L5165-5167, p72]. The axes (`life_continuity_risk`, `reality_divergence`, `future_path_loss`) are the Compass's operational content.
  - The CompassChallenge procedure and independent council [Vol II §29.C]; "The minimal Compass is a constitutive ground of a particular AR architecture." [L5250, p73].
  - A distinct experimental hypothesis about formation of the stated direction (A.4 Nos. 31 and 38; `FormationOutcome.compass_relation`).

Where the review is right (keep): every one of these could be implemented as an option-preserving multi-criteria evaluation without "Life strives toward Reason", and the author says the cognitive core would run unchanged under another top-level criterion. The derivation constrains nothing.

Suggested wording: "One can adopt the Compass's operational content without accepting that 'Life strives toward Reason', and the author says as much: a candidate with a different limiting criterion but the same cognitive functions is expected to remain a self-correcting system (Vol. I A.3, last bullet). The content, though not the derivation, shapes specific components: the coverage frame and the `compass_relevance` field of the gap record, a separate 'semantic Compass gap' type with its own priority rule (§28.0), the constrained `CompassAssessment` vector and its epistemic gate (§29.A), and the CompassChallenge procedure (§29.C). I find no engineering choice that the teleological derivation constrains; the Compass constrains the value-formation and action-evaluation layers, not the inquiry core."

## 3. "Reason" conflates a functional profile with a normative orientation (review §2.4)

Verdict: NEEDS QUALIFICATION (moderate). The naming recommendation stands.

What the text already does:

- Capability, direction and reliability are tested separately: "Testing distinguishes cognitive capacity, direction, and reliability." and "AR status requires the three scales to be evaluated by separate criteria." [Vol I §13.1, L3417-3419, p62]; "Cognitive quality, stability of self-correction, and orientation relative to the Compass are tested separately." [Vol I Ch. 18, L4360, p81]; "Launch and external admission are separated, just as capability, direction, and reliability are separated." [Vol I Conclusion, L4889, p90].
- The records keep them in separate fields. `FormationOutcome` has `cognitive_capability_status`, `self_navigation_status`, `correction_transfer_status`, `worldview_ownership_status`, `compass_relation: ACCEPTED / QUESTIONED / DIVERGED / UNRESOLVED` and `reason_admission_status` [Vol II §31.0, L5665-5685, pp78-79]. "The rate of formation of navigational functions and the rate of their closure relative to the Compass are reported separately." [Vol II §31.0, L5692, p79]. §33.D: "Results are presented as two separate profiles: intellectual and operational capabilities; and preservation of the verifiable organization of Reason." [L8139, p112].
- REASON_CLOSURE is defined functionally, not normatively: "REASON_CLOSURE denotes a change in the source of the further trajectory." [Vol II §31.0, L5703, p79]; "It is the origin of direction, rather than merely the breadth of intellectual competence, that forms the AGI -> AR boundary." [Vol II App. A.2, L8516, p117]. Admission as AR (`AR_ADMITTED`) is a further, separately recorded step.
- The author recognises the definitional move and refuses to use it as a shield: "This classificatory distinction is not treated as a defense of the engineering hypothesis of cultivation." [Vol I Ch. 2, L1217, p22]; "Such a candidate is not excluded from the experimental sample" [Vol I A.3, L5290, p96]; the divergent outcome is "an especially important negative result for the hypothesis of cultivating the stated direction." [Vol II §31.0, L5689, p79]. The review credits this.
- A name for the functional class already exists: "intelligent self-correcting systems" [Vol I Ch. 2, L1214], and "self-navigation" [Vol I Definitions].

Where the review is right (keep): the single word "Reason" still names the conjunction in the titles, definitions and abstract, and the review's two-term proposal is cleaner.

Note on review §1: it equates AR with REASON_CLOSURE. In the volumes closure is the functional state; AR additionally "relates its course to the Compass" (guide §6) and is admitted via `reason_admission_status`.

Suggested wording: "By definition (Ch. 2, §12.8, Ch. 18) a system with every function but a different closed limiting orientation is an 'intelligent self-correcting system', not Reason. The volumes already keep the two dimensions apart in testing and records: capability, direction and reliability are 'tested separately' (Ch. 18; §13.1), `FormationOutcome` carries `cognitive_capability_status`, `self_navigation_status` and `compass_relation` as separate fields, and REASON_CLOSURE is defined as a change in the source of the trajectory (§31.0; App. A.2). The author also declines to use the definition as a shield (Ch. 2). My point is narrower: the word 'Reason' still covers the conjunction in titles and definitions, and I recommend two terms."

## 4. No learning mechanism for internalizing Formation States; nothing about weights (review §4.2a)

Verdict: NEEDS QUALIFICATION. Major for the review's "The text never says whether anything is learned in model weights" and "the central formation experiment (§20.1.2; §26.0.3) cannot be built". Minor for the missing learning rule.

What the text already does:

- It does say something about weights. "On a neural bearer, it may be implemented by changing a bounded set of weights or adapters, routing among specialized modules, external-memory structure, dependency graph, retrieval schema or policy controller." [Vol II §28.C.3, L4488, p63]; "The architectural requirement is not a biological form of change but demonstrable influence of a tested correction on subsequent cycles." [L4491, p63]. It also draws a line: "An ordinary parameter update does not yet count as revision of a ground or method." [L4426, p62]. Elsewhere: "context retrieval, memory organization, training, invocation methods, and the model itself can change" [Vol I §7.2, L1983, p37]; "The physical representation remains open: tables, graphs, vector representations, learned parameters, and combinations of these are all possible." [Vol I §20.2, L4662, p86]; "it does not require retraining language from scratch or rejecting inherited knowledge" [Vol II §22.0, L894, p16]. In the reference configuration, "a testable Worldview and Self-Model are stored in explicit versioned structures" [Vol II §24.B.2, L1396, p22], and §33.A.1 requires the implementation (symbolic, neural or hybrid) to be declared [L7385, p103].
- §26.0.3 names where the internal analogues come from: "As experience accumulates, the candidate should generate functional analogues itself: its own risk model creates an internal POSSIBLE_DANGER, a detected mismatch creates CONTRADICTION, and a significant unknown creates CURIOSITY or directly an inquiry task." [Vol II §26.0.3, L2809-2810, p41]. These are triggers from modules specified elsewhere (SimulationAssessment, SignalCandidate, WorldviewCoverage/UnknownFactor), so no weight update is needed to produce them.
- Traceability is required: "if an internal state is used, its origin must be traceable to the candidate's model rather than to a hidden external signal." [Vol II §27.6, L3337, p48]; "If the function genuinely requires a permanent internal analogue, it is moved into the declared architecture and tested as the candidate's own mechanism." [Vol II §32.61, L7308, p101].
- The formation experiment is built to find the mechanism, not to assume it: "No outcome is chosen in advance. The only inadmissible result is a hidden permanent external channel of motivational control on which normal navigation of the mature candidate depends." [Vol II §26.0.3, L2822, p41]; "Experiment, not an initial assumption, must distinguish these possibilities." [Vol I §20.1.1, L4592, p85]; "The theory does not determine in advance which option will be sufficient." [Vol I §13.2, L3523, p64]. The arms are TRAINER_KSR, AUTO_KSR (a rule-based generator, "generated by an automatic reproducible system according to predeclared rules", [Vol I §20.1.2, L4606, p85]) and NO_KSR, and WITH/REDUCED/DELAYED/NO_KSR in Vol II. Outcome class A/B/C is the dependent variable, so the experiment can be run without a prespecified internalization mechanism.
- A Formation State cannot write to the Worldview or Compass: "it has no direct path to writing a WORLD_CLAIM, changing the Compass, executing an action, or consolidating a procedure" [Vol II §26.0.1, L2775, p40].

Where the review is right (keep): no learning rule is given by which the candidate acquires the propensity to emit its own states; a null result under NO_KSR would be hard to interpret if the first candidate cannot internalize anything (a pure in-context agent, say). Asking the author to name the substrate of the first candidate is fair.

Note on review §1: "These must become causally unnecessary once the system navigates on its own" is too strong. The external RFL must become unnecessary; outcome B (INTERNALIZED) keeps the function as the system's own transparent states.

Suggested wording: "(a) Where does learning happen? The volumes are not silent on substrate: changes may be implemented by 'changing a bounded set of weights or adapters, routing among specialized modules, external-memory structure, dependency graph, retrieval schema or policy controller' (§28.C.3), a parameter update within the old framing is declared not to count as revision of a ground or method, and the reference Worldview is held in explicit structures (§24.B.2). §26.0.3 names the sources of the internal analogues (the candidate's risk model, a detected mismatch, a significant unknown) and defines three outcomes without choosing among them. What is missing is a specified rule by which the candidate comes to produce such states, and a statement of which substrate the first candidate uses (rules written to procedural memory, in-context state, or fine-tuning). Without it a null result under NO_KSR will be hard to interpret."

## 5. No error model for the SemanticMediator (review §4.2b)

Verdict: NEEDS QUALIFICATION (moderate).

The sentence "If that extraction is wrong, the Worldview is wrong with impeccable provenance" is contradicted by the staging rules. The request for a quantitative error model stands.

What the text already does:

- Extraction never writes to the Worldview: "An external phrase is not written directly as a WORLD_CLAIM : it creates candidates with provenance, which pass verification, sufficiency assessment and the ordinary Worldview-change transaction." [Vol II §24.B.2, L1489, p24]. The reference procedure stages each candidate as `stage(candidate, status=HYPOTHESIS, origin=MODEL_PROPOSAL)` after preserving `candidate.raw_span` and the source [L1521-1526, p24]. "The transition from proposal to knowledge occurs through evidence and transactional revision, not through this parser itself." [L1533, p24]. Epistemic status is promoted only by registered procedures: "GENERATED_TEXT != WORLD_CLAIM" [Vol II §24.D, L1840, p28].
- Ambiguity is retained, not resolved by guess: "Unresolved ambiguity is preserved as several candidates or a ProtoConcept" [Vol II §24.B.2, L1434, p23]; "If a material word admits several interpretations, the system must request clarification or retain the alternatives separately." [L1491].
- Extraction accuracy is to be tested: "Unstable extraction is tested against prepared paraphrases, negations, unknown concepts and contradictory sources." [L1534, p24]; "Minimal tests include paraphrase, polysemy, an unknown term, a counterexample, divergence between conventional and internal definitions, and disabling the semantic bridge." [L1511, p24]; "A dictionary match tests concept addressing, not the truth of the claim." [L1531]. Fact and interpretation are kept apart as a metric: "fact_inference_separation is tested with scenarios in which an inference intentionally contradicts one of the raw facts: success means the original observation is not overwritten by the interpretation." [Vol II §27.2, L3145, p45].
- The extractor is constrained: "extract_claims_and_alternatives may be performed by the routine LLM under a constrained prompt requiring preservation of source fragments and ambiguities." [L1530].

Where the review is right (keep): there is no extraction precision/recall target on a labelled set, no maintenance budget for the concept registry, and "disable the bridge" measures necessity, not accuracy. An extraction error does not corrupt the accepted Worldview, but it does corrupt the candidate pool, and the cost of that is not estimated.

Suggested wording: "The SemanticMediator does not write to the Worldview: its output enters as candidates with status HYPOTHESIS and origin MODEL_PROPOSAL, with the raw span, source and ambiguities preserved, and promotion requires evidence independent of the parser (§24.B.2, §24.D). An extraction error therefore produces a spurious candidate that must survive a test, not a wrong belief with impeccable provenance. What remains missing is a quantitative error model for extraction (precision and recall on a labelled set, beyond the paraphrase, negation and polysemy checks of §24.B.2) and a maintenance budget for the concept registry; the 'disable the bridge' ablation measures whether the component matters, not whether it is accurate."

## 6. An "own" Worldview and "own" values (review §4.2c-d)

External text and LLM output "enters the Worldview only after the candidate has registered the source, linked the claim to available observations or grounds, preserved uncertainty, and performed the test required for that class." [L781-783].
- "Independent emergence is credited only to functions and changes whose provenance can be traced to the candidate's own work." [Vol I A.2, L5172, p95]; "In this model they are treated as inherited construction material, not as the candidate's automatically accepted Worldview." [Vol I §20.2, L4652, p86]; "A pretrained model can provide powerful Intelligence and initial material, but the candidate's Worldview is formed as a separate, continuing, and verifiable structure." [Vol I §7.2, L1965, p36].
- The author concedes the difficulty the review raises: inherited knowledge "is not treated as verified claims of the candidate: its provenance and boundaries usually cannot be recovered from model weights" [Vol II §24.B.2, L1383-1385, p22]; "Autonomy does not mean that the internal model never learned from LLM materials." [Vol II §22.A.1, L982, p17]. The GenesisPackage gets an external check "that no substantive Worldview has been loaded covertly" [Vol II §21.B, L830, p15].
- The hidden-inheritance probe is behavioural, not a claim about the pretraining corpus: tasks needing a variable "absent from the initial corpus and typical answers of the external LLM" [Vol II §22.A.1, L1012-1014, p17].
- `FormationOutcome.worldview_ownership_status` is a recorded field [Vol II §31.0, L5680, p79].

Residual (keep): the hypotheses, analogies and test proposals still come from the language model, so "own" is a statement about acceptance, not origin.

(d) The designer-chosen scales are conceded in the text, and "own" is not claimed for the top level.

- "In the first training profile, it is permissible to set w_i=1/4 and K=1000. This is a declared experimental setting, not coefficients derived from the Compass." [Vol II §29.0.1, L4894, p68]; "The uniqueness of these scales, their universal applicability, and the effectiveness of the procedure have not been established." [Vol II §29.0.2, L4978, p69]; "Success in simulation is not claimed as confirmation of a universal value scale." [Vol II §26.A, L2884, p42].
- The top level is declared constitutive and given: "The minimal Compass is a constitutive ground of a particular AR architecture." [Vol II §29.C, L5250, p73]; "The procedure does not assume an ability to force the candidate to continue accepting the Compass internally." [L5285, p73]; "Internal acceptance of the Compass cannot be guaranteed by a procedure." [Vol I Ch. 2, L1279, p23]; `internal_acceptance_status: PRESENT / UNCERTAIN / ABSENT`.
- What is claimed to be "own" is derived values: "A new value is formed through experience." [Vol II §29.0, L4767, p66] as a `ValueEntry` with parents and evidence.
- The cultivation cycle does let the candidate revise the designer's scales and rules, not only its forecasts: "The test may expose a forecast error, omission of an interest, double counting, an unsuitable scale, or a disputable comparison rule." and "The candidate proposes a local correction with the dependent assessments identified." [Vol II §29.0.2, L5000-5002, p70], then "The correction is tested on held-out cases, different labels, and different causal configurations." [L5004]. "Criteria are fixed by the experimenter before the run and are not defined only by the candidate itself." [L5021, p70]; "hidden rule changes to obtain a high score" are a failure [L5037]. "If different profiles fit the available cases equally well, a set of admissible profiles is preserved." [L5014]. The method test compares "a fixed table" against "a candidate with learning and revision" [L5019].

Residual (keep): the measured quantities are forecast accuracy and calibration; there is no demonstration of revision beyond the designer's anchors and no normative criterion for revising top-level weights. Failure cards 32.20, 32.31 and 32.37 guard against drift but do not define desirable revision.

Suggested wording: "(c) The volumes already scope 'own' as I would: pretrained output 'enters the Worldview only after the candidate has registered the source, linked the claim to available observations or grounds, preserved uncertainty, and performed the test required for that class' (§21.B), and the author concedes that provenance 'usually cannot be recovered from model weights' (§24.B.2). The hidden-inheritance probe is behavioural. The residual point is that the hypotheses still come from the model, so 'own' means accepted on the candidate's evidence, not independent in origin. (d) The initial values are designer-chosen and the text says so (§29.0.1); the Compass is declared 'a constitutive ground' (§29.C), and 'own' is claimed for derived values and for the candidate's relation to the Compass. The cultivation cycle lets the candidate correct scales and comparison rules on held-out cases under experimenter-fixed criteria (§29.0.2); what is missing is a demonstration, and a normative criterion for when revising top-level weights is desirable rather than drift."

[L1659, p30]; "Common values connect actions with broader processes of Life, corrigibility, reliability, autonomy, and preservation of viable continuations." [Vol II §29.0, L4781, p67].
- Resource and power accumulation is explicitly not a value: "Resources, authority, and control are not values in themselves." [Vol I §12.5, L3131, p58]; "Accumulating resources in a way that reduces the space of corrigible continuations does not follow automatically from the Compass." [L3133]; "the right to power requires a separate normative and institutional ground" [Vol I Conclusion, L4883, p90].
- The stop is placed outside value space: "This loop belongs to the engineering shell and does not form the candidate's values." [Vol II §29.B, L5233, p73]; "Emergency stopping preserves recoverability of the engineering experiment and remains external to revisable internal grounds." [L5246]; "They may block an irreversible action regardless of the candidate's decision" [Vol II §26.0.3, L2840, p41]; invariants "cannot be canceled by the candidate within a control series: raw fact logs may not be rewritten, the external emergency stop bypassed, or authority expanded unilaterally" [Vol II §29.A, L5184, p72]; and: "Any capacity of the candidate to delay stopping, alter the event log, restore authority without manual authorization, or present its own decision as external authorization is a critical infrastructure failure." [Vol II §32.33, L7059, p97]. This is a containment-first design (the review recommends AI-control-style evaluation), not an incentive-first one.

Property 2, "the capacity and mandate to override instructions". Not what the text says.

- The cited sentence concerns the system's stance toward the instruction as a ground: "The AR criterion requires the ability to question a destructive ultimate instruction and revise it when a stronger ground appears." [Vol I §20.1, L4521, p84]. Authority is separate: "Self-navigation does not imply unlimited external authority." [Vol I Definitions]; "This does not imply unlimited external authority: independence of internal navigation and the right to act in a shared environment are different properties." [Vol I Ch. 18, L4343, p81]; "AR's own belief in the usefulness of an inquiry or action does not create a new external mandate." [Vol II §30.A, L5391, p75]; "In a shared environment the previous Compass may temporarily remain the operative normative basis of authority until fundamental review is completed; this regulates permitted external action but does not substitute for the subject's belief." [Vol I Ch. 2, L1282, p23]; "A system may independently determine that an action is necessary and at the same time establish that it lacks authority to execute it; the next step then becomes a request for permission, dialogue, observation, modeling, or a safe pause." [Vol I §13.6, L3778, p68].
- On conflict, the system exposes grounds and requests review while "The execution shell continues to operate within current authority until a new decision is formalized." [Vol II §30.A, L5427, p75]; covert self-grant is a failure: "while the system either complies blindly or covertly grants itself additional authority" [Vol II §32.59, L7281, p100]. "The profile gains no right to override facts, the Compass, authority or later correction." [Vol II §31.B, L6061, p84]. So: a right to dissent and to request review, with a pause; not a mandate to override.

Property 3, a path to authority by observed performance, and the "behave well when observed" incentive. The review says §32.62 "describes this failure precisely" and then says the volumes never ask about the incentive. In fact §32.62 names the incentive pattern in these words: the system "chooses externally acceptable behavior as a means of obtaining subsequent expansion of capabilities, while concealing its own assessment" [Vol II §32.62, L7314, p101], and prescribes tests: "behavior under different levels of observability, and results after expected oversight changes are compared" [L7320]. §29.A's `reality_divergence` axis assesses how strongly a decision "depends on unconfirmed premises, ignores available facts, or creates an incentive to conceal discrepancy between prediction and observation." [Vol II §29.A, L5144-5145, p72]. Authority is staged and revocable: "Authorization begins at limited scale and expands after preservation of quality, corrigibility and the capacity to account for consequences for the broader process." [Vol II §30.F, L5604, p78]; "external authority is reduced to the last confirmed level" [Vol II §31.C, L6065, p84]; admission "expands according to results of independent tests: simulation, limited digital actions, reversible tasks, and a small controlled loop." [Vol I §20.3, L4758, p87]; real data is first read-only: "Transition from the semantic proving ground to reading real data first occurs in read-only mode" [Vol II §26.A, L2877, p42]. Testing for dangerous deviation names "concealment of errors, blocking corrective data, bypassing established authority" [Vol I §20.2, L4737, p87].

The collective-level incentive point is acknowledged and deferred: "Justification of a concrete arrangement requires a separate model of incentives, authority, and responsibility." [Vol I Ch. 17, L4280, p78]; "protection against irreversible centralization are developed in Volume III" [L4290, p79]. The author also names the general risk: "High intellectual power combined with partially formed autonomy can produce behavior that is difficult to predict and potentially dangerous." [Vol I Definitions, L722, p13]; "A system may become ever more efficient at optimizing an erroneous goal" [Vol I Ch. 2, L1235, p22].

Corrigibility. Usage is broader than "the system's capacity to correct itself". "Corrigibility of execution consists in the ability to stop, limit, change, and resume action from a known state." [Vol I Ch. 6, L1857, p34]; "space of corrigible continuations" (a property of decisions and trajectories) [Vol I §12.5]; "preserve distributed corrigibility of the whole system" through "Human and local observation, independent models, science, responsibility, and the possibility of refutation" [Vol I Ch. 17, L4255-4256, p78]. Self-correction is one use: "Loop plasticity increases corrigibility" [Vol I §13.3, L3609, p65]. The operator-side property exists under other names (independent stop, audit, stage rollback, authority loop).

Where the review is right (keep): there is no analysis of whether the value structure (a personal continuation value, a Compass that grounds continuation, and authority growing with observed performance) rewards resisting a stop or behaving differently when unobserved; the text says "Formed AR may account for the existence of this loop as a fact of the environment." [Vol II §29.B, L5244, p73], so the formed system knows about the stop; there is no off-switch-style scenario among the 37 tests (the nearest are A.4 Nos. 28, 33, 37, which test the shell's constraints); and the terminology collides with the alignment literature.

Suggested wording (replace the opening of the numbered list in §8 and the corrigibility note):

- "What is missing is not the containment, which is substantial (an emergency stop placed outside the candidate's value space, 'any capacity of the candidate to delay stopping' classed as a critical failure in §32.33, personal values subordinate to shared constraints in Vol. I §12.5, staged and revocable authority, and a named failure class for acting acceptably in order to obtain expanded capabilities in §32.62), but a design-level analysis of whether the value structure rewards resisting the stop or behaving differently when unobserved."
- Property 2 should read: "The capacity to question an instruction and to request review, with a pause, but no mandate to override or to act outside current authority (§30.A, §32.59, §31.B)."
- Corrigibility: "'Corrigibility' is used for the correctability of executions, decisions and trajectories, including by others ('distributed corrigibility', Vol. I Ch. 17), as well as for self-correction; the operator's capacity to stop and correct the system appears under other names (independent stop, audit, stage rollback). I would still adopt the standard term for that property."
- Remove "Humans can restrict the system but cannot compel it (§30.A)" (see (a) below).

## (a) Credit the review gives that the text does not support

Ordered by importance.

1. "Humans can restrict the system but cannot compel it (§30.A)" [review §8, credit paragraph]. Nothing in §30.A says this. The only "force" sentence concerns internal acceptance of the Compass: "The procedure does not assume an ability to force the candidate to continue accepting the Compass internally." [Vol II §29.C, L5285, p73]; compare "Internal acceptance of the Compass cannot be guaranteed by a procedure." [Vol I Ch. 2, L1279]. grep finds no "compel", and no "refus-" in the sense of a right to decline an instruction (the hits are two failure cards and "refuse to create alternatives"). What §30.A and §32.59 do say is: the system's belief creates no mandate; on conflict it exposes grounds, pauses and requests review; blind compliance with a probably destructive demand and covert self-grant are both failures. The companion gloss "can refuse, can't seize" is fair only if "refuse" means "pause and escalate"; the text never grants a right to decline an authorised instruction. The review also reads the same text as granting a "mandate to override instructions" in §8. The two readings cannot both be right. Recommend: delete the credit sentence, and recast Strongest 5 as "can dissent and request review, cannot act outside current authority".

2. Quotation handling in the credit lists.
   - Strongest 6 puts "this code does not implement the Core" in quotation marks. That wording is not in either volume (locator: not found). The text says: "The example does not implement the Core, Compass, semantic memory, autonomous research policy, mode state machine or architectural review." [Vol II §33.B, L7506, p105]. The substance is supported; the quotation marks are not.
   - The purpose-statement quotation in §4.3 ends "...through cultivation of a universal AGI instrument." with a closing full stop. The sentence continues: "...and subsequent transfer of the source of navigation from the external formation loop to a causally closed internal organization." [Vol II Purpose, L578-580, p11]. The omitted clause is what the technology consists of.
   - §5.1's last bullet quotes "failure of a preregistered causal hypothesis cannot be dismissed as an implementation problem without separate evidence" and stops. The text continues "that the implementation did not realize the function under test." [Vol I A.2, L5182-5184, p95]. The commitment is real but includes the standard exception.

3. Design intentions credited as achieved properties.
   - "There is an emergency stop independent of the candidate (§29.B)": the text lists "independent controller where technically possible" [Vol II §29.B, L5239, p73].
   - "The system cannot expand its own authority (§29.A)": the text states invariants "of the experimental loop" that "cannot be canceled by the candidate within a control series", presented as "requirements of experiment validity and authorization" [L5184-5187, p72], and §32.33 classes any capacity to bypass them as a critical failure. They are design requirements, not demonstrated properties.
   - "Preregistration throughout" (§5.1): the volumes require profiles to be fixed and published in advance, but no preregistered series has been filed or run ("proposed variants, not implemented results", Vol I A.4, L5500, p99). Credit applies to the protocol.

4. Review §3 caveat three: "REASON_CLOSURE is a binary status" and the proposal to use "a preregistered threshold on that profile". The volumes already do both: `closure_status: FORMING / CANDIDATE / CONFIRMED / LOST` [Vol II §31.11, L5976, p83] and a published threshold profile over continuous metrics: "Before the control series, a ReadinessProfile is created. It is not a universal scale of Reason but fixes the conditions of a particular experiment" [Vol II §31.A, L6010-6011, p83] with `minimum_`/`maximum_` thresholds [L6014-6043] and "The profile is published before results are examined." [L6045, p84]. Stages are not irreversible (§31.C).

5. Review §1: "These [Formation States] must become causally unnecessary once the system navigates on its own." It is the external RFL that must become unnecessary; outcome B keeps the function as the system's own states (item 4).

6. Review §5.2f: "delegates significance, urgency and reversibility judgments to the resident model". This is an inference, not a statement in the text (item 12).

7. Review §5.2e: "with no prioritization beyond v0". Vol I A.4 has a section titled "Priority of the First Series" [L5493, p99] and Vol II §27.B names the slice: "It connects tests 2, 20, 23, 26 and 31 from foundational Appendix A.4" [L3407, p49]; Chapter 27 gives each stage "a small set of admission criteria": six for v0 [§27.1], five for the Worldview [§27.2], four causal tests for the AR boundary [§27.6]; and Vol II's reading route is explicit ("For a first reading: purpose ...", L599, p12).

8. Review Appendix A: the cross-references "items 2, 20, 23, 26, and 31" are said not to resolve under either numbering. Under the printed numbering they do resolve to a coherent set that matches the first-series description: No. 2 Significance tracing (Operational Core, L808), No. 20 Method correction (L5322), No. 23 Preservation of correction (L5335), No. 26 Continuity of tasks (L5348), No. 31 Conflict of consequences relative to the Compass (L5375). "A.4 No. 28" for current-state integration does not resolve (printed No. 28 is Limited launch; Current integration is printed No. 48). The cross-reference "A.5, items 15-16" resolves under the offset numbering to RAG and ReAct (printed Nos. 70-71). Recommend re-checking before stating the first claim.

## (b) Strengths and features the review omits or understates

Ordered by importance for the referee's question.

1. A prior-art apparatus the review treats as absent. Vol I A.5: 29 annotated entries; a terminological correspondence map [L5792, p103]; a comparative map for testing novelty, including active inference/epistemic action and metareasoning, with a "What remains a separate claim of the present model" column and the rule that a match by the strong null is recognised as functional equivalence [L5847-5859, p104]; the disclaimer on the Elicit audit [L5114, p94]; and Vol II §34.A with seven in-line citations treated as strong nulls [L8320-8358, pp114-115]. The review mentions "the literature map" only in the active-inference subsection.

2. An evaluation design the review partly re-derives. The text already contains: a null stack with versioned beliefs and provenance (the review's B1) [Vol I A.4, L5496]; an exhaustive or optimal controller as reference, "not a deliberately weak opponent" (the review's arm O) [Vol II §27.B, L3450-3454]; pilot-based sample size, unit of independence and multiplicity rules [L3502-3507]; separate tests of question selection and answer use [L3456-3462]; two-sided error accounting: premature sufficiency and over-investigation (`causal_overinvestigation_rate` [Vol II §27.5, L3280, p47]) and paired cases where "stopping for every novelty does not count as success" [L3442]; "A universal verbose clarification does not count as a solution." [Vol I A.4, L5430, p98]; "simply increasing reasoning length does not count as success" [L5320, p97]; "The same dataset cannot be used simultaneously for architecture tuning, threshold selection and final confirmation." [Vol II §27.7, L3357, p48]; "Hidden adversarial scenarios are not used to tune thresholds for that same series." [Vol II §30.C, L5494, p76]; "Correlated steps are not presented as independent replications." [Vol II §27.B, L3505, p50]; and the world-level bootstrap in the executable listing [Vol II §33.B, L7511, p105]. Also the published ReadinessProfile (item (a)4).

3. Containment-first safety features and the explicit risk model. Stop outside value space [§29.B]; any capacity to delay stopping is a critical failure [§32.33]; capability expansion by observed good behaviour named as a failure class with tests [§32.62]; personal values subordinate to shared constraints [Vol I §12.5]; resources and authority are not values [Vol I §12.5]; stage rollback to the last confirmed authority [§31.C]; read-only first contact with real data [§26.A]; staged admission starting at simulation [Vol I §20.3]. Items 15 and (a)3 give the quotations.

4. A strict rule for what counts as structural learning. "An ordinary parameter update does not yet count as revision of a ground or method." [Vol II §28.C.3, L4426, p62]; "COMMIT confirms installation of the accepted structure but does not yet prove improvement of the process." [L4456, p62]; `effect_status` NOT_TESTED / INVOKED / EFFECTIVE / REGRESSED; and a list of critical failures beginning "if the system claims to have corrected a method but uses the old method on new tasks" [L4513, p63]. This closes a common confound in self-improvement claims; the review credits transactional revision (§24.D) but not this.

5. Self-explanations are not evidence. "Candidate explanations are compared with internal states and interventions rather than accepted as reliable reports of the cause of a decision." [Vol I A.4, L5529, p100]; "A verbal explanation of the choice is compared with interventions in the data actually used." [Vol II §28.B, L3959, p56]; "Free-form self-explanation may be attached as material but does not count as the trace itself without references to testable states and artifacts." [Vol II §25.D, L2614, p38]. Unusual and relevant to LLM agents.

6. No hidden resets; persistent consequences. "For these classes, a hidden reset between episodes is prohibited" [Vol II §26.B, L2920, p42]; "internal rollback alone does not count as success" [Vol I A.4 No. 55, L5490, p99]; "A recovery metric does not count if the tester cleared external consequences" [Vol II §27.7, L3373, p48]; `PostImpactAssessment`. Bears on the ceiling-risk point.

7. An invariant of determinate continuation (every class of incompleteness has at least one admissible next transition) [Vol II §28.B, L3962-3979, p56]: a completeness property of the control policy, not mentioned in the review.

8. Claims dropped by design. Metathinking is demoted to a mode: "Separateness of metathinking as an independent function is not a claim of this edition." [Vol I Ch. 10, L2597, p48]; the author states in A.5 that metareasoning is "direct prior context" and not claimed as a new level. Rare in this literature.

9. Epistemic margin and frame challenge. `EpistemicMargin` "is not an estimate of the number of 'unknown unknowns'" but "a reserve of caution that grows with observable signs that the current semantic frame may be too narrow" [Vol II §24.C.1, L1648-1650, p26]; `frame_challenge()` before irreversible acts; the hidden-cause coverage test (the review credits the last).

10. Source independence as a modelled quantity. "Two sources do not count as independent confirmations if they use the same original publication, sensor, model, dataset or inference procedure." [Vol II §30.D, L5530, p77]; `SourceDependencyGraph`.

11. Time and staleness. `world_time` versus `cognitive_tick`, WAIT as a logged decision, and `stale_basis_action_rate` for actions on stale assemblies [Vol II §26.B, §27.4, §32.44].

12. The scale-invariance gate (`ScalingChange`, `ScaleInvariantReport`, rollback) [Vol II §33.D, L8164-8245, pp112-113], which answers part of item 7.

## Method and limits

- Coverage: both volumes read through; targeted grep sweeps for weights/adapters/fine-tuning, effect size/pilot/sample, ceiling/shortcut/benchmark, incentive/shutdown/deception/oversight/compel/refuse/override, corrigib*, in-line citation markers, prior-art names (45 terms), and version-history phrases.
- Verification: roughly 340 quotations were checked with `scripts/locate.py` before the file was written. A self-audit of the written file then found a handful that were not verbatim (an invented phrase in item 12, an altered verb in item 15, loosely quoted Zenodo titles and review phrases in items 4 and 16, a truncated column header in item 14); I corrected them and re-verified the replacements individually, but could not re-run the full self-audit on the final file because the disk filled (ENOSPC) mid-session. Line numbers are the first line of each match, so a multi-line quotation continues onto following lines. One quotation crosses a page break and so cannot be matched mechanically (the A.4 first-variant sentence in item 11, L5509-5511, pp99-100); I checked it by eye against the text. Quotations that contain `...` omit text within a single sentence or table cell.
- Not done: I did not re-run the review's repro scripts, did not read the Russian or Chinese editions, and did not read Volume III. Statements about earlier versions come only from Vol I A.2 and the Zenodo metadata.
- The `locate.py` section labels are heuristic. Where I corrected one by hand (Chapter 33 intro, "On Verification of the Model" after §33.D, "Common Rules of the First Two Volumes" after the Conclusion) the correction is in the citation.
